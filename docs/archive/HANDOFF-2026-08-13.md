## 3. THE BPP+ INVESTIGATION — full record

### 3.1 How it started

Brennan saw "randomly changing pixel noise / fuzz" in flat skies on **Easy Rider** (BPP+ 78) and later
**Lawrence of Arabia** (BPP+ 134, 26 GB, H.264 8-bit, Bluray). His objection: *"a 78 shouldn't be a
noticeably distorted and artifact ridden picture"*, and separately, *"I want a 100 of every film to be
perfectly watchable... I'm happy for there to be grain... the random compression noise on the grain is
crap."*

### 3.2 Playback was exonerated first — do not re-litigate this

Jellyfin is **not** re-compressing video. From Brennan's own session log at the exact timestamp he was
inspecting:

```
-ss 00:18:48.669 -i "...Lawrence of Arabia (1962) Bluray-1080p.MKV"
  -codec:v:0 copy  -bsf:v h264_mp4toannexb
  -codec:a:0 libfdk_aac -ac 2 -ab 256000
```

`-codec:v:0 copy` — bit-exact video. Only audio is transcoded (AC3 5.1 → AAC stereo, because browsers
can't do AC3). 25 DirectStream + 6 DirectPlay decisions in recent logs, **zero video transcodes**.
Confirmed on Streamyfin/iPhone too. "Less bad on the phone" is just angular resolution — same pixels,
smaller visual angle.

**So the artifacts are in the FILES.** Lawrence is a *re-encode*, not a remux: ~14 Mb/s video average
where a true BD remux is 28–35 Mb/s.

### 3.3 The root cause we found

`controller/scripts/probe-film.sh` measures complexity with `libx265 -crf 20` and **no tune**. x265's
default RD model treats film grain as perceptually irrelevant and discards it — which is precisely why
`--tune grain` exists as an opt-in. So the probe asks *"how many bits does this film need?"* and x265
answers *"few, I'll throw the grain away."* Grain-heavy films measure EASY → target sinks → BPP+ reads
HIGH.

The derived correction (no fudge factor):

```
grainShare = 1 - defaultBytes / tunedBytes        (same samples, same CRF)
complexity_faithful = complexity / (1 - grainShare)
```

**Design decision (Brennan's, and it is right): do NOT fold this into `complexity`.** It has 1026
measurements and 96 calibration pairs behind it; changing it makes all history incomparable and
destroys the calibration set reconstructed from the event log on 08-12. Grain is a **separate signal**
combined in the **target function** — `DESIGN-CRF-PROBE.md §8` designates that layer as free to change.

Brennan also **rejected a damping constant `k`** ("if this IS true, I'd sort of like to know") and
**rejected using an off-disk remux as a calibration anchor** — its bitrate is a mastering choice, not a
property of the film, and the size was unverified torrent metadata. So: **k = 1, and the DISTRIBUTION
is the validation.**

### 3.4 THE RESULT — the camera hypothesis FAILED

10 films, 5 expected-grainy vs 5 expected-clean, `veryfast`/1280/5 samples:

| film | prior | gShare | per-sample | BPP+ now | k=1 | Δ |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2001: A Space Odyssey | 65mm | **0.595** | 0.53–0.66 | 153 | 97 | −56 |
| Lawrence of Arabia | 65mm | 0.528 | 0.39–0.72 | 134 | 92 | −42 |
| **Whiplash** | **digital** | **0.452** | 0.36–0.55 | 94 | 69 | −24 |
| Alien | 35mm | 0.421 | 0.20–0.77 | 155 | 118 | −37 |
| **The Social Network** | **digital RED** | **0.421** | 0.37–0.48 | 136 | 104 | −33 |
| Gone Girl | digital RED | 0.380 | 0.26–0.62 | 100 | 79 | −21 |
| Blade Runner | 35mm | 0.354 | 0.15–0.67 | 142 | 114 | −28 |
| Skyfall | digital Alexa | 0.354 | 0.17–0.57 | 149 | 120 | −29 |
| Raiders of the Lost Ark | 35mm | 0.218 | 0.02–0.61 | 111 | 98 | −13 |
| **Blade Runner 2049** | **digital Alexa** | **0.216** | 0.04–0.70 | 207 | 183 | −24 |

```
FILM    n=5  range 0.218-0.595  median 0.421
digital n=5  range 0.216-0.452  median 0.380
```

**The groups overlap across nearly their entire range.** Whiplash (digital) beats three 35mm films.
BR2049 (digital, 0.216) ≈ Raiders (35mm, 0.218). Social Network (digital) == Alien (35mm) to three
decimals. Medians differ by 0.04 against ~0.38 of within-group spread.

**What `-tune grain` actually measures is the cost of preserving HIGH-FREQUENCY DETAIL.** Grain is one
source of that; fine texture in sharp digital photography is another. It is a **faithfulness** measure,
not a grain or camera detector.

### 3.5 What survives the negative result

- **gShare spans 2.7× across films and is perfectly reproducible.** Lawrence returned 2.120 / 0.528 on
  two separate runs, byte-identical, *across the memory crisis* — proving encode outputs are
  deterministic and that memory pressure corrupts timing but never measurement.
- It is per-film, automatic, no year/genre heuristic — Brennan's core requirement.
- **Lawrence lands at 92**, which matches his perception ("watchable, but the noise is very noticeable
  now I'm looking").
- The two 65mm films did take the top two slots — but with digital scattered through the same band,
  that ordering is no longer *evidence* for capture format.

### 3.6 ⚠️ THE NEXT TEST — do this before building anything

**Every film drops 13–56 points and clean digital drops as much as film does.** BR2049 loses 24 points
despite being the cleanest, best-sourced file in the library. If the correction penalises everything
alike it is a whole-library rescale, not a per-film insight — the degeneracy Brennan warned about.

**Run the offset-stability test.** Per-sample ranges are enormous (Raiders 0.02–0.61, Alien 0.20–0.77),
so 5 samples may not pin a film's value at all:

```sh
# same film, different scenes — does its gShare move?
GRAIN_SAMPLES=5 ./scripts/probe-grain.sh --fast          # baseline offsets
GRAIN_SAMPLES=7 ./scripts/probe-grain.sh --fast          # different offsets, same films
```

If a film's number swings by more than the group separation, then the 0.216–0.595 spread is
**scene-selection luck rather than a film property**, and the correction collapses. This is cheap
(~6 min/film) and decisive. It also directly motivates **#9** (store per-sample values).

### 3.7 The cheap detector is DEAD — three independent failures

`jitter` = mean luma residual after `atadenoise` (temporal denoise). Rationale was sound: grain is
temporally uncorrelated. **It does not work.**

| evidence | r |
|---|---|
| n=3, similarly-paced films | **+0.988** |
| n=4, after adding Raiders | **−0.050** |
| n=4, fast mode | **−0.186** |

**Why it fails, structurally:** a temporal denoiser removes *motion* as well as grain, and an absolute
measurement of a two-source quantity cannot separate them. Raiders is the most kinetic film in the set
— second-highest jitter, lowest grain cost. 2001 is the clincher: **lowest jitter of any film (0.4629)
and the highest grain cost (0.595)**.

**Why the expensive metric avoids this: it is DIFFERENTIAL.** Both passes see identical pixels; motion
and detail cost bits in *both* and cancel in the ratio. Any future cheap detector must have that
property or it will fail the same way. A possible fix, untested: measure only in regions that are both
spatially *flat* and temporally *static* — which is where Brennan actually sees the artifact.

The column is retained in output as a record. **Do not use it as a proxy.**

### 3.8 Cost — the corrected numbers

The reference measurement (`medium`/1920) was timed on the thrashing box and those figures are junk
(~50 min/film, "850 h / 140 nights"). **On a healthy box, `--fast` is 1–10 min/film, ~6 min mean →
a library-wide pass is ~100 h, about 17 nights at 6 h.**

**`--fast` is validated:** r = **+0.912** against medium on the 4 shared films. Only Blade Runner and
Alien swapped, and they were **0.012** apart in medium — far below per-sample scatter, i.e. never
resolvable by either mode. Lawrence vs the 35mm pair, a real gap, is preserved in both.

If a long nightly pass is ever run it needs a **hard runtime cap** — this box has form: the 2026-07-09
trickplay task ran uncapped and cooked it for a full day (fixed with a 4 h `MaxRuntime`).

## 4. THE AUDIO FIX — written, NOT DEPLOYED (#12)

`controller/lib/arr-inspect.js`. When `mediaInfo.videoBitrate` is 0 or untrusted (~18% of the library),
`bppOf()` fell back to size/duration and charged **every audio track to video**. This biases in the
worst direction: it most flatters multi-dub and lossless-audio releases, exactly what we don't want
flattered.

**Done:**
- `audioToSubtract(mi, total)` — subtracts the reported audio bitrate, capped at 40% of the container
  as a backstop against a bogus value wiping out the video figure.
- `bppOf()` uses `total - audioToSubtract()` on the fallback path only; the trusted-`videoBitrate` path
  is untouched.
- `bppSource()` grew from 2 states to 4: `video`, `total-minus-audio`, **`total-minus-audio-partial`**
  (more audio tracks exist than *arr gave bitrates for → bpp is still a CEILING), `total`.
- Verified: Lawrence bpp 0.33415 → 0.32514, source `total-minus-audio-partial`.

**Still to do — this is where most of the error lives.** *arr reports `audioBitrate` for ONE track.
Lawrence has `audioStreamCount: 2` and reports only the 448k AC3; the **DTS-HD MA track that matters
(measured 2.18 Mb/s by packet sampling) reports nothing**. So the fix only moves Lawrence 134 → 133,
where subtracting all real audio gives 123. Recovering those ~10 points needs us to **measure audio
ourselves** — sum audio packet sizes per stream during the nightly probe, which already decodes
samples, so marginal cost is ~zero. Store it on the probe entry and prefer it over mediaInfo.

`docker exec controller grep -c audioToSubtract /app/lib/arr-inspect.js` (should be 3, currently 0).

## 5. TRAPS — all of these were hit, all of them silent

1. **`-x265-params tune=grain` is IGNORED by x265 3.2.1.** It produced BYTE-IDENTICAL output. `tune`
   must go through ffmpeg's own `-tune`. Without a size assertion, every film scores gShare 0 and you
   "prove" grain doesn't matter. `probe-grain.sh` now aborts on byte-identical totals.
2. **`metadata=print` logs at INFO**, so under `-loglevel error` a filter-based detector emits nothing
   at all. Use `file=-`.
3. ***arr sanitises paths: `:` → ` - `.** `2001: A Space Odyssey` is on disk as
   `2001 - A Space Odyssey (1968)`. Matching the probe-cache title literally **silently skipped it from
   an entire 2-hour run**. `find_file()` now tries the sanitised forms.
4. **`ManualImport` without `languages` half-works, destructively and silently.** It moves the file,
   deletes the file it replaced, returns `result=successful "Manually imported 1 files"` — and writes
   NO movieFile row. The movie then reads as MISSING with its bytes correctly on disk. Recovery is
   `RescanMovie`, not a second import (the bytes are already in place). **Never trust that command's
   own success report — verify the DB row.** Saved to memory as
   `arr-manualimport-languages-required`.
5. **Dimensions live in `mediaInfo.resolution`** (`"1920x1080"`), parsed by `dimsOf()`. There are no
   `width`/`height` keys. I claimed a resolution backfill was needed; that was wrong and is retracted.
6. **`iostat`'s first sample is the since-boot average.** It showed 126 ms read latency; live samples
   were 2.21 ms. I nearly reported disk contention that didn't exist.
7. **Editing a running bash script can corrupt its execution** — bash reads the file lazily. Never
   patch a script that is mid-run; kill it first.
8. **One pathological file can stall an entire run forever.** `probe-grain.sh` now wraps every encode
   in `timeout`, deletes stale outputs before each pass (a timed-out encode otherwise leaves the
   *previous sample's* files for `stat` to find, fabricating a ratio from two unrelated scenes),
   excludes failed samples from the average, counts coverage, and prints
   `ALL N SAMPLES TIMED OUT` rather than silently skipping.
