## 3. THE ACTUAL FINDING — blockiness, and it was free all along

### 3.1 The grain metric is dead

`gShare = 1 - defaultBytes/tunedBytes` does **not** measure grain. **Up (2009), rendered CGI with zero
photochemical grain, scored 0.407** against Casablanca (grainy B&W stock) 0.422 and Alien 0.421.

Mechanically: `-tune grain` is **eight simultaneous knobs**, not a grain switch — `aq-mode 0`,
`cutree 0`, `psy-rd 4.0`, `psy-rdoq 10.0`, `sao 0`, `recursion-skip 0`, plus QP flattening. x265's own
docs say its purpose "is neither to retain nor eliminate grain". `aq-mode 0` is a large,
content-dependent change that costs most on flat dark films — which is why 2001 and Lawrence topped
the table and invented a 65mm story that was never there.

Confirmed by measurement: **r(gShare, mean luma) = −0.885** across photographed films. It was tracking
DARKNESS. (Collapses to −0.584 once CGI is included — rendered content breaks the rule.)

**Do not resurrect this.** `scripts/probe-grain.sh` and `probe-darkness.sh` are kept as tools.

### 3.2 `blockMean` separates the films Brennan has judged — and it is already measured

`blockdetect` has run on every probe since day one, on the **decoded source** (`VF="$SCALE,blockdetect,blurdetect"`),
so it measures artifacts already in the file. 881 movies have it. **Zero new cost, no re-probing.**

Library: p10 1.10, **p50 1.33**, p90 2.20, p99 6.29, max 10.36.

| film | blockMean | pct | blurMean | R | src | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Easy Rider | 3.80 | 96th | 45th | 0.61 | 6.1 Mb | **artifacts** |
| Lawrence of Arabia | 2.36 | 90th | **1st** | 1.80 | 16.6 Mb | **artifacts** |
| Raiders | 1.39 | 58th | 30th | 1.24 | 12.7 Mb | — |
| No Other Choice | 1.05 | 1st | 9th | 0.33 | 2.0 Mb | looked good *(weak)* |
| Cinema Paradiso | 1.04 | 1st | 72nd | 0.49 | 1.9 Mb | looked good *(weak)* |

**Bitrate gets all four backwards** — the two that looked GOOD are the two most starved; the one that
looked BAD is the least starved. blockMean orders all four correctly.

Lawrence's signature is diagnostic: **sharpest 1% of the library AND blockiest 10%** — detail
retained but block-quantised, i.e. "compression noise on the grain". A smeared YIFY-class rip shows
the opposite (low block, high blur), so block+blur together separate "artifacted" from "smoothed to
death". Neither alone does.

### 3.3 Bits DO fix blockiness — measured, not assumed

Same 4 s Lawrence scene re-encoded (run inside the controller; **the host's ffmpeg 4.2 has no
blockdetect and silently outputs nothing**):

| CRF | size | blockMean |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 18 | 2,674 KB | 2.43 |
| 24 | 356 KB | 2.91 |
| 30 | 132 KB | 3.83 |
| 36 | 63 KB | 4.66 |

Monotonic. So blockiness is **not** an independent axis — it is the direct observation of what BPP+
predicts indirectly. The library-wide r(block, R) = 0.16 reflects varying *encoder competence and
vintage*, not causal independence. **An earlier claim in this project that they are decoupled was
wrong; this table is the correction.**

### 3.4 wBPP+ — the proposed shape, NOT yet implemented

```
wBPP+ = BPP+ x (MEDIAN_BLOCK / blockMean) ^ E        MEDIAN_BLOCK = 1.33, E = 0.40 (provisional)
```

**Anchor on the library MEDIAN, not on the clean floor of 1.0.** Anchoring at the floor means every
film can only lose points — a silent 15% rescale of the whole library, which is exactly the
anchor-breaking Brennan rejected for grainShare. Median-anchored, the typical film is unchanged,
cleaner films rise, blockier ones fall (median multiplier 0.959).

Preview (25 films, `/tmp/.../wbpp.js`): Jackie Brown 157→78, Moon 151→79, La Haine 112→57,
No Country 81→36, **Lawrence 132→105**, **Easy Rider 63→41**, Raiders 109→107, 2001 141→141,
**Casablanca 125→127**, **No Other Choice 46→51**, **Cinema Paradiso 69→76**.

All four judged films move the right way without being told which was which.

**TWO OPEN RISKS — do not implement before resolving them:**

1. **E = 0.40 is fitted to ONE judgement** (Lawrence ≈ 105). At E = 0.25, Jackie Brown is 102 not 78 —
   a 24-point swing on identical evidence.
2. **Every big loser is a grainy film** (Jackie Brown, Moon, La Haine, No Country — all grainy, all
   well-supplied with bits). If `blockdetect` partly responds to GRAIN rather than BLOCKING, wBPP+
   would systematically punish grainy films — the opposite of what Brennan wants. Against it:
   Casablanca 1.28 and Raiders 1.39 are grainy and sit at the floor.
   **Jackie Brown, Moon, La Haine and The Boy and the Heron are all in the blind list**, so his
   verdicts settle exactly this.

Also unverified: `blockdetect` measures block-grid discontinuities, whereas what Brennan describes
(shimmering fuzz on grain) is arguably closer to mosquito noise/ringing — same quantisation family,
not the same artifact.

## 5. TRAPS HIT TODAY

1. **The host's ffmpeg is 4.2 and has NO `blockdetect`/`blurdetect`** — it fails silently and prints
   empty columns. **Always run detector work via `docker exec controller`** (ffmpeg 5.1).
2. **`-vf` is an OUTPUT option and must precede the output URL.** Placed after `-f null -`, ffmpeg
   emits nothing and every sample reads as "failed".
3. **2001: A Space Odyssey is intrinsically pathological in x265** — 287 s for one 4 s encode on a
   healthy box vs ~35 s for Lawrence. The previous handoff blamed memory pressure; that was wrong.
   Excluded from all sets.
4. **Audio measurement is SEEK-bound, not window-bound.** 120 s window = 161 s, 30 s window = 120 s on
   a 28 GB file. Shrinking `AUDIO_WIN` is the wrong lever. ~24 s/file typical → ~6 h for 884 movies.
5. **This box has 4 cores.** Two concurrent encode suites drove load to 13 and 89 °C while halving
   each one's throughput. Run experiments strictly sequentially.
6. **Group-ordered film sets waste a stopped run** — interleave groups so an early stop still carries
   the contrast.
7. **A single `top`/`docker stats` sample lies.** Radarr looked like it was eating 65% of a core
   permanently; sampled properly it averages 29% and idles near zero between 5-minute RSS syncs.
   (Same class of error as the `iostat` first-sample trap in the previous handoff.)
