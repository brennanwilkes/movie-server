E.1  SUBJECTIVE VERDICTS OF 2026-08-13 — RETRACTED
================================================================================
Backup 11.1, trap 8, open question 0, 16.A.4. Run 2026-08-13, invalidated 2026-08-18.
The model has never been tested against a valid subjective label and there is no
replacement. THIS IS THE SINGLE LARGEST GAP IN THE PROJECT, and it is not a
measurement problem — it needs labels that do not exist here.

E.1.1  THE TEST, AND THE NUMBERS IT PRODUCED
Eleven films Brennan had ACTUALLY WATCHED, judged on a phone at native resolution,
scored 1 = artifacts / 0.5 = maybe / 0 = clean. Spearman against the eye:

    src Mb/s      0.905
    complexity    0.824
    R             0.146
    blurMean     -0.266
    blockMean    -0.090

E.1.2  WHAT WENT WRONG
What Brennan was scoring as "artifacts" was GRAIN. Nobody can separate the two by eye
from a single UNPAIRED clip — which is exactly why subjective standards use paired or
reference-anchored presentation and not "rate this one".

THE TABLE INVERTS ON THAT READING:
  - src Mb/s 0.905 is NEAR-TAUTOLOGICAL against a grain label. High-bitrate files
    reproduce grain faithfully, and grainy catalogue films get generous disc encodes.
    Both push the same way.
  - complexity 0.824 is the same, since complexity is very largely grain cost (E.3.7).
  - R 0.146 is the ONLY one of the three that is NOT a grain proxy, because R divides
    grain out of the numerator AND the denominator. *** R SCORING ~0 AGAINST A GRAIN
    LABEL IS R BEHAVING AS IT SHOULD. ***
An earlier edition of the design doc called 0.146 "the model's central problem".
WITHDRAWN.

WHAT IS LEFT STANDING: NOTHING. The test neither validates nor falsifies BPP+.

E.1.3  WHAT FELL WITH IT — the collateral, listed because parts of it keep being
       re-cited as if they had survived
  - blockMean -0.090 and blurMean -0.266 were withdrawn WITH the labels, together with
    the "do not revive blockMean" verdict and the false-positive case list behind it.
    blockMean's -0.090 says blockiness and grain-visibility are unrelated, which tells
    you nothing about whether it detects blocking. THAT IS NOT AN ARGUMENT TO REVIVE
    THEM EITHER — the evidence in both directions does not exist. UNTESTED, NOT
    FALSIFIED, and the distinction must stay sharp (backup 11.4).
  - wBPP+ = BPP+ x (1.33/blockMean)^0.40, killed 2026-08-17 on a Spearman of -0.090
    against these verdicts. The term is still unsupported, but its evidence evaporated
    with the labels. It had been measured, stored, correlated, given a plausible
    mechanism and NEARLY SHIPPED (backup 16.B.3) — which is why "VALIDATE BEFORE
    WEIGHTING" is step 3 of the adoption order and is recorded there as the one step
    this project skipped.
  - the inference that grain contamination explained R's 0.146 (backup 9.1). Withdrawn.
    GRAIN IS NOT ESTABLISHED AS BPP+'s CENTRAL PROBLEM: grain boiling is a physical
    finding on two clips (E.3.6) never connected to any quality judgement. The only
    defect ever demonstrated under control is source pinning.
  - the magnitudes in the does-BPP+-over-reward-grain check (backup 8.7). The
    DIRECTION survives, because it rests on measured complexity rather than on labels.
    See E.3.7.
WHAT WAS NOT AFFECTED: detector GRADING. A split-half reliability needs no label at
all — split a detector's per-clip readings in half, average each half, correlate the
halves across films. blockMean and blurMean sat ungraded for months on the false
belief that grading needed a subjective label (backup 9.5). Realising it did not is
what unblocked the whole artifact panel. See E.4.11.

E.1.4  THERE IS STILL NO CONFIRMED CASE OF COMPRESSION DAMAGE IN THIS LIBRARY
Every artifact report on record resolved to grain. BPP+ has never been shown to
misrank anything.
    *** THE HONEST STATE OF THE MODEL IS UNTESTED, NOT BROKEN. ***
And the second consequence, which is the one that qualifies everything else in this
file: EVERY FITTED WEIGHT, BAND EDGE AND CALIBRATION CONSTANT IS UNVALIDATED.

E.1.5  THE DURABLE LESSON — backup trap 8. Two traps, and the second cost the dataset
  (a) JUDGE ONLY FILMS BRENNAN HAS WATCHED. A first-time viewer cannot separate "this
      encode looks wrong" from "this film always looked like that". The first blind
      list was mostly unseen films and had to be rebuilt from Jellyfin's played list.
  (b) NEVER ASK FOR A VERDICT ON A SINGLE UNPAIRED CLIP. The 2026-08-13 test asked
      "artifacts: yes/no" and got back a grain rating. Show TWO versions of the SAME
      SCENE and ask which is worse: grain is then identical across the pair BY
      CONSTRUCTION, so only compression can move the answer. Forced choice also kills
      the tie-heavy Spearman that made n=11 look more informative than it was.

E.1.6  THE REPLACEMENT PROTOCOL — designed, never run (backup 16.A.4)
Forced-choice A/B on the SAME SCENE of the SAME FILM: "which looks worse". Generate B
by re-encoding the SAMPLED CLIP at a known-degraded bitrate — probe-starve.sh already
produces exactly these clips and the ~/grain-test rig is the same shape. Target 20-30
films Brennan has WATCHED, balanced across clean modern digital, clean older transfer,
heavy-grain film, B&W grain, animation, known low-bitrate encode, known re-encode and
deliberately-degraded photography. Judge on the phone at native resolution, and record
"visible on the projector?" as a SEPARATE binary — the real decision is whether an
upgrade changes what Brennan sees in the room.
ONE PASS RE-TESTS EVERYTHING AT ONCE: R, blockMean, blurMean, gridRatio, grainShare,
and HEADROOM_TARGET (where 100 sits).
STATUS: NOT BUILT. It is open question 0 in the backup and 9.0 in the core doc, and
everything downstream waits on it.

E.2  THE NIGHTLY CRF PROBE AND THE DENOMINATOR
================================================================================
Backup sections 4, 6 and 6.5. This is what makes BPP+ per-film rather than
one-size-fits-all: every night, in a gated window, the controller takes 8 four-second
samples spread across a title, encodes them at CRF 20 TO A NULL OUTPUT, and records
the bitrate that costs. That is `complexity` — what THIS content costs to look right.
Nothing is written; /data is read-only in the container.

E.2.1  WHY CRF, AND THE CAVEAT THAT IS DOING REAL WORK
CRF is a PERCEPTUAL-QUALITY anchor, not a bitrate one. CRF-20 encodes of Casablanca
and Blade Runner 2049 are APPROXIMATELY the same visual quality; they cost 3853 vs
1000 kb/s. That is exactly the per-film number the denominator needs.
"APPROXIMATELY" IS NOT FREE. Fixed CRF is not exactly fixed quality across content:
published spread is ~3-sigma 10 VMAF against ~2.6 for quality-targeted encoding
(MainConcept). Whether that residual runs with grain HERE — which would make the
denominator's error SYSTEMATIC rather than random — is open question 4 and has never
been measured. VMAF cannot be the instrument: grainy films score lower VMAF at the
same CRF, so the test would measure VMAF's own grain bias and report it as an encoder
property.
HEADROOM_TARGET is a READ-TIME constant. Moving it does not require re-probing, since
x265 bitrate runs ~-13.6%/CRF point (E.2.7): a preference for CRF 22 is a multiplier.
The expensive measurement stays valid.

E.2.2  THE MEASUREMENT THAT KILLED THE FLAT CONSTANT             [SOLID]
Before 2026-08-06 the denominator was one number, BPP_TARGET = 0.13, library-wide.
Measured complexity across 1031 units:

    min      0.0346    The Father, clean digital
    p10      0.0705
    p25      0.0880
    median   0.1120
    p75      0.1497
    p90      0.2141
    max      0.9270    12 Angry Men, B&W heavy grain
    spread   26.8x

The flat 0.13 was wrong by roughly 2x in BOTH directions and SYSTEMATICALLY: it
overrated grainy film-stock transfers, whose bits buy grain reproduction rather than
detail, and underrated clean modern digital ones. The median 0.112 shows 0.13 was a
decent library-wide AVERAGE and a poor per-film ANSWER — the exact failure mode you
cannot see without measuring. It survives ONLY as the no-probe fallback.

E.2.3  PRECISION MACHINERY (what the nightly does beyond first measurement)
    PROBE_SAMPLES    8      the floor, not a luxury: scene complexity varies 8.2x
                            WITHIN a single film
    SAMPLES_MAX      16     used when a unit's spread or RSE says 8 was not enough
    POOL_MAX         64     samples accumulate across visits; SE falls as 1/sqrt(n)
    RSE_GOOD         0.12   +-12% on the mean = precise enough to stop spending encodes
    RSE_WIDE         0.20
    SPREAD_WIDE      6      ~p75 of the library, measured 2026-08-17
    IMPRECISE_SHARE  3      one pick in 3 goes to refinement once fresh work runs out
    AUDIO_BATCH      8      audio files measured per tick
Pooling only happens when the bytes on disk are unchanged (`sameFile`), so a replaced
file starts clean rather than averaging two different encodes. Nightly priority order:
first-measurement -> audio backfill -> refinement.

E.2.4  THE POOLING BUG — REPEAT VISITS RE-MEASURED THE SAME CLIPS  [SOLID, FIXED]
Found 2026-08-18 by Brennan, from banding in the lab's sample plot. Sample offsets are
deterministic (T = start + span*i/N), which is correct and necessary — a ladder or a
re-probe must hit the same scenes. But x265 is ALSO deterministic, so re-probing with
the same N re-encoded IDENTICAL clips and returned byte-identical bitrates. Those
duplicates were pooled, and since SE = sd/sqrt(n) THE ERROR BAR SHRANK ON EVIDENCE
THAT DID NOT EXIST — and it got worse the more a film was "refined", the exact
opposite of pooling's purpose.

    unit                        stored n   distinct   cxRSE reported   truth   under by
    Dunkirk (2017)                 64         16          0.1986       0.3972    100%
    Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance     56         16          0.1790       0.3344     87%
    Casino Royale (1967)           16          8          0.1300       0.1903     46%
    Drive (2011)                   24         16          0.1508       0.1844     22%
    Gladiator (2000)               24         16          0.1379       0.1648     19%

Every unit visited more than once was affected in proportion to how often; units
visited once (Frozen Planet II, Lessons in Chemistry) were correct. WHY 24 READINGS
YIELD 16 DISTINCT: samplesFor() returns 8 or 16 and the 16-grid CONTAINS the 8-grid
(span*i/8 == span*2i/16), so a second visit at N=16 re-measured all 8 and added 8.

*** AND A WARNING ABOUT CHECKING THIS CLAIM. *** A 2026-08-19 review reported that
the Dunkirk anecdote "does not reproduce" — because it recomputed from the CURRENT
cache, where the duplicates have already been re-counted and the number cannot exist
by construction. It reproduces to three decimals from the pre-repair backup
(/config/probe-cache.json.bak-dedupe-20260819052712. — note the trailing dot in the
filename). A BEFORE/AFTER CLAIM CAN ONLY BE CHECKED AGAINST THE "BEFORE" ARTIFACT,
and if that artifact is a backup the claim must name it. Dunkirk after the repair:
48 distinct of 64, cxRSE 0.1667 — still the library maximum.

TWO FIXES, BOTH NEEDED:
  1. EFFECTIVE n. sampleStats counts DISTINCT clips — by position where known, falling
     back to distinct values for legacy entries (two scenes returning identical
     bitrates to 5 dp does not happen, so an exact repeat is a re-measured clip).
     Reported as `sampleNEff`; `sampleN` still counts readings.
  2. PHASE-SHIFTED GRIDS. probe-film.sh takes --phase (0 <= p < 1, in units of one
     sample gap); probe.js advances it per visit along a van der Corput sequence
     (0, 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/8...), so each visit lands in the largest remaining gap and
     pooling reduces the error bar for the right reason. `visits` is persisted.

*** CONSEQUENCE FOR EXISTING DATA, AND IT IS PERMANENT: every pooled cxRSE written
before this fix is too small, AND THEY ARE NOT RE-DERIVABLE. *** The fix corrects
future measurements and re-derives effective n for existing ones, but the samples stay
duplicated until those units are visited again under a new phase.

E.2.5  UNCERTAINTY IS PUBLISHED, NOT HIDDEN
Until 2026-08-18 a number pinned to +-3% and one pinned to +-40% printed identically,
and the Audit tab would act on either.
THE SQUARE ROOT HALVES THE ERROR: a relative error e on the target becomes ~e/2 on the
index, so a complexity known only to +-24% still yields a BPP+ good to about +-12%.
bppRSE() returns rse/2, and BPP_RSE_LOOSE = 0.10 ON THE INDEX is a looser gate than it
first appears.
UI CONTRACT: past the threshold the score renders as `bpp+*`, with the real number and
error bar in the tooltip. A NULL RSE IS TREATED AS CONFIDENT, NOT DOUBTFUL — most of
the library has no per-sample data yet, and marking 1000 films provisional would make
the mark meaningless. The mark is for films we KNOW are loosely measured.
STATE AT THE TIME (the weakest live area): 169 units carry a cxRSE (148 movies, 21
seasons), 116 pooled past 8 effective samples. Median cxRSE where it exists is 0.111
(p90 0.128), i.e. ~+-5.5% on the index. spreadRatio median 4.01, p75 6.24, p90 9.10.
    AND THE REFINE QUEUE CANNOT REACH MOST OF THE LIBRARY: 761 of 1031 units can NEVER
    gain a measured error bar under the shipped selection rule. nextImprecise() only
    picks units it ALREADY BELIEVES imprecise, and with no cxRSE that judgement falls
    back to spreadRatio > 6 — so a unit under the threshold is never revisited, never
    gains per-sample data, and never produces a cxRSE that could disagree with the
    proxy. THE UNITS WE ARE MOST CONFIDENT ABOUT ARE THE ONES NOTHING HAS CHECKED. And
    spreadRatio is max/min, which one near-black frame inflates. The proposed fix is
    shaped like STALE_SHARE: give never-sampled units a small guaranteed share of the
    queue. NOT DONE.

E.2.6  TV SEASONS
A season is measured as a whole: episodes sampled, complexity episode-weighted,
all-or-nothing. `disagree` records how much the sampled episodes disagreed. Median
disagree 0.241 across 146 seasons, 71 exceed 0.25 — SEASON NUMBERS ARE MUCH SOFTER
THAN MOVIE NUMBERS, and SEASON_DISAGREE_MAX = 0.25 gates them out of disk-reclamation
suggestions (that gate removed 92 GB of fiction in one pass).
A SERIES row is the episode-weighted mean of its own measured seasons, basis
`measured:series`, and requires EVERY season measured — a partial average would
silently describe a subset.

E.2.7  THE CRF LADDER PILOT                       [SOLID, 20 films, 2026-08-18]
Four rungs (CRF 16/20/24/28), 4 samples each, IDENTICAL SCENES at every rung.
Cost, measured not guessed: ~10 min/film, ~3.5 h for the pilot; library-wide would be
~170 h, which is why it is a pilot.

  FINDING 1 — THE RELATIONSHIP IS A CLEAN EXPONENTIAL. NO KNEES.
      R2 >= 0.977 on every film, median 0.998. Max |curvature| 0.008.
    log(bitrate) against CRF is a straight line for all 20, across the full range of
    content from Arrival (complexity 0.028) to 12 Angry Men (1.09). The
    -15%-per-CRF-point form is the right SHAPE.

  FINDING 2 — THE SLOPE VARIES ~2x BETWEEN FILMS, AND IT IS NOT NOISE.
      median -13.6%/point, range -9.9% to -19.4%, SD 2.98 points
    Because each individual fit is precise, that spread is real content variation. The
    library-wide constant should be -13.6%, not the -15.0% used before.

  FINDING 3 — SLOPE TRACKS SUPPLY ACROSS THE LIBRARY.  *** FALSIFIED — see E.3.1. ***
      Spearman(slope, supply R)     -0.750
      Spearman(slope, biasFactor)   +0.736
      Spearman(slope, src Mb/s)     -0.731
      Spearman(slope, complexity)   +0.308    <- barely anything
      Spearman(slope, year)         +0.121    <- nothing
      shallowest: Blair Witch (14% supplied) -9.9%, Tangerine (26%) -10.0%
      steepest:   Casablanca (157%) -19.4%, Asteroid City (163%) -18.5%,
                  Parasite (558%) -17.8%
    This LOOKED like a direct, non-circular instrument for source exhaustion and was
    the most promising result of the ladder. The starvation experiment killed it:
    holding content constant, slope predicts pinning at +0.335 while R predicts it at
    -0.924. The library-wide correlation was CROSS-FILM CONFOUNDING — grainy old films
    are both starved and shallow-sloped for separate reasons.
    THE GENERAL LESSON: a correlation measured ACROSS the library is not evidence about
    a mechanism WITHIN a film. Only a controlled experiment separates them, and one
    costs ~10 minutes per film.

  WHAT SURVIVES: findings 1 and 2. THE SLOPE IS NOT AN INPUT TO BPP+, and a 2x spread
  around a constant that only sets a read-time conversion does not justify 170 h of
  encoding. Do not build the library-wide ladder cron.

  THE EXTERNAL CROSS-CHECK, verified 2026-08-28: the 8-film starvation set at level 1
  gives a mean of -17.4%/CRF with a range of -19.5% to -13.6% (E.8), so our controlled
  films sit at or above the library figure. Consistent, and the bound is satisfied.
  The 59-film perPoint content-line run (E.8.6) later measured -13.8%/CRF, essentially
  exact against -13.6%.

================================================================================
E.3  SOURCE PINNING, REMUXES AND biasFactor
================================================================================
Backup section 5 in full, plus 8.3, 8.7 and trap 11. This is the ONLY defect ever
demonstrated under control, and the correction is applied to every score.

THE MECHANISM. The probe's input is OUR COPY, and CRF 20 cannot ask for detail the
source does not contain. A starved copy measures as an EASY film, its target sinks
toward that copy's own bitrate, and the deficient file scores close to 100. *** THE
WORSE THE COPY, THE MORE IT FLATTERS ITSELF *** — so BPP+ was systematically
OVERSTATED, most where accuracy matters. This is the worst possible SHAPE of error:
it hits the starved side of an upgrade comparison and not the good side.

FIRST PROOF, same film, same probe, only the source changed. Paris, Texas, 2026-08-12,
a 2.22 GB copy replaced by a 12.73 GB Criterion transfer:
    srcBitrate x6.02   ->   complexity 0.05982 -> 0.13939   (x2.33)
And Easy Rider, measured BPP+ 78, was visibly full of grain-shimmer on flat walls;
Brennan put it at "a 60ish, or even lower like a 40" by eye. Corrected: ~65.

THE PREDICTOR IS R, with no genre or year heuristics. R < 1 means the file sits BELOW
its own measured target — the probe ran out of source before it ran out of appetite —
so the number is a FLOOR. R > 1 means the probe had headroom.

E.3.1  THE STARVATION EXPERIMENT — THE DECISIVE RESULT      [SOLID, 2026-08-18]
Tooling: controller/scripts/probe-starve.sh + scripts/starve-experiment.js.
Data: data/starve-experiment.json.

DESIGN. Take 8 films the model ALREADY TRUSTS (R >= 1.2, no correction applied),
starve them to 1/2 and 1/4 bitrate, re-measure. The starving re-encodes ONLY THE
SAMPLED CLIPS — a full-film re-encode is ~7 h, and starving the exact clips the ladder
measures is equivalent and takes seconds. Content is byte-identical across levels, so
ANYTHING THAT MOVED WAS CAUSED BY STARVATION AND NOTHING ELSE. `cx20` at each level IS
the complexity the probe would report for that copy, so the understatement is MEASURED,
not inferred. THE PREDICTION WAS RECORDED IN THE SCRIPT BEFORE THE RUN.

  RESULT 1 — REAL, MONOTONIC, UNIVERSAL IN THE SAMPLE. 8/8 films flatten monotonically
  as the copy is starved; mean flattening unstarved -> most-starved is 4.12 slope
  points, over 16 controlled points (8 films x 2 starved levels).

  RESULT 2 — R PREDICTS THE UNDERSTATEMENT; LADDER SLOPE DOES NOT.
      Spearman(observed R,   pinning understatement)   -0.924
      Spearman(ladder slope, pinning understatement)   +0.335
  *** THE LADDER-SLOPE HYPOTHESIS IS FALSIFIED. *** biasFactor's design, indexing on R,
  is VINDICATED rather than merely necessary. The hypothesis had been that a starved
  source has thrown detail away, so lowering CRF buys nothing back and the bitrate
  stops responding — a shallow slope — making slope a direct, non-circular measure of
  exhaustion. It looked strong at rho -0.750 across 20 films (E.2.7 finding 3).
  DO NOT REVIVE IT.

  RESULT 3 — *** THE OLD R_TRUST = 1.2 CAP WAS WRONG. *** 6 of the 16 points sit ABOVE
  R 1.2, where the live rule applied NOTHING; they average x1.270 understatement, and
  Requiem for a Dream at R 2.58 still shows x1.269.
  WHY THE CAP EXISTED, AND WHY IT WAS REASONABLE: no banked upgrade pair sits above
  R 1.2, because you only re-download films that were bad, so a factor up there would
  have been INVENTED rather than measured. Sound reasoning FOR OBSERVATIONAL DATA. The

THE ANCHORING, RECOVERED AND WRITTEN DOWN 2026-08-20. It had never been recorded beyond
that one phrase, which is not enough to reproduce a fit:

    R_anchored = level * R_live * under        under = cx20(base) / cx20(level)

That is the OBSERVED R the library would compute for the starved copy: its bitrate is
level * src, and the complexity it would report is the base complexity divided by
`under`. The anchoring uses the LIVE probe's base complexity (8-16 samples) in place of
the experiment's own (3 samples), so the absolute scale comes from the probe and only
the RATIO comes from the experiment.

CONFIRMED BY REPRODUCTION: least squares of log(under/1.06) on log(R_anchored), ALL 16
POINTS, R_live taken PRE-audio-fix, returns A 1.337, B -0.349, rho -0.915 — the
then-shipped constants exactly. (The "n=15" in an older note is a slip: the fit
reproduces with all 16. An earlier edition GUESSED that Blade Runner 2049 at level 0.5
had been excluded for setting the floor. The reproduction shows it was not. Guess
withdrawn.)

*** `under` IS ON BOTH SIDES OF THIS FIT, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. *** It is the
y-variable and it also sits inside R_anchored. That looks exactly like a mistake and it
is not: at runtime biasFactor only ever has the OBSERVED R of a starved file, which is
inflated by the very understatement being predicted. A calibration curve must be fitted
on the axis it will be EVALUATED on. The consequence is that rho here CANNOT be read as
evidence of a causal relationship — the self-reference induces a POSITIVE bias, so a
strong NEGATIVE rho survives it rather than being produced by it. Use the unanchored
own-R rho (-0.924) for the causal claim and this one only for the calibration.
That gave 1.337 * R^-0.348 instead of the unanchored 1.414 * R^-0.298 — slightly
weaker, which is the right direction to err.

WAS 3 SAMPLES PER STARVATION POINT ENOUGH? Probably, on n=1 evidence. The 8-sample
re-run mostly failed (docs/REPORT-starve-8s-and-banding-starvation-2026-08-21.md) but
ONE film completed at both sample counts: The Social Network's understatement moved
-1.1% at level 0.5 and -2.6% at 0.25, against the ~6% discrepancy that drove the

E.3.4  THE x-AXIS HAD AUDIO IN IT — FIXED 2026-08-20        [SOLID, MAJOR]
R must be video-only on BOTH sides, for the same reason audio came out of the numerator.
It was not. probe-film.sh takes srcBitrate from the video stream when ffprobe reports
one and FALLS BACK TO THE CONTAINER TOTAL when it does not, and on the fallback path
every audio track sat inside R. An inflated R makes biasFactor too SMALL, so the
correction was UNDER-applied — on multi-dub and lossless-audio releases, i.e. exactly
the class the audio rule exists to stop flattering. The same bias, one layer deeper, on
the correction's own input.

MEASURED (ffprobe ground truth, all 898 movies, 2026-08-20):
    container-path (polluted)  361        video-path (already clean)  537
    of the polluted rows, 271 changed biasFactor
    BPP+ was inflated by a median 2.17%, max 8.33% (The Witch, 39% audio)
    16 films sat in the wrong band

THE FIX IS SCOPED, NOT BLANKET, AND THAT DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE FINDING. The obvious
one-liner — subtract audioBps from every srcBitrate — would have DEFLATED the 537
already-clean rows by a median 2.1% (max 8.5%), A NEW ERROR LARGER THAN THE ONE BEING
REPAIRED. probe-film.sh now records which numerator it used (`srcBasis`),
scripts/backfill-srcbasis.js filled it in for every pre-existing entry, and videoR() in
probe.js subtracts only on the container path. Applied: 251 movies fell, all of them,
median -2.35%; the controls that must not move did not.

*** DO NOT "FIX" THE AUDIO IN probe-starve.sh. *** All 8 calibration films are
container-path files (audio 7.3-25.5%), but probe-starve.sh extracts its clips with
-an -sn and starves them with `-b:v level*SRCBPS`, so a starved clip genuinely CARRIES
level*SRCBPS bits of VIDEO and the R attributed to it is that clip's true video-only R.
The audio inflated how much each level actually starved, and THE LABEL MOVED WITH IT.
Only levels below 1.0 enter the fit, so the untouched control — the one copy that really
does carry audio it is not credited for — never lands on the x-axis. The experiment was
self-consistent; only the library-side application was wrong. "Fixing" it would break
that identity and mis-calibrate PIN_A/PIN_B. There is a comment saying so at the point

E.3.6  THE GRAIN CONTAMINATION WORRY                        [UNTESTED]
The OBSERVATIONAL fit cannot separate pinning from grain: when a starved copy of a
grainy film is replaced by a better transfer, complexity rises both because more detail
is present AND because more grain is resolved. Some grain may therefore be absorbed into
a coefficient labelled "source pinning".
THE CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT IS LARGELY IMMUNE, a real advantage of the method: the same
film at three starvation levels has the same grain BY CONSTRUCTION, so what it measures
is starvation. If the two methods agree where they overlap, the contamination in the
observational fit is bounded.
Closing it needs a grain covariate that is NOT raw complexity — raw complexity is what
the grain question is ABOUT and cannot also be its control. gridRatio was the candidate.

E.3.8  R IS NOT THE RATIO BPP+ DIVIDES BY                    (backup trap 11)
Treating it as one produces a convincing false alarm. R divides by the UNCORRECTED probe
rate; BPP+ divides by the CORRECTED target (cxEff * headroom), so R overstates it by
roughly biasFactor. MEASURED across 898 movies, 2026-08-20:
    bppPlus / (100*sqrt(R))            median 0.847
    bppPlus / (100*sqrt(bpp/target))   median 1.000 — an identity, as it must be
Plotting the library against R therefore puts EVERY film below the 100*sqrt(R) curve and
looks exactly like the model failing to fit its own data. bpp-lab's Quality curve view
did this until 2026-08-18.
AND R ITSELF IS NOT src/probe. Two corrections sit between them, and skipping either has
burned an analysis already:
  (a) CODEC NORMALISATION — the probe always encodes x265, so an h264 source is divided
      by 1.6. R = srcBitrate / (probeBitrate * eff). Omitting it inflates every h264 row
      by 1.6x and makes starved copies look supplied.
  (b) AUDIO — `srcBitrate` is the container total on 470 of 1044 units
      (`srcBasis: 'container'`); R has that audio subtracted and `rawR` does not
      (E.3.4). Median container audio share is 9.8%, max ~47%.
The dataset carries `R` (corrected, what scoring uses), `rawR` and `srcBasis` so an
offline analysis can reproduce any of the three deliberately.

E.4.0  THE PREMISE — AND THE NEGATIVE RESULT THAT NEARLY KILLED IT
The false premise the project reasoned from for weeks: "artifacts are caused by bits
and content, therefore they carry nothing beyond bits and content." That is wrong. The
causal chain is

    artifacts = f(bits, content, ENCODER, SETTINGS, MASTER, GENERATIONS, PREPROCESSING)

and BPP+ sees THE FIRST TWO ONLY. Two files with identical bits and identical content
can differ in quality by up to 2x through the other five. A library spanning Bluray
remuxes to WEBRips, a dozen release groups and twenty years of encoder practice varies
enormously in exactly those. So artifacts MUST carry information BPP+ lacks.

This exists because Brennan refused to accept the negative below and demanded a reason
rather than a dataset: "Tell me through logical reasoning why it doesnt work. Not via
data but by logic. If we cant reason as to why it cant work, then it can, and we need
to FIND the experiment and the formula to make the concept work."

THE NEGATIVE, AND IT IS STILL THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE IN THIS FILE (backup 11.11).
On CVQAD, within-sequence correlations look strong (blur -0.839, grain +0.773) and are
mostly the detectors tracking bitrate. Removing bits with progressively fairer models:

    bits shape removed        cambi    block     blur    grain
    global linear            -0.194   -0.145   -0.492   +0.283
    global cubic             -0.143   -0.071   -0.456   +0.258
    per-sequence LINEAR      +0.048   -0.198   -0.466   +0.478
    per-sequence QUADRATIC   -0.062   -0.111   +0.027   +0.031
And the cleanest test of all — same source, same delivered bitrate, different encoder
preset, so bits are held exactly constant — gives coin flips:
    cambi 48%   block 49%   blur 49%   grain 51%   (n = 86-89 pairs)
ONCE EACH CONTENT'S OWN QUALITY-VS-BITS CURVE IS MODELLED WITH ANY CURVATURE, EVERY
DETECTOR ADDS NOTHING.

WHY THAT IS NOT "THE FRAMEWORK IS DEAD", and the reading has two parts.
  (1) Blur and grain survive a per-sequence LINEAR fit and die under a QUADRATIC one.
      The quality-vs-bits relation SATURATES; blur and grain are curved functions of
      bits; so they are proxies for WHERE A FILM SITS ON ITS OWN DIMINISHING-RETURNS
      CURVE. That is consistent with everything else measured — inert on our narrow
      ladders (1.013 and 1.016 swing at a 30% cut), strong across CVQAD's 90x range,
      and vanishing when the curve is modelled. And it is still the question we care
      about: BPP+ is linear in log bits against a complexity-derived target and models
      NO SATURATION AT ALL. Brennan's framing — "if increasing the bits DOESNT improve
      the viewing experience then our perception of the film being bit-starved is
      wrong" — is precisely a question about position on the knee. The honest reframing
      is that we do not have artifact detectors that beat bits+content, and we may have
      SATURATION detectors, which is a different instrument aimed at the same decision.
  (2) EVERY CLIP IN A CVQAD SEQUENCE IS THE SAME MASTER THROUGH THE SAME ENCODER,
      varying only bitrate and one preset step. That corpus holds constant precisely
      the variables that make artifacts informative, so IT IS STRUCTURALLY INCAPABLE OF

CAVEAT ON THE CORPUS, kept because it cuts the other way too: CVQAD is 1080p UGC and
broadcast, and its per-sequence curve is a near-perfect within-content fit on ~6 points.
BPP+'s content model is a cross-film complexity estimate carrying real error. "Adds
nothing over a perfect content model" is not "adds nothing over BPP+", and that gap is

GRAIN IS REHABILITATED. Its swing here is 1.121x against 1.044x on the encoder axis and
1.014x on generations. IT IS A GRAIN DETECTOR; it was written off twice on axes it had
no reason to be good at. JUDGE A DETECTOR ON THE AXIS IT LIVES ON.

*** THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG AND THE REASON MATTERS. *** Denoising was expected to make
encoding EASIER and banding to improve. BANDING GETS FAR WORSE INSTEAD — 2.437x median,
and 11.8x on The General. The mechanism is the masking effect running in reverse: grain
was DITHERING the gradients, and removing it exposes the banding underneath. Only 2 of
10 films showed cambi or block improving. This corroborates Norkin DCC 2026, which is
why AV2 added debanding to the grain-synthesis step.

AND IT PRODUCES THE FIRST REAL CASE OF THE COVERING SET EARNING ITS NAME. High banding
is AMBIGUOUS alone — either too few bits or a denoised source — and those demand
OPPOSITE actions:
    too few bits     -> a bigger file of the same release helps
    denoised source  -> a bigger file does NOT help; a different release is needed
GRAIN DISAMBIGUATES. A denoised file reads LOW grain; a bit-starved file keeps its grain
and bands anyway. Neither detector can call it alone; together they identify the cause.

    ENCODER AXIS   was the data spent competently?          BPP+ CANNOT SEE THIS AT ALL,

shouldnt be ... then its a higher #".

EXCHANGE RATES, each derived on the axis the detector actually lives on:

E.4.0f  THE DIRECTION IS SPECIAL AT SPANNING PROVENANCE    [SOLID, 2026-08-27]
Tested where provenance is KNOWN — on the controlled experiments, not the field.

E.4.2  WHY THE GROUP-LEVEL RESULTS SURVIVE WHILE THE PER-FILM ORDERING DOES NOT
The asymmetry is the whole lesson of this project. The four controlled axes encode THE
SAME CLIPS at each rung, so scene selection cancels exactly there. The codec control, the
720p control, the YIFY null and the WEB-vs-Bluray gap are GROUP MEANS, and a zero-mean
within-file nuisance adds variance to a group mean WITHOUT BIASING IT. All of those are
correct. It is specifically the PER-FILM ORDERING — the only thing the rule actually ships
— that is contaminated.
    THE FRAMEWORK LOOKED HEALTHY ON EVERY CONTRAST AND IS WEAKEST ON THE ONE THING IT SELLS.

E.4.4  TWO CHALLENGES TO THE SHRINKAGE, one resolved and one not
  - IS IT f OR sqrt(f)? RESOLVED by reasoning, not measurement, because it is a choice of
    objective rather than an empirical question. f is MSE-optimal per film; sqrt(f) is
    spread-matching, preserving the ensemble histogram at the cost of over-adjusting every
    individual title. This score is consumed PER FILM — the Disk and Upgrade paths both
    threshold individual units and nothing downstream reads the spread — so the loss
    function is per-film squared error and f is right. f is what ships, so NO CHANGE; what
    was wrong was the JUSTIFICATION, which had been sold as an estimation result. The
    choice matters: sqrt(0.109)/0.109 = 3.0x, larger than any measurement dispute here.
  - provFraction has F = 1.95 at permutation p = 0.124, with about half the point estimate
    being the null expectation of eta^2. 11.17 met the same statistic at F = 1.07 and
    correctly called it uninformative. A LOWER BOUND THAT CANNOT BE DISTINGUISHED FROM
    ZERO IS A LOWER BOUND OF ZERO. UNRESOLVED — but largely superseded by E.4.5, which
    measures the same quantity a better way.

E.4.7  THE FIELD FACTOR AS FIRST MEASURED (backup 11.17, 11.26-11.28)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The controlled axes all hold bits and content exactly constant. THE LIBRARY NEVER DOES,
so the field version must read a RESIDUAL against a bits+content model. Two hypotheses
had to be separated: the residual is PROVENANCE (build on it) or MODEL ERROR (do not).

FIRST: IT IS NOT NOISE (2026-08-26). Two independent readings of the same film's
banding — the nightly probe's 8 clips and the ladder lossless rung's 4 DIFFERENT clips:
    residual correlation 0.861,  Spearman-Brown 0.926,  n=101
So ~86% of a single reading's residual is a stable property of the FILE.

SECOND: NEITHER CRUDE PROXY EXPLAINS IT.
    content features  (luma, resolution, cx^2)    7.2% of the residual
    provenance labels (source tier, codec, year)  5.7%
    -> ~73% STABLE AND UNATTRIBUTED
Do NOT read the low provenance number as evidence against provenance. Source tier is a
terrible proxy: "Bluray-1080p" covers a careful remux-derived encode and a lazy one
alike, and encoder settings, generation count and denoising appear in no label.

THIRD: THE RELEASE-GROUP TEST IS UNINFORMATIVE FOR A DATA REASON, NOT A MODEL ONE.
Group identity is the best available provenance proxy — groups have house styles, and
content has no reason to cluster by who encoded it. Result: F = 1.07, 4.8% explained,
null. But the sample cannot show the effect:

      is unrecoverable from the filename
    - the groups that survive are YTS(30) YIFY(29) LAMA(5) ETRG(4) — ALL small-file
      re-encoders with near-identical philosophy. The careful groups (SPARKS, RARBG,
      remuxes) are exactly the ones that were renamed.
RESTRICTED RANGE: the same class of error as the CVQAD null (E.4.0). A null from a
sample with no variation in the treatment is not evidence.
    THE UNBLOCK IS A DATABASE QUERY, NOT AN EXPERIMENT: Radarr and Sonarr retain the

THE SHIPPED SHAPE (backup 11.29). What the controlled work established is that our

E.4.9  THE SHRINKAGE HISTORY, AND THE YIFY NULL (backup 11.37, 11.38, 11.50)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SHRINKAGE WAS WRONG, AND THE ANCHOR MEASUREMENT IS WHAT EXPOSED IT. Adopting the
measured 33.1% produced adjustments of +-39.8% at 5-95 and +-78.4% worst case, which
FAILS the external bound. The anchor measurement was clean, so something else had to be.
    THE ARITHMETIC LOCATED IT EXACTLY. The same experiment measured dP/dgen = 0.112 P-sd
    per generation. ONE STANDARD DEVIATION OF P IS THEREFORE 8.9 GENERATIONS, and the
    library's +-3 sd span is +-27 GENERATIONS. Nothing here is twenty-seven generations
    from its master. Even shrunk by the reliability of 0.393 it is +-10.5 generations,
    which is still not a thing that exists.
WHY RELIABILITY WAS THE WRONG QUANTITY, stated carefully because it was not obviously
wrong: split-half reliability answers "how much of P is a COMMON FACTOR across disjoint
detector halves". That is real, and it is what established the factor exists at all. BUT
A COMMON FACTOR IS NOT A PROVENANCE FACTOR — anything shared across detectors and not
captured by the bits+content surface lands in it: shooting style, grade, lens choice,
mastering house, deliberate softness. Those are properties of a FILM, and NO DOWNLOAD
FIXES THEM.
    total variance of P                    1.0000 (standardised)
    between-source-label variance          0.0115
    PROVENANCE FRACTION, lower bound       0.0115  ->  sd 0.107 P-sd
    split-half reliability, in use before  0.393   ->  3.7x too large
A BRACKET, NOT A POINT, and both ends derived rather than chosen:
    FLOOR   0.107  between-label variance. A LOWER bound because labels are coarse — two
                   Blurays can differ by release group, preset and generation while
                   sharing a label, and that real provenance sits in the within-label term
    CEILING 0.149  from physics: requiring the library's +-3 sd span to be at most 4
                   generations gives s <= 4 * 0.1121 / 3
    0.393 sits far outside both.
ONLY THEN WAS THE EXTERNAL BOUND CONSULTED, which is the point — shrinking P until the
adjustments looked plausible would be fitting to the bound and would destroy its value:
    shrinkage 0.393 (old)  +-1 sd -18.7%  5-95 +-28.9%  FAILS
    shrinkage 0.107 (new)  +-1 sd  -5.5%  5-95  +-8.9%  PASSES
SHRINKAGE IS A SCALAR ON P, so the ORDERING of films is completely unchanged and every
control is untouched. What changes is the SIZE. *** MEASURING THE TWO CONSTANTS PROPERLY
MADE THE ADJUSTMENT SMALLER, NOT LARGER, AND THAT SHOULD BE SAID PLAINLY. ***

THE ORIGINAL DIAGNOSIS OF THE FAILED CHECK WAS A DETECTOR FLOOR, AND IT WAS WRONG
(backup 11.40 -> 11.41). P uses log(cambi), and the 266 added units are HIGH-bitrate
Blurays of which 20.1% sit below cambi 0.05, against 0.3% in the low-bitrate tercile; the
proposal was a soft offset log(L + c) with c MEASURED, never chosen.
scripts/cambi-floor.js measured c directly and REFUTED THE PREMISE. 14 films spanning the
cambi range, 8 independent 2-second reads each:

    low          4     0.108      0.120       1.65
    mid          4     0.715      0.726       1.02
    high         6     2.380      2.452       0.92
sd/level is about 1 AT EVERY LEVEL. There is no crossover, so THERE IS NO FLOOR, and the
c = 0.4711 the script printed is a computational artifact of assuming a monotone crossing
that does not exist. IT WAS NOT APPLIED.
AND log IS THE RIGHT TRANSFORM AFTER ALL: constant sd/level means MULTIPLICATIVE noise,
whose variance-stabilising transform is exactly log (sd(log cambi) low 2.00, mid 2.06,
high 1.68). THE TRANSFORM WAS NEVER THE PROBLEM — THE SIZE OF THE NOISE WAS. It is not
instrument error, it is SCENE HETEROGENEITY: Marty Supreme's eight reads run 0.005,
0.254, 3.039, 3.375, 3.679, 3.833, 6.796, 8.5 — A 1700x RANGE INSIDE ONE FILM. Banding
lives in a handful of smooth-gradient scenes.

changed is the explanation.

FIRST ATTEMPT AT THE FIX WAS KILLED AFTER 4 UNITS, for two reasons both worth recording:
  1. TOO SLOW. Re-running artifact-backfill.js at 12 clips took 12.5 min/unit, not the ~2
     min estimated, because --stratify interleaves by source label and therefore started
     on Bluray REMUXES, whose decode cost scales with source bitrate. 300 units would
     have been 60+ hours.
  2. IT COULD NOT HAVE ANSWERED THE QUESTION. artifact-backfill.js averages its clips and
     DISCARDS the per-clip values, so a 12-clip run yields one number per unit — a single
     noisy contrast, not a payoff curve. THE DESIGN WAS WRONG, NOT JUST SLOW.
Replaced by a WITHIN-UNIT design (scripts/clip-reliability.js), cheaper AND strictly more
informative: read every unit at 12 positions and KEEP EVERY CLIP. Two disjoint k-clip
means from the same unit, correlated across units, ARE the reliability of a k-clip
measurement. 1.9 min/unit.

E.4.13  CONSTRUCTIONS ONE THROUGH SEVEN — the dead ends, in order
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Backup 11.6-11.10, 11.19-11.21, 11.25, 11.33-11.35. Every one of these tried to turn
artifacts into a BPP+ ADJUSTMENT and failed on the same rock: IT NEEDS AN EXCHANGE RATE,
AND CONVERTING AN ARTIFACT INTO BITS DIVIDES BY A SHALLOW SLOPE WHICH AMPLIFIES EVERY
CONFOUND. Read this before proposing an eighth.

(1) THE COVERING-SET MIXTURE. Each artifact gives m_a = (T_a/L_a)^(1/S_a), the bitrate
    multiplier bringing artifact a to its visibility threshold; p_a is the probability
    a is the one BINDING; ln m = sum_a p_a ln m_a; BPP+_adj = BPP+ / sqrt(m).
    NOT SUM, because correlated detectors cannot double-count. AND NOT A HARD MAX EITHER
    — that was a cut-off in disguise and it was caught in use. Brennan, 2026-08-26, moving
    the grain slider one step: "at 0.91 big lebowski is +254 but at 0.92 its +0". A max
    over POINT estimates ignores how well each is known: The Big Lebowski's banding
    estimate is sharp (m 0.061, sigma ~0.1), its grain estimate useless (sigma ~8, because
    S_grain is 0.025 and everything divides by S^2). After the mixture fix it moves
    150 -> 151 across the WHOLE slider range.
    NO CUT-OFFS, AND THIS IS A DESIGN RULE. Four guards (L_FLOOR 0.25, M_UP_FLOOR 0.5,
    M_CAP 50, FLAT 0.25) were added one at a time after watching the lab produce specific
    absurdities, and ALL FOUR WERE DELETED once the variance was computed properly:
        Var(ln m) = [ Var(ln L) + (ln m)^2 Var(S) ] / S^2 + (c * outside)^2 / S^2
    *** THE DISTANCE MUST NOT BE HINGED. *** It was first written as max(0, loEdge - lnM,
    lnM - hiEdge) — exactly zero inside the ladder, switching on outside it. Divided by
    S^2 that kink is amplified by 1/S^2, which for a grain slope of 0.025 is 1548x,
    TURNING A KINK INTO A CLIFF. It was a hard cut-off wearing a variance costume.
    Both constants MEASURED, not chosen:
      Var(ln L) = 0.059  from 21 films carrying two independent readings of their own
                  banding. The additive noise floor fitted to ZERO — CAMBI is scale-free,
                  agreeing with itself to ~24% relative across three orders of magnitude.
                  THERE IS NO FLOOR TO FIND, which FALSIFIED the expected fix. This
                  replaced an INFERRED 0.612 from split-half reliability; reliability
                  answers "how much of the between-FILM spread is real", NOT "how far is
                  one reading from another of the same film".
      c = 0.306 per ln-unit  fitting each ladder on 0.7-1.3 only and scoring the wide
                  rungs. Residuals: sd 0.059 at distance <0.4, 0.198 at 0.4-0.8, 0.219 at
                  0.8-1.5 with mean -0.206.

(2) BANDING SATURATES, AND CAN REVERSE  [SOLID] — the first direct test of the
    extrapolation the upward direction rested on. Fit on 0.7-1.3, predict the wide rungs:
    ratio 1.00 at every rung inside the window, 0.90 at 0.5x, 0.62 at 0.3x. KNOCKED UP
    GOES FURTHER AND REVERSES — 5.475 at 0.85x, 4.989 at 0.30x — because at low bitrate
    the encoder stops producing banding and starts producing BLOCKING, smoothing the
    gradients away. So banding is NOT MONOTONIC in bits, and for many films the crossing
    point does not exist at all. m = (T/L)^(1/S) has no way to say "never" and returns a
    huge number instead. That, compounded with (3), is what produced The Godfather at 782.

(3) THE COVERING HOLE IS GRAIN-SHAPED  [SOLID]
        corr(|S_block|, complexity) = -0.459 (n=21), replicated -0.315 (n=29)

(4) THE GRAIN AXIS SERVES TWO ARTIFACTS AT ONCE — OPEN  [WEAK]
    Brennan, by eye: "our slider currently is moving films that I dont consider to really
    have much grain at all." He is right, AND THE FIRST EXPLANATION OFFERED WAS WRONG.
    THE WRONG DIAGNOSIS: grainOf at the lossless rung reads ~1.0 for nearly every film
    (0.61-1.29 across a 10x span in complexity), so it looked like a ratio to a
    meaningless number. IT IS NOT THE MECHANISM — the absolute level CANCELS out of m
    exactly: Full Metal Jacket m = 0.7463 at 1x, 0.7463 at 2x, 0.7463 at 0.5x.

    contamination makes grain UNDER-fire; flat-region masking would make MORE films move

    THE ACTUAL PROBLEM IS TAXONOMY. One measurement serves two artifacts: on a grainy film

    CONSEQUENCE: on films where blocking is actually VISIBLE the slope is ~2.2x the library
    median, so 1/S is about 4.3 rather than 9.7. THE REJECTION WAS QUANTITATIVELY
    OVERSTATED — argued with a slope drawn from films where blocking sits at its floor and
    physically cannot move, which are precisely the films the score does not need to judge.
    THE CONCLUSION STANDS; THE ARITHMETIC WAS PESSIMISTIC BY ABOUT A FACTOR OF TWO. Any
    future construction that needs a slope must use THE FILM'S OWN and say which regime it
    was measured in.

(8) THE MATCHED FILTER  [FALSIFIED — do not revive without fixing the noise model]
    Idea: stop converting artifacts to bits; treat the four detectors as a VECTOR, note
    that a bitrate change and an encoder change move that vector in different directions,
    and PROJECT each film's residual onto the encoder direction. A matched filter never
    divides by any single detector's slope.
        cos(u_bits, u_encoder) = 0.909 raw, 0.899 noise-whitened      angle ~25 deg
        orthogonal fraction of u_encoder surviving removal of bits: 0.42
        and that surviving component is almost ENTIRELY blocking (0.408 of 0.416)
    which is the two-axis result reached by an independent route.
    THREE DEFECTS, ALL WORTH NOT REPEATING:
      1. THE WRONG STATISTIC WAS PRE-REGISTERED. "cos > 0.9 means dead" assumed bits were
         UNKNOWN. bpp is known exactly, so the bits direction can be removed exactly and
         the quantity that matters is WHAT SURVIVES REMOVAL, not the cosine. Recognising
         this AFTER seeing cos = 0.909 is moving a goalpost, and it is recorded as such:
         the test's evidential force is reduced, not rescued.
      2. THE NOISE MODEL WAS WRONG, AND THIS IS THE REAL KILLER. Whitening used ladder
         scatter — WITHIN-film repeatability, ~0.02-0.07 in log. The library's BETWEEN-film
         residual sd is 0.17-1.34, i.e. 15-60x LARGER. Whitening by a noise that is not the
         dominant noise tunes the filter for the wrong problem, and the library spread it
         then reported was model error, not encoder differences.
      3. A VACUOUS CHECK REPORTED AS EVIDENCE. "corr(E, log bpp) = 0.0000, must be ~0" —
         OLS residuals are orthogonal to their own regressors BY ARITHMETIC. That check can
         never fail. Say so in the output so a later reader cannot mistake it for a pass.
    CONSEQUENCE: the 5-95 adjustment band came out at +-43% with a full range of +-83%,
    which fails the external bound the same way L* did. Rejected on that, before any
    standard error was looked at.

(9) THE DIRECT (T/L)^k RULE — FAILS DIFFERENTLY THAN PREDICTED, AND THE REAL FAILURE IS A
    DILEMMA WITH NO INTERIOR SOLUTION  [FALSIFIED]
    The rule was described here and on the Blend tab as "well-behaved downward, runs away
    upward". Measured (k=0.25, n=762) THAT DESCRIPTION IS WRONG AND THE TRUTH IS WORSE:
        p05 -8.6%   median +29.5%   p95 +56.1%   range -38.9% .. +84.5%
        library median BPP+ 64 -> 85, a +33% RE-BASING
        worst up / worst down = 2.2x, not the runaway that was claimed
    IT DOES NOT SCATTER FILMS. IT SQUASHES THEM TOWARD 100 FROM BOTH SIDES. Films at 40 go
    to ~70; films at 200 come down to ~130. That DISCARDS THE INFORMATION BPP+ ALREADY
    CARRIES, which is a worse failure than an ugly tail because it is invisible in a
    summary statistic.
    THE DILEMMA, ALGEBRAIC RATHER THAN EMPIRICAL:
        small k  score_a = 100*(T/L)^k is nearly FLAT in bitrate, so every film scores about
                 the same and blending drags BPP+ toward that common value. Information
                 destroyed.
        large k  to give score_a the same bitrate sensitivity as BPP+ (which is bpp^0.5)
                 needs k*S = 0.5, i.e. k = 0.5/S. For blocking S = 0.103 so k = 4.85 — and
                 (T/L)^(1/S) under a square root IS the bits-equivalent multiplier m. The
                 "seventh construction" at that k is ALGEBRAICALLY IDENTICAL to the first
                 six.
        THERE IS NO k IN BETWEEN. This closes the direct route for good, on algebra.
    AND A SEPARATE DEFECT WORTH ITS OWN LINE — THE GRAIN THRESHOLD MIS-FIRES ON SMOOTH
    CONTENT. Firing rates by complexity tercile:
        artifact      smooth    mid   grainy
        banding        26.0%  25.1%    8.7%
        blocking        4.3%   2.4%    2.0%
        blur           31.1%  24.3%   19.0%
        grain loss     56.7%  37.3%   15.8%     <- backwards, and diagnostic
    Grain loss firing on 56.7% of SMOOTH content is not damage detection: a digitally-shot
    film has little grain to retain, so "grain below 0.905" reads CONTENT. ANY RAW-THRESHOLD
    RULE INHERITS THIS. The provenance factor does NOT, because it residualises grain
    against complexity before reading it — an argument for the residual framing that was
    not anticipated when it was chosen.

(10) A VACUOUS ANALYSIS, CAUGHT BY ITS OWN UNANIMITY  [WITHDRAWN]
    Recorded because the mistake is subtle, the output looked like a decision tool, and it
    was one edit away from being written into the handoff as a feature. THE QUESTION: for a
    film flagged as damaged, would a bigger download fix it? The test compared the delivered
    file's artifact against OUR re-encode at 2.5x the bitrate. It returned "BITS DO NOT
    HELP" for 18 of 18 flagged artifacts.
    *** THE UNANIMITY WAS THE TELL. *** Every ladder rung is re-encoded FROM THE DELIVERED
    FILE, so an artifact already baked into the delivered pixels is INHERITED BY EVERY RUNG
    including 2.5x. You cannot un-band pixels that arrive already banded. THE TEST COULD
    ONLY EVER RETURN ONE ANSWER. Answering the original question needs a HIGHER-QUALITY
    SOURCE than the file being judged, which this library never has. DO NOT REBUILD THIS
    TEST ON LADDER DATA.
    WHAT THE SAME DATA CAN LEGITIMATELY ANSWER is whether the artifact responds to bitrate
    ON THAT CONTENT at all — the per-film version of the relevance gate. All 11 flagged
    films: 44 artifact/film pairs measured, ZERO responding in the wrong direction; of the
    23 pairs above visibility, 12 ARE INERT TO BITRATE on their own content. A flagged
    artifact that is inert is the case where BPP+ alone gives ACTIVELY MISLEADING advice.
        Mr. Robot S01   banding 7.354 (2.6x T)   slope +0.018  r2 0.07  NO RELATIONSHIP
        Severance S02   banding 8.262 (2.9x T)   slope -0.034  r2 0.70  visible and inert
        Bloodline S03   banding 10.247 (3.6x T)  slope -0.096  r2 0.70  visible, RESPONDS
    Bloodline sits at a HIGHER banding level and still responds, so "high banding implies
    inert" is NOT a rule — it is a tendency with real exceptions.
    A DISCREPANCY NOTED HONESTLY: the nightly probe reads Mr. Robot S01's banding at 10.94,
    the ladder's lossless rung at 7.354. Different runs, and LADDER ABSOLUTES ARE ALREADY
    KNOWN NOT TO TRAVEL BETWEEN RUNS. Both are far above 2.817 and the conclusion is
    unaffected, but do not quote the two side by side as if they were one measurement.

THE RELEVANCE GATE, AND A HARD BOUND ON THE UPWARD DIRECTION  [SOLID, n=666]

    complexity   n     cambi fires   block fires   either
    smooth      222        28.4%         5.4%       32.9%
    mid         222        26.6%         2.7%       28.4%
    grainy      222         9.0%         2.3%       11.3%
    cut points: cx < 0.119 smooth, cx > 0.176 grainy
Smooth and mid are indistinguishable. *** RESTATE THE GATE AS "GRAIN BLINDS", NOT
"SMOOTHNESS HELPS" *** — the tercile framing was an artifact of the smaller sample.
THE BOUND THIS PUTS ON THE UPWARD DIRECTION, and it is the number to quote whenever
someone proposes raising a score because nothing fired: on SMOOTH content, where banding
is most relevant, it fires on only 28.4% of files, SO A CLEAN BANDING READING RULES OUT AT
MOST 28% OF THE WAYS A FILE CAN BE BAD; on grainy content that falls to 9%. Finding a flaw
proves a flaw; not finding one proves little. NO BLENDING FORMULA CAN EXCEED THAT BOUND,
because it is a property of how often the detectors have anything to say.

P IS SYMMETRIC, AND THE 28% BOUND DOES NOT CARRY OVER  [SOLID]
The shipped rule moves a film up as readily as down, which deserved a challenge.
THE STATISTICAL TRAP, AVOIDED: splitting films by P and computing split-half reliability
within each group is CONDITIONING ON A COLLIDER, since P is built from both halves.
Instead, regress one detector half on the other and compare SLOPES either side of zero —
selection on the PREDICTOR does not bias the regression of the outcome on it.
    4 of 6 comparisons favour "agrees more about damage"   median t = 0.12
    the largest single |t| is 4.89 pointing the WRONG way, and its own mirror pairing
      points the right way — THE SIGNATURE OF NOISE, NOT STRUCTURE
VERDICT: NO ASYMMETRY; the symmetric rule is correct. AND THERE IS A REASON, which is why
this is a result rather than a null: the direct (T/L)^k rule reads AN ARTIFACT IS ABSENT —
absence of evidence for four failure modes, and 28% is exactly the strength of that. P
reads THE FILE IS BETTER THAN PREDICTED from its own bits and content, which is POSITIVE
evidence about the encode. DO NOT IMPORT THE 28% BOUND INTO P BY ANALOGY.

blockMean IS NOT LUMA-DEGENERATE — AND A CORRELATION IS NOT AN EFFECT SIZE  [SOLID]
Blocking is the ENCODER-axis detector, the second most reliable of the four, and carries a
quarter of P. If its reading were partly a function of how BRIGHT a scene is, that confound
would propagate into every adjustment. THE TEST: take a real clip, shift its LUMA without
touching its structure, encode each shifted version identically. Edges, texture, motion and
bitrate constant by construction.
    A DESIGN FLAW CAUGHT ON THE FIRST OUTPUT ROW. Fixed offsets of +-0.25 in eq units are
    about +-64 luma levels, but these films sit at base luma 38-86. Mr. Robot came out at
    LUMA 2.6 — CRUSHED TO BLACK, its grain reading collapsing from ~1.0 to 0.14. That is
    not a shifted image, it is a CLIPPED one, and clipping changes structure, which is
    exactly the premise the test rests on. Rewritten to scale each film to its own
    headroom, min(base, 255-base), plus a guard that drops any rung still landing near a
    limit. The guard earned its place immediately: Mr. Robot's extreme rung still lands at
    luma 9.1, so it is excluded rather than regressed.
    detector   corr(luma, log det)   slope per 50 luma   as % of residual sd
    cambi            0.591                0.0053                0.4%
    block            0.047               -0.0006                0.2%
    blur             0.666                0.0195               11.8%
    grain            0.649                0.0612               23.5%
*** THREE DETECTORS "TRACK LUMA" AT r ABOUT 0.6 AND TWO OF THEM MOVE BY UNDER HALF A
PERCENT OF THEIR OWN SPREAD. *** With five rungs and a tiny monotone trend, a correlation
can sit near 0.7 while the effect is nothing. ALWAYS CONVERT A CORRELATION INTO AN EFFECT
SIZE AGAINST A SPREAD THAT MATTERS. This is the same discipline that killed the 4.64x L*,
arriving from the opposite direction: there a tight error bar hid an implausible magnitude,
here a strong correlation hides a negligible one.
BLOCKING IS CLEAN: r = 0.047, effect 0.2% of its residual sd. The luma degeneracy question
is ANSWERED, NO.
GRAIN IS THE ONE WITH A REAL EFFECT, 23.5% of its residual sd per 50 luma levels, and films
here span roughly 85 luma. Grain carries weight -1 in P, so it was checked against the
contrast the calibration actually runs through:
    WEB minus Bluray mean luma  -5.2 +- 3.4  t = -1.54  (302 Bluray, 22 WEB-DL)
    contributes 0.006 P-sd to a gap of 0.209 P-sd  =  UNDER 3%
    corr(luma, P) -0.104   corr(luma, z_grain) 0.254   corr(luma, z_blur) 0.007
The two groups barely differ in brightness, so the grain-luma response adds a little NOISE
to P and essentially no BIAS to the gap. THE LIMIT ON THIS CHECK: only 330 of 1048 units
carry a luma reading and the WEB side is 22 units. Measuring luma library-wide is cheap
(signalstats alone, no libvmaf) and would both firm this up AND allow luma into the
residual surface, cleaning grain's residual of a 23.5% contaminant.

DOES ARTIFACT VISIBILITY TRACK BPP+? — the first independent handle on where 100 sits
    BPP+ band      n   banding>T   blocking>T
    <50          155        25%         0%
    50-74        501        22%         2%
    75-99        228        17%         8%
    100-124      108        11%         8%

BANDING FALLS CLEANLY AND MONOTONICALLY, 25% to 2%, and it keeps doing so INSIDE every

the score's construction. It is the first bullet of core-doc 9.1.
*** THE INFERENCE DRAWN FROM IT IS RETRACTED. *** The original reading was: "at 50-74,
where the library median lives, 77% of files show no visible banding or blocking at all;
if 100 meant barely enough, most files at 67 should be visibly short and they are not —
THAT IS EVIDENCE THE SCALE IS PESSIMISTIC AND THE MEDIAN BELONGS HIGHER." E.9.1 and trap

banding or blocking" is not "transparent" — a file can be visibly softer than its source

*** AND A CORRECTION TO MY OWN READING OF THE SAME TABLE, made at the time. *** Blocking
appears to RISE with BPP+ (0% to 8%), which was reported as an anomaly and guessed to be
blockdetect reading grain or texture as blocking on high-bitrate content. THE GUESS WAS
WRONG (corr(block, cxEff) -0.063, corr(block, grain) -0.132; above threshold med cx 0.129
and med grain 0.857, below threshold 0.149 and 0.957 — the blocky films are SMOOTHER and
LESS grainy, which is physically right because block edges show in flat areas and are
MASKED by grain). BUT THE REAL ANSWER IS THAT THE OBSERVATION WAS NEVER SUPPORTABLE: only
42 of 1048 units clear the blocking threshold, so the apparent trend is 41 films spread
over five bands — counts of 0, 10, 18, 9, 4. Stratifying makes it worse, on cells of 26 to
128. THERE IS NO BLOCKING TREND HERE, ONLY NOISE, AND THE COUNTS SHOULD HAVE BEEN CHECKED
BEFORE CALLING IT AN ANOMALY AND BEFORE OFFERING A MECHANISM FOR IT.

E.5.10  BANDING AS A GATE — the axis that became a term, and the two attempts that failed
Backup 10.5a-10.5e. Kept here because banding entered the system AS A GATE and the core
doc's section 6 is what it became.
CAMBI IS GENUINELY NO-REFERENCE — it keys on flat regions within a single frame, so only
libvmaf's plumbing wants two inputs and both are pointed at the same file. BANDING IS
THEREFORE MEASURABLE IN PRODUCTION, FOREVER, WITH NO MASTER. VMAF proper is NOT usable
that way (a same-file comparison scores ~100 by construction) and stays calibration-only.
*** THE TOOL IS A SECOND ffmpeg AND MUST STAY SEPARATE. *** Nothing here has libvmaf, so
scripts/setup-vmaf-tool.sh fetches a PINNED static build into tools/. NEVER use it for
complexity and never "fix" the controller by upgrading its ffmpeg: that x265 made all 1044
complexity measurements and is the only ruler for them.

FIRST NEW SIGNAL TO SURVIVE ITS OWN DEGENERACY TESTS (40 films, stratified by complexity
AND R, because all 26 grain-heavy films are also starved so complexity-only sampling would
make grain and supply inseparable). Thresholds fixed before the numbers were read; |r| >
0.6 would have failed it:
    vs complexity (grain)   -0.245 pearson / -0.392 spearman   passes
    vs mean luma (darkness) -0.254 / -0.295                    passes
    vs BPP+ (redundancy)    -0.313 / -0.322                    passes
For scale, gShare died at -0.885 against luma. AND THE DEGENERACY TESTS REPLICATE on 45
further films that were never used to set any threshold: vs complexity -0.444, vs luma
-0.022, vs BPP+ -0.273. LUMA COMES BACK AT -0.02 — the darkness degeneracy is dead
outright, not merely under the line.
AND THE MECHANISM IS THERE, not just independence: among the SMOOTH half, starved copies
band 2.33x more than supplied ones (median cambi 2.682 vs 1.151).
IT DISAGREES WITH BPP+ IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, WHICH IS THE POINT:
    The Graduate    BPP+ 75 warn, cambi 4.13 — adequate on bits, bands anyway
    Army of Shadows BPP+ 81 warn, cambi 3.36 — same
    Bridge on the River Kwai  BPP+ 67 bad, cambi 0.033 — starved, but bits are the ONLY
                    problem, so a replacement would actually fix it
Grain dithers gradients away, which is why the grainiest tercile bands LEAST (median 0.936
vs 2.087 mid). The relationship is NON-MONOTONIC and the smooth tercile banding less than
the middle (1.467 vs 2.087) IS UNEXPLAINED.
PREVALENCE, from a targeted run restricted to BPP+ >= 75 (a random sample mostly draws
films the score already condemns): OF 58 FILMS BPP+ CALLS ACCEPTABLE, 8 BAND VISIBLY = 14%
(95% CI ~5-23%); warn 75-99 6/34, ok 100-124 2/17, wow >=125 0/7. Rate falls with the
score, which is the sanity check it needed. Two of the eight are GREEN with supply ABOVE
transparency — Fargo (BPP+ 109, R 1.22) and Obsession (BPP+ 103, R 1.12). NO BITRATE
REASONING REACHES THOSE. [Prevalence figures here are film-level 4-clip readings; E.9
supersedes them at scene level.]
THE BLIND SPOT IS SMOOTH CONTENT, as the mechanism predicts: banding films have median
complexity 0.058 against 0.102 for clean ones, 6 of 8 below 0.10.
`cambi` IS null WHEN UNMEASURED, WHICH IS NOT "CLEAN". Every consumer must distinguish the
two or it will assert the whole unmeasured library is banding-free. In bpp-lab unmeasured
rows are drawn grey and are DROPPED from the banding axis rather than coerced to zero,
which would pile ~90% of the library on the left edge as "least banding of all".
IT SHARES THE PROBE'S GATES AND ITS ONE NIGHT BUDGET. Not its own — THAT IS THE SAFETY
PROPERTY: the box never runs more than PROBE_NIGHT_BUDGET_MS of encoding a night no matter
which job spends it. A second budget would silently double the nightly heat. Banding takes
the window while the probe has only REFINEMENT left; genuinely fresh complexity work still
wins, because a missing complexity number degrades every score for that title while a
missing banding number degrades nothing.

CAN BANDING BE FOLDED INTO BPP+ BY ADJUSTING THE DENOMINATOR? NO, AND THE REASON IS
SPECIFIC. Brennan proposed the obviously right shape — predict EXPECTED banding from the
film's characteristics and read measured/expected as "needs more bitrate". Tested on 85
films (docs/REPORT-banding-excess-2026-08-21.md):
  IT WORKS DIRECTIONALLY. Excess tracks supply better than raw banding does (R -0.412 ->
  -0.542, BPP+ -0.416 -> -0.528), and the residual is clean of the content it was regressed
  out of (cxEff -0.004, luma -0.055, year +0.003).
  *** BUT RAW bpp PREDICTS BANDING BETTER (-0.684) THAN BPP+ DOES (-0.416), AND THAT IS THE
  MECHANISM. *** Banding is quantisation coarseness, set by ABSOLUTE bits per pixel, not by
  bits relative to content difficulty. A grainy film needs many bits (high complexity, so
  BPP+ normalises them away) but its grain ALSO DITHERS BANDING AWAY. The per-film
  normalisation that makes BPP+ right for adequacy WORKS AGAINST IT FOR BANDING. The cxEff
  coefficient flips sign between the two models and says the same thing: -0.50 content-only
  (complex films band less), +0.27 holding bpp fixed (at equal bits, harder content bands
  more). Both true, different questions.
  AND A RATIO OF SMALL NUMBERS IS NOT A SIGNAL. At high bpp expected banding goes to ~0, so
  any reading gives a huge ratio: the illustrative adjustment gave 2001: A Space Odyssey
  (cambi 0.32, BELOW the library median 1.08) THE MAXIMUM PENALTY, 141 -> 111. That is the
  model failing, not the film. The obvious repair adds nothing: requiring high absolute
  banding AND positive excess flags 16 films; the absolute threshold alone flags the same
  16. The guard removes 0 of 16.
WHAT IS CONFIRMED IS THE GAP: among films at similar BPP+, banding still spreads 25-101x.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER, concretely: predict expected banding from the thing that
physically causes banding-proneness — THE FRACTION OF FRAME AREA THAT IS FLAT OR NEAR-FLAT
GRADIENT — instead of proxies like year and luma. If content R^2 rose from 0.15 to ~0.5 the
ratio would stabilise.

THE ARTIFACT-RESIDUAL FRAMEWORK — Brennan's idea, and the ancestor of the shipped rule.
"At its core BPP+ is saying: is your file big enough to look good. And what does looking
good mean if not that it's free of bad artifacts?" So predict how much of an artifact a
film OUGHT to show from its content AND its bitrate, measure how much it does, and let the
disagreement CORRECT THE SCORE — up when a copy is cleaner than one that size should be,
down when it is worse. BIDIRECTIONAL: on 85 films, 36 up, 46 down, 3 unchanged.
IT IS NOT THE SAME AS THE DENOMINATOR QUESTION ABOVE: this does not touch the denominator,
it treats the artifact as an INDEPENDENT ESTIMATOR of what BPP+ estimates. That INVERTS the
earlier methodology rule — there the expected-model had to be BLIND to bitrate, here SEEING
bitrate IS the mechanism.
  RELIABILITY SHRINKAGE, and this is what makes the framework safe to EXTEND. The residual
  is signal plus noise; Kelley's correction shrinks it by its split-half reliability,
  measured 0.579 at 4 clips per film. A junk detector then contributes ~nothing instead of
  contributing garbage, WITH NO SUBJECTIVE LABEL NEEDED. ACTED ON: BANDING_SAMPLES 4 -> 8
  (reliability 0.73; 12 clips would give 0.80).
  *** THE PER-FILM LADDER CHANGED THE ARITHMETIC *** (5 films, 5 starvation levels):
    Skyfall -1.534 R2 0.962 | Confessions -0.910 R2 0.987 | Goldfinger -0.618 R2 0.996 |
    E.T. -0.554 R2 0.990 | El Mariachi -0.435 R2 0.691
  1. EVERY per-film slope is FLATTER than the cross-film -1.78 (mean ~-0.81). The
     cross-film fit OVERSTATES SENSITIVITY ~2.2x — the same between-vs-within confound the
     pinning work hit (E.3.5). Only a ladder holds content constant.
  2. THE NAIVE 1/|S| SCALING IS BACKWARDS. It gives a FLAT responder a BIGGER shift, but if
     banding barely moves with bitrate then banding is UNINFORMATIVE about bitrate.
     Inverse-variance weighting gives shift proportional to |S|*r, NOT r/|S|. That is why
     E.T., the flattest responder, took the maximum penalty in the naive version.
IT GENERALISES, and grain fits in the direction people forget: LESS grain than a 1974 film
should have is a denoised or generation-lossed source — A QUALITY LOSS THAT CURRENTLY RAISES
THE SCORE, because a denoised copy measures as easy content.
THE CAUTION: several corrections must not double-count. Reliability screens each detector
alone; INDEPENDENCE needs checking pairwise. Blocking, blur and banding all rise together
under starvation, so the right treatment is probably ONE COMBINED RESIDUAL, not three
additive shifts. [That is what P became.]

*** THE CROSS-FILM FORM IS FALSIFIED (2026-08-25, n=247). *** Applied as a BPP+ shift it is
not merely weak, it is BACKWARDS:
    k = 0.15   78 of 247 films move >= 10 points   Sunset Boulevard 61 -> 138
    k = 0.30  135 of 247 films move >= 10 points   Sunset Boulevard 61 -> 313
Top movers at both settings: Sunset Boulevard, Return of the Jedi, The Big Sleep, Full
Metal Jacket, Taxi Driver — GRAINY FILMS, MOST OF THEM GENUINELY STARVED. Two causes, and
only the second is a bug:
  (1) GRAIN PREVENTS BANDING. The 15 films scoring bad with cambi < 0.25 sit at complexity
      p84-p99 — the grainiest files in the library. By decade: 1940s median cambi 0.053,
      1970s 0.168, 2000s 0.855, 2020s 2.053. CAMBI IS NOT FAILING HERE; it correctly
      reports no visible banding. What does not follow is "therefore this file has enough
      bits" — a grainy starved copy fails through a DIFFERENT artifact.
  (2) THE LOG FLOOR. log(max(cambi, 1e-4)) sends a zero-banding film to -9.2, so the films
      carrying the LEAST information dominate the fit as a pure artifact of the transform.
INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATION THAT THE MOVERS ARE WRONG: Sunset Boulevard has R 0.53 — the
supply axis already says that copy is starved, with no reference to banding at all.
WHAT SURVIVES: banding still grades sensibly as a RANKING (over-threshold rate by band: wow
0.0%, ok 7.1%, warn 17.2%, bad 20.7% — monotone), and the blind spot is real (Dune 2021 at
BPP+ 110 with cambi 4.99, E.T. at 123 with 3.89, Once Upon a Time in America at 107 with
3.87, all R > 1). WHAT IS DEAD IS THE CROSS-FILM RESIDUAL AS A SHIFT.

LEVEL AND SLOPE ARE DIFFERENT FACTS   [DIRECTION, n=6]
From the per-film ladders, spearman(banding level, |slope|) = -0.886. How much banding a

    film                    cambi@1.0    S      reading
    The Big Sleep (1946)      0.086   -2.95   responsive but far below visibility
    The Man Standing Next     6.042   -1.30   visible AND responsive -> bits help

AND IT ANSWERS "HOW COULD WE EVER TELL BAKED-IN? WE DONT HAVE THE SOURCE." (Brennan,

E.5.11  DETECTOR GRADING WITHOUT LABELS — the unlock  [MEASURED, 2026-08-25]
A detector can be graded on REPEATED READINGS ALONE: split its per-clip readings in half,
average each half, correlate the halves across films, Spearman-Brown back to full length.
IF IT CANNOT AGREE WITH ITSELF ON TWO SAMPLES OF THE SAME FILM IT CANNOT BE MEASURING A
PROPERTY OF THE FILM. probe-film.sh computed the per-clip readings all along and averaged
them away; since 2026-08-21 it emits them (scripts/grade-detector.js):

    detector      n    clips  split-half  Spearman-Brown   vs cx    vs BPP+
    blockMean     71     9.0     0.856        0.922        -0.000    +0.080
    blurMean      71     9.0     0.367        0.537        -0.233    -0.009
    cambi        247     8.0     0.643        0.782        -0.208    -0.347
    complexity   256    17.7     0.915        0.955           --     -0.365

blockMean IS THE MOST RELIABLE ARTIFACT DETECTOR WE HAVE — more reliable than CAMBI, which
shipped. It also passes every degeneracy test available: complexity -0.000, BPP+ +0.080,
log bpp +0.063, resolution +0.167. For scale, gShare died at -0.885 against mean luma.
THIS DOES NOT CONTRADICT THE 2026-08-17 KILL: what was killed was blockMean as a SCORE TERM
(E.1.3), and grading a DETECTOR asks a different question — is the reading a stable property

THE LUMA TEST COULD NOT BE RUN HERE — only 2 of the 71 units carried a luma reading, because

AND RELIABILITY IS NECESSARY, NOT SUFFICIENT. A misspecified detector repeats itself too.

E.6  LAMBDA IS 1/|BITS SLOPE| — THE CONSTANTS ARE NOT FREE
================================================================================
2026-08-27. Caught with the wrong substitution already written down.

THE SHIPPED RULE          adj = BPP+ * exp(-strength * provShare * lambda * P / 2)
                          lambda = ln(1 + anchor) / dPdGen

The two constants in lambda are NOT independent. The anchor was never measured
directly; it came from the crossed ladder as
                          anchor = exp(B/A) - 1
with B = dP/dgen and A = |dP/dlog bpp|. Therefore ln(1+anchor) = B/A and

    *** lambda = (B/A) / dPdGen = 1/A   whenever the same generation step is used
        in both places. THE GENERATION STEP ENTERS, CANCELS, AND LEAVES. ***

NUMERICAL CHECK. A = 0.6555 (crossed ladder) -> 1/A = 1.526 against the shipped
lambda of 1.519. It is an identity, not a coincidence.

WHAT THIS PREVENTED. The end-to-end test (E.7) measured a real first generation at
2.48x the ladder's step, and the obvious move was to substitute that into dPdGen.
That would have paired a ladder-measured numerator with a real-first-generation
denominator — the exact cross-population transfer the crossed ladder was built to
eliminate — and scaled every shipped adjustment by 0.40x on arithmetic rather than
on a finding.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE RESEARCH PROGRAMME. The only quantity lambda depends on is how
fast P responds to BITS. A better generation measurement belongs in the ANCHOR.
This is trap 16 in the core doc.

The anchor sets lambda, lambda scales every shipped adjustment, and the project has

    published BD-rate, external              5 - 40%

E.7.1  THE END-TO-END TEST (scripts/end-to-end-generation.js, n=23)
First test of the RULE rather than the detectors. 24 library files, each re-encoded
exactly one generation down at matched bits, both copies pushed through the shipped
surface, standardisation, direction, shrinkage and lambda.

    dP per generation      0.4971 +- 0.0459     sign 23/23
    dPdGen from ladders    0.2005               ratio 2.48x
    denoise arm            0.8107               sign 23/23

The chain TRANSMITS: the signal survives all four steps between the detectors and
the score, which had never been tested. The denoise arm also manufactures the
preprocessing-dominated field contrast the library does not naturally contain.

WHY THE LADDER UNDERSTATES: it measures the WRONG STEP. The ladder's "generation 1"
is already a re-encode, so its fitted slope averages steps 1->2 and 2->3. With the
measured 0.62 per-step saturation, that predicts a 2.0x ratio; observed 2.48x.

NOTE THE LIMIT, and do not quote past it: both copies use the SAME CLIPS, so
scene-sampling noise cancels exactly. This isolates the CALIBRATION. It says nothing
about the per-film ordering (E.9). Two different failures.

E.7.2  BOTH LEGS ON IDENTICAL CLIPS (scripts/anchor-e2e.js, n=24)
Leg A measured on the same clips as leg B, with leg A's 1.0x rung BEING the gen1
encode, so both legs are pinned to a common encode and per-film offsets cancel.

    leg A  dP/dlog bpp < 0    24/24        median |A| 0.4313   sd 0.1698
    leg B  dP/dgen     > 0    24/24        median  B  0.4886   sd 0.2226
    ratio of medians 1.1330   median of ratios 1.0518  (consistent, no 1/A blow-up)
    lambda = 1/|A| = 2.319    anchor = exp(B/A)-1 = 210.5%

B replicates the end-to-end value (0.4886 vs 0.4971). But A on these clips is 0.4313
against the crossed ladder's 0.6555, so the anchor goes UP, not down. 210% is 5.3x
outside the published bound. REFUSED — an external bound is checked before a
standard error (trap 8), and 24/24 on both direction checks does not make 210% true.

E.7.3  THE DIAGNOSIS, AND THE TEST THAT DECIDES IT
gen0 is a segment of a file encoded AS PART OF A WHOLE FILM with 2-pass rate control
and full lookahead — and 721 of 803 library files are rc=2pass (E.8.4), so this is
the normal case. gen1 is a TWO-SECOND CLIP encoded 1-pass ABR, forced to hit the
FILM'S AVERAGE bitrate on a segment whose own complexity may sit well above or below
that average. So gen0->gen1 is

    one generation  +  2-pass -> 1-pass  +  film-average bits on a specific segment

and only the first is a generation. Leg A compares clip encodes to clip encodes, so
every one of those nuisances cancels THERE. The contamination lands entirely in B,
and B/A is the anchor — exactly the asymmetry needed to inflate it.

RUNNING (scripts/generation-step2.js): the gen1 -> gen2 step, where both sides are
clip encodes from the identical recipe at the identical bitrate, so the nuisances
cancel and what remains is a pure generation. PRE-REGISTERED:
    B2 near 0.30 (= 0.62 x 0.4886)  the step is clean, 210% stands as real

E.7.5  THE RIGHT EXTERNAL BOUND IS CRF POINTS, NOT BD-RATE
The published 5-40% figure has been used as the external check all along and it is the
WRONG COMPARISON. BD-rate measures one encoder against another AT EQUAL QUALITY. Our
"generation" is a transcode of already-compressed content AT MATCHED BITRATE. Those are
different quantities and there was never a reason to expect them to agree.

We have a better external unit of our own: -17.4%/CRF measured on the starvation set
(-13.6%/CRF library-wide, E.2). Expressing every candidate anchor in CRF points:

    shipped                 35.6%   = 1.59 CRF
    published BD-rate top   40.0%   = 1.76 CRF
    corrected, A = 0.6555   46.3%   = 1.99 CRF     <- the one to quote
    corrected, A = 0.4313   78.3%   = 3.03 CRF
    naive same-clip        210.5%   = 5.93 CRF

A first generation costing 2-3 CRF is entirely ordinary. 5.9 CRF is not, which is what
the external-bound check was for. The contamination correction moves the answer from
implausible to unremarkable.

E.7.6  WHICH A? — AND THE ANSWER IS THAT THE SHIPPED RULE DOES NOT CHANGE
Because lambda = 1/|A| and the anchor never enters it (E.6), THE ANCHOR IS A REPORTED
QUANTITY, NOT AN INPUT. The rule changes only if A changes:
    crossed ladder,  8 films, ladders from the ORIGINAL files   A 0.6555 -> lambda 1.526
    same clips,     24 films, rungs from gen1 CLIP RE-ENCODES   A 0.4313 -> lambda 2.319
Shipped lambda is 1.519, so the first means NO CHANGE and the second a 1.53x increase.

TESTED, not assumed (scripts/bits-slope-flattening.mjs, n=24). If the same-clip A is
measured at an already-degraded operating point, then within those 24 films the more
damaged ones should show flatter slopes:
    |A| vs P at gen0 (ORIGINAL file, shares no encode with leg A)  rho -0.479 +- 0.218
    |A| vs P at gen1 (shares leg A's 1.0x rung — circular)         rho -0.282
    |A| vs log complexity (masking, a DIFFERENT mechanism)         rho +0.022
CONFIRMED at 2.2 SE on the clean version. Note the circular version is WEAKER, not
stronger, so the shared-encode artifact is not what produces it. And the flat

CONCLUSION. The same-clip A = 0.4313 is measured on files already one generation down,
where 11.72's saturation flattens the response, so it UNDERSTATES the field slope. The
crossed-ladder A = 0.6555 is the better estimate for library files.
    *** lambda STAYS AT ~1.52. NO CHANGE TO THE SHIPPED RULE. ***
That is the outcome of a day and a night of anchor work, and it is worth stating
plainly: the shipped constant is vindicated BY MEASUREMENT, not retained by inertia.
What did change is the honest anchor, 35.6% -> ~44%, which is a REPORTING correction
and moves nothing downstream.

CAVEAT ON n. The flattening test is 24 films at 2.2 SE — suggestive, not decisive. If
it is wrong, lambda would rise 1.53x. The cheap way to settle it: measure leg A rungs
on gen2 as well; the flattening hypothesis predicts |A| flatter again at gen2 than at
gen1, on the same films.

E.8  DETAIL LOSS — BLUR AND GRAIN ARE THE WRONG COORDINATES
================================================================================
2026-08-28. The hole in the covering set, and the one that could invalidate the
"clean artifacts => good enough" premise: detail loss is SMOOTH degradation with no
artifact signature. Nothing appears; things quietly stop being there. All four
detectors are "something appeared" detectors.

E.8.1  THE NULL WAS AGAINST BPP+, NOT AGAINST BITS
(scripts/detail-coordinate-check.mjs, n=1048, proper SEs from (XtX)^-1)

Fit log(detector) on log bpp and log cxEff freely, then force onto each single axis:

  det     b_bpp     b_cx    b_bpp-b_cx        R2 free  R2 log(b*c)  R2 log(b/c)
  blur   -0.0367  -0.0383   0.0017 +-0.0154    0.0371    0.0371       0.0005
  grain  +0.0754  +0.0738   0.0016 +-0.0304    0.0375    0.0375       0.0007
  cambi  -1.5194  -0.3609  -1.1585 +-0.1251    0.3306    0.2757       0.0897
  block  +0.0680  -0.0788   0.1468 +-0.0347    0.0168    0.0000       0.0167

BLUR AND GRAIN ARE PURE log(bits x content) FUNCTIONS. b_bpp equals b_cx at 0.1 SE,
forcing onto log(bpp*cxEff) loses NOTHING (0.0371 -> 0.0371), and forcing onto
log(bpp/cxEff) loses EVERYTHING (-> 0.0005). BPP+ is log(bits / content). The two
axes are ORTHOGONAL BY CONSTRUCTION, so the projection is zero as a matter of
coordinates and no quantity of data will ever change it.

This REPLACES the old reading ("blur is dead cross-sectionally, -0.011 +- 0.016").
It is a coordinate mismatch, not a dead detector. Amend anything that says otherwise.

SCOPE, and it matters: this kills blur and grain for CALIBRATION questions — where
100 belongs, adequacy — but NOT for P. P is a residual after bits and content are
removed, so the sum/difference structure is already gone and their residuals can
still carry provenance.

BLOCK IS ANOMALOUS AND UNEXPLAINED. It is a near-pure ADEQUACY function (b_bpp+b_cx
= -0.0109 +- 0.0269, 0.4 SE from zero; forcing onto log(bpp/cx) loses nothing,
0.0167 vs 0.0168) but WITH THE WRONG SIGN: more bits reads as MORE blocking. Yet its
scene-fraction correlation with bpp is correctly signed at -0.232 (E.9). Level and
prevalence disagree. Unresolved; do not build on blockdetect levels until it is.

E.8.2  THE TWO-DIRECTION TEST — the general bar, and the useful part of all this
For any candidate D:
    CONTENT direction     dD/d log(complexity) ACROSS films at their operating points
    STARVATION direction  dD/d log(complexity) WITHIN one film under starvation
D can separate "shot soft" from "detail destroyed" IF AND ONLY IF those differ. If
they coincide, the confound is PARALLEL to the signal and no residualisation,
normalisation or extra sampling recovers the distinction. This is the degeneracy bar
that killed gridRatio, sharpened: not "does D correlate with grain", but "does D move
DIFFERENTLY under content and under damage".

    blurdetect   content -0.0565 +- 0.0324   causal -0.0371 +- 0.0009   0.6 SE apart
    -> FAILS. Structurally incapable, independently of E.8.1 and agreeing with it.

E.8.3  THE ONE CANDIDATE THAT PASSES (scripts/perpoint-two-direction.mjs)
perPoint = d log(probeBitrate)/d CRF, already sitting in data/starve-experiment.json,
collected for biasFactor and never examined for this. Mechanism: on a rich source,
raising QP harvests a large high-frequency tail so rate collapses fast; on a starved
source that tail is already gone so raising QP saves less. It is a BITRATE-domain
measure of how much destructible detail remains — no detector, no threshold, no log
of a near-zero quantity, so the failure mode that has bitten this project three times
cannot occur.

                              matched codec        codec-crossed (x264 starve)
  monotone under starvation     8/8                  7/8
  |pp@1| - |pp@0.25|            0.0412 +- 0.0046     0.0337 +- 0.0062
                                t = 8.90             t = 5.42
  CONTENT direction            -0.0254 +- 0.0101    -0.0254 +- 0.0101   (n=8)
  STARVATION direction         -0.0897 +- 0.0028    -0.0970 +- 0.0039   (n=24)
  difference                    0.0643 +- 0.0105     0.0716 +- 0.0108
                                6.1 SE, 3.54x        6.6 SE, 3.82x

CIRCULARITY CHECK, and it is not optional. cx20 and perPoint are computed from THE
SAME four probe points. For rungs at CRF 16/20/24/28 the OLS slope weights go as
(x - xbar) = (-6,-2,+2,+6), so an error e in the CRF-20 point shifts perPoint by
-0.025e while raising log cx20 — inducing a NEGATIVE apparent slope, exactly the sign
measured. Re-run against log(LEVEL), which is experimentally controlled and carries
no measurement error at all:
    d perPoint / d log(level)   -0.0297 +- 0.0021  t = 14.1   (matched)
                                -0.0243 +- 0.0026  t =  9.2   (codec-crossed)
Sign matches. The starvation response is real and not manufactured by shared noise.

WHY A RESPONSE STATISTIC AND NOT A LEVEL — the structural insight. Every level
statistic is content-dominated by construction: content sets the level over decades
while damage moves it a few percent. The discriminating information is in how a file
RESPONDS to a perturbation, not in what it READS. A response is also a within-clip-set
differential, so the scene-sampling nuisance that is 52% of the split-half (E.9)
largely cancels. That is the mechanical reason levels fail cross-sectionally and
slopes do not.

HONEST LIMITS. The CONTENT line rests on n = 8 films (its slope is 2.5 SE from zero).
The specificity leg has NEVER been run: soft content at fixed bits, via gblur (a
LINEAR low-pass, the correct model of optical softness) rather than hqdn3d (which is
amplitude-thresholded, i.e. deadzone-shaped, and would confound with starvation by
construction). And the ladder-inheritance objection applies — a ladder built from our
copy tells you what a re-encode would lose, not what the original encode already lost.
The argument that this makes perPoint CONSERVATIVE is reasoning, not measurement.

E.8.5  *** LEG C — THE SPECIFICITY TEST. perPoint PASSES. *** (2026-08-28, n=10)
The test that could have killed the whole idea, and the only one that addresses the case
that actually matters: A FILM THAT IS GENUINELY SOFT, AT FULL BITRATE, WITH NOTHING
DESTROYED. Every earlier result contrasted starvation against films that merely DIFFER in
content; none tested softness directly. If perPoint could not separate the two it would be
a SOFTNESS detector, and it would condemn every Super-8 pastiche and soft-focus romance —
the failure mode 9.6 already flags as unhandled.

DESIGN. 10 films spread across complexity, 3 clips each, three arms ON THE SAME CLIPS:
gblur sigma 0 / 0.6 / 1.2, each encoded at CRF 16/20/24/28 and perPoint fitted as
d log(bitrate)/d CRF. Within-film, so content, bitrate, codec and scene are all exactly
constant and only the applied blur differs.

gblur AND NOT hqdn3d, which is the whole point. hqdn3d is amplitude-thresholded — it
removes small-amplitude detail and keeps large — which is EXACTLY the shape of a quantiser
deadzone, so it would have confounded softness with starvation BY CONSTRUCTION. E.8's own
axis table already measures denoise and bits as inseparable in the (blur, grain) plane. A
Gaussian blur is a linear low-pass with no amplitude threshold: the correct model of a soft
lens or a weak transfer.

E.8.7  CONTENT-INVARIANCE MAKES AN ABSOLUTE REFERENCE CONCEIVABLE — which is the one
       thing P can never have. Design note, 2026-08-28.

BRENNAN'S OBJECTION, and it is the sharpest criticism the project has received:
    "basing P on the library itself is not ideal. What if our library IS under or over
     scored by BPP+ currently compared to the artifacts?"
He is right, and it is structural. P is standardised to mean zero across the library, so
it is ZERO-SUM: it re-ranks films against each other and CANNOT say the library as a whole
is mis-scored, however wrong the level is.

*** THIS NOTE WAS WRITTEN ON THE n=30 "FLAT CONTENT LINE" READING, WHICH IS RETRACTED
(E.8.6). At n=60 the content slope is -0.0151 +- 0.0057, NOT zero. *** The argument below is
therefore WEAKER than first written, and is kept because the opportunity and the trap are
both still real — but the premise now needs a step it did not before.

WHY perPoint IS STILL DIFFERENT IN KIND. Not because it is content-invariant — it is not.
Because its content dependence is a MEASURED, SHALLOW slope (-0.0151) that is 5.9x
transverse to the damage direction. So the undamaged value for a film of known complexity
is, in principle, PREDICTABLE from that line rather than defined by where its neighbours
sit. A film could then be compared against an absolute reference instead of against the
library, which is exactly the property P structurally lacks. That remains the first route
in the project to answering the LEVEL question rather than the RANKING question — but it
now requires the content line to be correct in its INTERCEPT, not merely its slope, and an
intercept fitted on library films is library-centred by construction.

*** BUT THE REFERENCE IS NOT IN HAND, AND THE OBVIOUS SUBSTITUTE REINTRODUCES THE FLAW. ***
Defining the damage reading as perPoint minus the LIBRARY MEAN (-0.1376) is library-centring
by another name — it would inherit precisely the defect Brennan identified, while looking
like it had escaped it. Do not do that and call it absolute.

WHAT AN HONEST ABSOLUTE REFERENCE WOULD NEED, in rough order of strength:
  1. A KNOWN-PRISTINE SOURCE encoded through our own pipeline. We hold none (constraint 1.1a),
     which is the whole reason this project exists. Closest available: a camera-original or
     a synthetic render, neither of which is library content.
  2. AN UPPER ENVELOPE. If detail loss only ever FLATTENS perPoint, the steepest values
     observed bound the undamaged value from below. The 60-film run gives -0.2131 (Raging
     Bull) and -0.2462 (The Matrix, from the specificity run). An envelope is a weaker claim
     than a point estimate but it is genuinely absolute, and it is monotone in the right
     direction. THE ASSUMPTION IT RESTS ON — that nothing makes perPoint steeper than
     pristine — is UNTESTED and would need its own check; film grain in particular might.
  3. A THEORETICAL VALUE from rate-distortion theory for the deadzone quantiser. Would owe
     nothing to this library at all, and is the only route that is fully external.

STATUS: none of these is done. Recording the OPPORTUNITY and the TRAP together, because the
trap is attractive and would silently undo the one advantage the new instrument has.

The prediction was that GRAIN would dominate, since a large high-frequency tail is exactly
what raising QP harvests. It does not. Instead the residual tracks BANDING — a damage marker
— and RESOLUTION, with 720p files reading detail-poor against 1080p, which is physically
correct. Critically it does NOT track grain or complexity, which are the main CONTENT axes
cambi itself responds to, so the shared variance is more likely damage than content. The
cambi correlation is convergent validity between two INDEPENDENT instruments — one
bitrate-domain, one picture-domain — agreeing about which films are damaged.

THE STANDING POSITION, and it relocates the problem precisely. Three facts hold together:
    real          reliability 0.945
    non-redundant 67% is information BPP+ lacks (adj R2 0.333 on log bpp + log cxEff)
    damage-like   banding +0.415, height -0.303, no content axis fires

E.8.10  perPoint IS A STARVATION DETECTOR, NOT A GENERATION DETECTOR — and that reframes
        what its residual can possibly mean. INTERIM, n=11 of 24, 2026-08-28.

*** THAT IS NOT A DEFECT. IT IS PHYSICS, AND IT IDENTIFIES THE INSTRUMENT. *** perPoint
measures how much DESTRUCTIBLE DETAIL a file still holds. Re-encoding at the SAME bitrate
mostly ADDS artifacts — banding, blocking — while leaving the high-frequency tail largely

and perPoint is built to see the second one. It should have been predicted.

CONSEQUENCE FOR PRICING, and it explains a stubborn puzzle. Both candidate exchange rates
now give impossible answers for the library residual (sd 0.0280):
    via the starvation slope   p10-p90 ~ 7x   in source starvation
    via this generation step   p10-p90 ~ 10 generations (and this is a LOWER bound, since
                               E.7.4 measured ~49% of the step as rate-control contamination,
                               making the true dPP smaller and the count larger)
No library spans either. When two independent units both return the impossible, the fault
is not in either rate — IT IS IN THE ASSUMPTION THAT THE RESIDUAL IS A COMPRESSION DEPTH AT

THE LEADING ALTERNATIVE, and it is already visible in E.8.8: the residual correlates -0.303
with HEIGHT. A 720p file genuinely holds less detail than a 1080p one, and that is neither
starvation nor generations — it is SOURCE FORMAT. A resolution axis would price sensibly in
neither compression unit, which is exactly the symptom observed. It is also a legitimate
quality fact: a 720p copy of a 1080p film IS compromised, just not by compression.
TEST, and it is cheap: residualise perPoint on log(height) as well as log(cx20), then see
whether the REMAINING spread prices sensibly in either unit. If it does, the residual splits
into a resolution part and a compression part, and only the second should ever be converted.

DO NOT WEIGHT perPoint INTO ANYTHING UNTIL THIS IS SETTLED. The instrument is validated
(reliability 0.945, specificity 19% against a 70% kill line, 11.8 SE two-direction
separation); what it MEANS across films is not.

E.9.7  *** P IS AN INDEX, NOT A MEASUREMENT *** — and the structure is 3+1, not 4
       (2026-08-28, scripts/P-one-thing.mjs, n=1048 with W4 from 19 60-clip films)

THE QUESTION. Everything the project says ABOUT P — that it has a reliability, that
re-weighting it is meaningful, that it is "the provenance FACTOR" — quietly assumes the
four detectors are noisy readings OF A COMMON QUANTITY. Task 95 asked whether they are.

THE STATISTIC. Eigenvalue spectrum of the 4x4 correlation matrix of the z residuals in
the sign-aligned basis (grain flipped, so a common factor shows as a positive manifold).
For 4 variables the independence spectrum is (1,1,1,1), so lambda1 reads directly as
"how much more than nothing".

PRE-REGISTERED, before the number existed:
    ONE THING   lambda1 >= 2.0  AND all loadings same sign  AND cos(PC1,pre-reg) >= 0.8
    INDEX       lambda1 < 1.5
    PARTIAL     between

Also pre-registered: THREE EXISTING RESULTS PREDICTED "INDEX" — P's reliability being
only 0.674 at 4 clips, re-weighting being unidentified (cos 0.549), and block firing on
a median 0% of scenes. Stating them first is what stops the answer being rationalised
either way.

*** THE TRAP THIS HAD TO CLEAR. *** All four detectors read THE SAME FOUR CLIPS, so a
dark quiet scene raising cambi and grain together lands in the between-film covariance
looking exactly like a shared provenance factor:
        C_obs  =  C_true  +  W4
W4 is the sampling covariance of the 4-clip statistic. It was SIMULATED, not derived:
the delta-method shortcut Var(log mean) ~ Var(log)/k is unusable because the minimum
clip-level cambi in the sample is 1.7e-9, whose log is -20, so clip-level log variance is
dominated by near-zero readings the MEAN never produces. Each film's own 60 clips were
resampled into 4-clip draws and the covariance of log(mean of 4) taken directly — the
exact statistic the exporter computes, so no approximation enters. Scale comes from
resSd, now emitted by export-provenance.js (the fitted part is not stored, so resSd
cannot be recovered from the z's alone).

THE RESULT

  scene sampling removed 1.399  1.080  0.814  0.708     (35.0 27.0 20.3 17.7 %)
  PC1 loadings           0.588  0.536  0.199  0.573     (cambi block blur grain)
  cos(PC1, pre-registered)  0.947        all loadings same sign
  independence null      0/2000 shuffles reached lambda1 = 1.399

VERDICT: INDEX. lambda1 = 1.399 against a pre-registered INDEX line of 1.5. The factor is
REAL (0/2000 under independence) but WEAK: PC1 takes 35% where nothing takes 25%.

ROBUST ACROSS THE WHOLE ADMISSIBLE RANGE OF W4, which matters because W4 comes from 19
band-restricted films whose per-film spread is 4-14x:
    W4 x0.0  lambda1 1.447   INDEX        W4 x1.5  lambda1 1.372   INDEX
    W4 x0.5  lambda1 1.425   INDEX        W4 x2.0  impossible — blur's between-film

The verdict does not depend on W4 at all: it holds even at zero correction. So the

*** THE STRUCTURE IS 3+1. *** The nuisance-corrected correlation matrix:
                cambi   block    blur   grain
        cambi   1.000   0.152   0.169   0.208
        block   0.152   1.000   0.008   0.214
        blur    0.169   0.008   1.000  -0.043
        grain   0.208   0.214  -0.043   1.000
cambi/block/grain are EQUICORRELATED at r = 0.191 (spread only 0.062). Blur shares
essentially nothing with any of them (mean r 0.045) while carrying a quarter of P's
weight. A "3-equicorrelated-plus-independent-blur" model predicts eigenvalues
(1.383, 1.000, 0.809, 0.809) against the observed (1.399, 1.080, 0.814, 0.708) — so the
whole covariance structure of P is two numbers: r = 0.19 among three detectors, and blur
standing apart. PC1 communality: cambi 48%, block 40%, grain 46%, BLUR 5.5%.

WHY THIS DOES NOT CONTRADICT E.9.4, which found 52% of P's reliability to be scene
sampling. The nuisance is itself strongly COHERENT (nuisance corr cambi-block 0.577) and
supplies 26-38% of the observed cambi/block/grain covariances — 89% of block-blur. But it
also inflates the DIAGONALS (25% of cambi's between-film variance, 55% of blur's), and
removing both at once leaves the CORRELATIONS almost untouched. Scene sampling badly
damages how precisely P is measured; it barely touches P's factor STRUCTURE. Those are
different questions with different answers and the two results are consistent.

TWO OF THE THREE PREDICTIONS CONFIRMED, ONE REFUTED — and the refutation is the useful part.
  CONFIRMED  reliability 0.674 is EXPECTED, not disappointing. Four noisy readings of one
             quantity average well; a weak-factor composite does not.
  CONFIRMED  re-weighting is unidentifiable BY CONSTRUCTION, not for want of data. A
             near-spherical covariance has no preferred direction to find. cos 0.549
             between half-sample optima is what a sphere gives you.
  REFUTED    "a detector that is silent cannot be reading a shared factor" is WRONG.
             Block fires on a median 0% of scenes yet loads 0.536 with 40% communality —
             squarely inside the triple. The error was conflating two things: P uses the
             continuous residual LEVEL, not the threshold-crossing FRACTION. Block carries
             provenance information at levels that never cross 3.710. Do not use firing
             rate to argue about a detector's contribution to P.

WHAT MUST CHANGE IN THE DOCS. "The provenance FACTOR" claims a latent quantity is being
estimated. It is not. P is a composite damage INDEX in a pre-registered direction — which
is legitimate, still carries both field contrasts (E.4.3b), and is still the right thing
to shrink by provShare. But it is an index, and the language should say so.

BLUR IS INDICTED THREE SEPARATE WAYS, AND THE OBVIOUS FIX DOES NOT WORK. Blur is
orthogonal to the rest of P (mean r 0.045), 55.5% of its between-film variance is scene
sampling (both measured here), and E.11 reported its backfill reliability as 0.404 against
the probe's 0.664. The natural conclusion was to source blur from the probe. THAT WAS
TESTED THE SAME DAY AND REJECTED — it fails the arbiter (355/20000 vs a bar of 100) and
E.11's reliability premise violates its own attenuation bound. See E.11.1. The first two
indictments stand; the third is not established, and the fix does not follow from them.

E.9.8  *** THE SCENE FRACTION IS A CUT-OFF, AND IT COSTS 1.6x TO 20x OF THE MEASUREMENT ***
       — but you cannot get it back by modelling the fraction
       (2026-08-28, scripts/threshold-vs-smooth.mjs + scripts/implied-fraction.mjs, 21 films)

THE DESIGN RULE SAYS NO CUT-OFFS — a max is a cut-off and so is a hinge. The blend rule
obeys it. THE THRESHOLD TRACK DOES NOT: "fraction of scenes past T" applies a hard cut-off
per scene and then counts, so a scene at 2.80 and a scene at 28.0 are treated identically
on their own side of the line. This measures what that costs, on the same films and the
same clips.

T IS NOT DELETED — IT IS DEMOTED FROM A GATE TO A UNIT. 2.817 is the project's only
external calibration and the reason the threshold track can answer "where does 100 sit"
when P cannot. So each clip is measured in multiples of T as x = log(L/T), sign-flipped for
grain, and summarised smoothly. T is exactly where x = 0.

(cambi 0.763 vs 0.695 and 0.441; grain 0.878 vs 0.891 and 0.522). So the loss comes from
BINARISING EACH CLIP, not from which summary is taken afterwards. Any continuous summary
recovers most of it.

*** AND NOW THE PART THAT DOES NOT WORK, WHICH MATTERS MORE THAN THE PART THAT DOES. ***
Brennan's criterion — "5% or less of visible artifacts is good enough" — is intrinsically a
COUNT, so the obvious repair is to keep the criterion and estimate the count smoothly:
model each film's per-clip log-excess and read the fraction off the fit,
impliedFrac = Phi(mean(x)/sd(x)). No scene is thresholded and T is retained exactly.
Scored WITHIN FILM against each film's own 60-clip counted fraction:

  detector   counted |err| from 4 clips   implied |err|   gain    implied bias
  cambi              14.7%                    8.1%       1.82x     +0.3pp
  blur               16.8%                   16.0%       1.05x     +0.6pp
  grain              17.7%                   17.0%       1.04x     +1.2pp
  block               ~0                      ~0          n/a      +0.0pp   (fraction is 0
                                                                    for almost every film,
                                                                    so neither is tested)

  Unbiased everywhere — worst film 7-9pp, median 0.3-1.2pp — so the normality assumption,
  which was pre-registered as the risk and IS violated (per-clip skew -1.02 to +1.25),
  does not bite. But the PRECISION gain is real only for cambi and negligible for blur and
  grain, against 1.6-1.7x for those same detectors on the ranking statistic.

WHY THE TWO RESULTS DIVERGE, WHICH IS THE ACTUAL LESSON. logEx wins as a RANKING variable
because it uses every clip's magnitude. Converting it back into a fraction spends that
advantage immediately: Phi(mean/sd) needs an SD estimated from 4 clips, and a 4-clip
variance is very noisy. THE FRACTION IS THE LOSSY OBJECT, not the way it is computed. You
cannot recover a cut-off statistic's information by estimating the cut-off statistic more
cleverly.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE DESIGN. Stop reporting a scene fraction as the measured quantity.
Report the smooth log-excess as the number, which is what the design rule asked for
anyway — a weighted number, not a percentage of scenes. A fraction may still be QUOTED for
human communication, with its error bar, but it must not be the thing the score is built
from. This is a decision about what is measured, and it removes the last cut-off in the
project outside the artifact report itself.

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE: where 100 sits still needs the band gradient from the finished
  40-film run, and the smooth statistic does not supply an external anchor by itself — T
  does, and T is unchanged. This changes HOW the threshold track is summarised, not what it
  is calibrated against.

nothing — the partials are small and point the WRONG WAY. If the complexity
denominator were doing its job, films at equal BPP+ would show equal artifact rates
and frac~BPP+ would be STRONGER than frac~bpp. It is weaker, every time.

THE FORK, both branches load-bearing:
  (a) THE NORMALISATION IS NOT EARNING ITS PLACE. Complexity MASKS artifacts —
      grain hides banding and blocking. Dividing by complexity double-counts: a
      complex film is credited for NEEDING more bits but never debited for SHOWING
      less damage at the same bits. A real mechanism, not a fitting artifact.
  (b) THE PANEL IS NOT VALID ADEQUACY GROUND TRUTH. If equal adequacy genuinely does
      not mean equal visible-artifact rate, the covering-set premise is what breaks.

CAVEATS BEFORE ANYONE ACTS. n=60, and these films were selected for the reliability
study, not sampled for representativeness. The partials are mostly within 2 SE of
zero, so "adds nothing" is supported and "is actively worse" is NOT. Blur's threshold
is separately suspect (E.9.1), and E.8.1 shows blur and grain cannot carry adequacy
information at all — which weakens two of the four rows here by construction. The
cambi row is the one to trust, and it is also the strongest.

DO NOT CHANGE THE SHIPPED DENOMINATOR ON THIS. The next step is to re-run on a
representative sample once the backfill retains per-clip values.

E.10.1  THE EXPONENT SWEEP — AND WHY ITS ANSWER IS BIASED BY THE HOLE IN E.8
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-08-28, scripts/complexity-exponent.mjs. BPP+ scores on log(bpp) - a*log(cxEff)
with a = 1, fixed by assumption and never fitted; raw bitrate is the same family at
a = 0. Sweeping a against scene-fraction (n=60):

       a     cambi     block      blur     grain
   0.000    -0.569    -0.232    -0.115    -0.320   <- raw bitrate
   0.500    -0.499    -0.153    -0.063    -0.226
   1.000    -0.382    -0.067    -0.014    -0.093   <- SHIPPED BPP+
   1.500    -0.253    +0.030    +0.002    +0.013

  cambi   best a = -0.25   rho -0.603   bootstrap 95% CI [-0.50, 0.75]
  block   best a = -0.50   rho -0.287   CI [-0.50, 2.00]  uninformative
  grain   best a = -0.45   rho -0.448   CI [-0.50, 0.10]
  blur    best a = -0.25   rho -0.139   CI [-0.50, 2.00]  uninformative

Every detector prefers a BELOW the shipped 1, and for CAMBI — the only detector with
both an external threshold and real adequacy loading — the 95% interval EXCLUDES 1.
Read naively that says BPP+ over-normalises for complexity and Brennan's suspicion
("we don't need as many bits as we thought") is confirmed.

*** DO NOT READ IT NAIVELY. THERE ARE TWO REASONS IT OVERSTATES. ***

1. THE OPTIMUM IS AT THE SEARCH BOUNDARY. cambi's curve is monotone across the whole
   swept range and the fine-grid argmax lands on the edge at -0.50. A boundary hit
   means the optimum is UNIDENTIFIED — the honest statement is "a <= 0 fits these
   data best", not "a = -0.25". A negative exponent would mean complex films need
   FEWER bits, which is physically absurd, and that absurdity is the tell that
   something other than adequacy is driving the fit.

2. *** THE COVERING SET'S HOLE BIASES THIS DOWNWARD, SYSTEMATICALLY. *** Complexity
   does two opposing things: it RAISES the bits a film needs (the reason a > 0), and
   it MASKS artifacts — grain hides banding and blocking. Every artifact we can
   currently measure is a MASKED one, so every one of them pushes a downward. The
   effect that would push a UPWARD is detail retention — and E.8.1 shows we have no
   instrument for it at all, because blur and grain are orthogonal to the adequacy
   axis by construction.

   So this sweep is not a neutral estimate of a. It is an estimate of a computed
   from exactly the subset of failure modes that complexity conceals, with the one
   failure mode complexity aggravates entirely absent. It would return a < 1 even if
   a = 1 were correct.

THE STANDING CONCLUSION. The artifacts genuinely do say "most films need fewer bits
than BPP+ assumes" — but they would say that whether or not it is true, so the
statement carries almost no weight until detail loss has an instrument. This is the
strongest available argument for prioritising E.8.3 (perPoint): the detail measure
is not merely a missing feature, it is the missing COUNTERWEIGHT, and without it
every calibration this project runs is biased toward "fewer bits needed".

Note also that a and HEADROOM_TARGET are not separately identified by prevalence
data: changing a re-ranks films while changing HEADROOM_TARGET shifts the level, and
scene-fraction only constrains the ranking. Any move on a must be argued from
mechanism, not fitted from this table.

E.10.2  THE EXPONENT IS NOT IDENTIFIED BY ANY INSTRUMENT WE OWN — a failed attempt to
        route task 99 around task 93 (2026-08-28, scripts/exponent-bracket.mjs, n=60)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE HOPE. E.10.1 concluded that a counterweight is needed and that perPoint cannot serve
until its referent is named. But BRACKETING needs no exchange rate: if masked artifacts
prefer a < 1 and detail retention prefers a > 1, then a = 1 sits inside the bracket with
no pricing, no unit and no human label. Only perPoint's RANKING would be consumed, and
that is reliable at 0.945.

FIRST, A BETTER ESTIMATOR — worth keeping even though the application fails. Maximising a
correlation is the wrong procedure: any damage proxy that responds strongly to bits will
"prefer" a = 0, because that is the score most correlated with bits. The identified
question is which a makes films of EQUAL SCORE equally damaged:
        damage ~ b0 + b_bpp*log bpp + b_cx*log cx
        a* = -b_cx / b_bpp
One regression, no sweep, no search boundary, and INVARIANT TO THE SIGN of the damage
variable because both coefficients flip together.
    MEASURED   b_bpp -0.0319   b_cx -0.0024   a* = -0.075   95% CI [-0.47, 0.19]
               P(a* > 1) = 0.0%

*** AND a* MUST NOT BE BELIEVED, FOR A REASON THAT KILLS THE WHOLE APPROACH. *** The
estimator is valid only if the damage measure is CONTENT-FREE. perPoint is not: it is
destructible detail remaining IN ABSOLUTE TERMS, so at fixed bitrate a complex film has
more of it simply because it started with more. b_cx is therefore a SUM of two opposing
effects —
    content    more complex -> more absolute detail -> b_cx NEGATIVE, nothing to do with
               adequacy
    adequacy   more complex -> more starved at fixed bpp -> b_cx POSITIVE
— and measuring it at -0.0024, indistinguishable from zero, is precisely what a
CANCELLATION looks like. The estimator cannot decompose them, so a* is uninterpretable
rather than small. THE BRACKET FAILS: perPoint does not supply an independent direction.

*** THE GENERAL STATEMENT, SHARPER THAN E.10.1'S. *** It is not that we lack a
counterweight. EVERY damage measure this project owns confounds content with damage, in
the same structural way, differing only in which direction the contamination runs:
    banding, blocking   complexity MASKS them            -> pushes a down
    perPoint            complexity SUPPLIES the detail   -> cancels, sign unknown
So a is not identified by ANY of them, and no combination fixes it — combining two
contaminated measures yields a third contaminated measure. What breaks the deadlock is a
judgement that is content-aware BY NATURE, which is exactly what a person supplies for
free when asked "does this look bad FOR WHAT IT IS". E.10.1 said task 93 would help;
this says task 93 IS THE IDENTIFICATION STRATEGY, not one item among several.

TWO ERRORS ON THE WAY, recorded because both nearly reached a document.
  1. THE SIGN. The first version asserted "HIGH perPoint is GOOD" and maximised rho. It is
     the OPPOSITE — starving RAISES perPoint (-0.0297/log level, E.8.10) and a generation
     RAISES it (+0.0068), so HIGH = LESS DETAIL = WORSE. The whole sweep was read upside
     down. This is why the a* estimator is now stated in sign-invariant form.
  2. THE VERDICT BRANCH ORDER. The first version printed "BRACKETED. Task 99 is no longer
     blocked on task 93." on an argmax sitting at the search boundary (a = 2) with a
     bootstrap CI of [-0.5, 2] — the ENTIRE swept range. The uninformative check sat below
     the bracket check in the if-chain, so a result that was pure noise announced a pass.
     A VERDICT FUNCTION MUST TEST "IS THIS INFORMATIVE AT ALL" FIRST. -> trap 24

THE PRE-REGISTERED ADOPTION RULE, written into export-provenance.js before running:

    beat source tag alone   2.73%          10.64%

NOT A MEASUREMENT-SCALE CONFOUND. Probe and backfill agree on level to within 0.5-0.8%

*** THE PREMISE OF E.11 DOES NOT SURVIVE ITS OWN ATTENUATION BOUND. *** Two noisy
measures of the same quantity cannot correlate more than sqrt(r1*r2). On the RESIDUALS —
which is what P uses, and the right comparison, since most of RAW blur is content that
both sources measure reliably:
    block   residual corr 0.861   ceiling sqrt(0.836*0.910) = 0.872   CONSISTENT
    blur    residual corr 0.609   ceiling sqrt(0.404*0.664) = 0.518   IMPOSSIBLE
Block's reliability figures check out; blur's do not. Since the two sources sample
DIFFERENT scenes, their underlying targets differ slightly, which pushes the observed
correlation DOWN — so the bound is violated a fortiori. At least one of the two blur
reliabilities is understated: if the probe's 0.664 is right, the backfill's blur residual
reliability is at least 0.559, not 0.404. The claimed 0.404 -> 0.664 precision gain, on
which E.11's whole case rested, is not credible as stated.

THE ALTERNATIVE READING, AND WHY IT MOSTLY FAILS. E.9.4 established that the shared-clip
nuisance points along (+,+,+,-), the same direction as the signal. So one could argue the
BASELINE's gap is nuisance-inflated and the swap is revealing that rather than losing
signal — which would make the arbiter itself nuisance-assisted and 11.44's pass flattered.
That argument is BACKWARDS for a standardised gap. The gap is expressed in sd units of P.
Nuisance common to both groups inflates sd(P), the DENOMINATOR, and therefore DEFLATES the
standardised gap. Removing nuisance should RAISE it. It fell 29%. So the swap lost
provenance signal; it did not remove a gap-inflating artifact.
    THE ONE CONDITION UNDER WHICH THAT REASONING FAILS: if the scene-sampling nuisance is
    systematically LARGER for WEB than for Bluray films, it would shift the means rather
    than only the spread. That is a much narrower claim than "the nuisance points along the
    signal", it has not been tested, and testing it needs per-clip data on enough films of
    BOTH source tags — which the 40-film 60-clip run will not supply. Recorded as an open
    caveat on E.4.3b rather than resolved.

WHAT THIS COSTS AND WHAT IT BUYS. The structural argument for splitting the sampling
across two independent draws is still sound in principle — it is the only proposal that
attacks E.9.4's 52% rather than averaging harder at it. What is now known is that the
probe's block/blur are not a drop-in substitute: they carry measurably less of the
source-tag contrast. Any future attempt must show it preserves BOTH field contrasts, not
just improve a reliability figure.

AND IT CORRECTS E.9.7'S CLOSING SENTENCE. That section listed three independent
indictments of blur and said "all three are fixed by the same change". They are not. Blur
being orthogonal to the rest of P and heavily scene-sampled remains true; sourcing it from
the probe does not fix it, and makes P worse by the arbiter's measure.
