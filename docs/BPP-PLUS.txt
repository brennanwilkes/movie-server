WHAT IT MEASURES, IN ONE LINE, BECAUSE THE NAME MISLEADS. BPP+ is
BITRATE ADEQUACY PER CONTENT: does this file carry the bits ITS OWN content needs
for a transparent encode? It is NOT a quality rating and cannot be one — it never
looks at the picture, only at how many bits are spent against how many a CRF-20
encode of the same file would spend (section 1.2c: there is no master anywhere, so
no reference metric is available even in principle).

The distinction is not pedantry, and three separate arguments in this document
turn on it. A grainy 1080p scan SHOULD score lower at equal bits, because grain
IS screen content and genuinely costs more (section 9). A sterling Criterion
remux of an expensive black-and-white film can read "bad" with no better copy in
existence (section 8). And a file can be perfectly adequate for its content and
still be unwatchable for reasons BPP+ is blind to by design — wrong source tier,
10-bit HEVC the hardware cannot decode, a truncated mux (section 10). Every
"BPP+ is not a quality measure" complaint in the literature and the forums
(section 17) is a complaint about the name, not the number.

The title says "quality score" because that is what it is called in the app and
in Brennan's own usage, and renaming a thing mid-flight costs more than it buys.
Read it as adequacy. FLAGGED FOR BRENNAN: a rename is a real option and it is his
call, not an agent's.

0. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS, AND HOW TO KEEP IT TRUE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The single source of truth for the BPP+ algorithm AND the research log behind it.
Meant to be handed to another agent cold, and usable in two directions: to
maintain the shipped system, and to work out what research has been tried, what
it cost, and what it returned.

*** MAINTENANCE RULE ***
Whenever the algorithm changes — a constant, a gate, a term, a band edge, a new
measurement, or a falsified hypothesis — UPDATE THIS FILE IN THE SAME CHANGE.
It is a design document, not a snapshot: it should always describe the shipped
system. If you change code and not this file, this file becomes a liability,
because its whole value is that a reader can trust it without reading the code.
Re-run the numbers in sections 7-9 when you touch the model; they are all
derived from live data and go stale silently.
SECOND RULE, for the log half: a hypothesis that was tested and failed belongs in
section 11, not deleted. This document was previously grown by APPENDING
corrections, so it argued with its own earlier editions in five places. Fold
corrections INTO the current statement; keep a retraction only where a reader
would otherwise repeat the mistake.

1. THE PROBLEM, STATED PROPERLY — AND THE CONSTRAINTS THAT BOUND IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 THE DECISION BEING MADE
~3.2 TB of 1080p-and-below films on an 8 TB drive, ~1000 titles. The task is
CHOOSING BETWEEN TORRENT SOURCES:
    UPGRADE   this copy is compromised; is a release on offer meaningfully
              better, and worth the bandwidth and the disk?
    DOWNGRADE this copy spends bits nobody can see; is there a smaller release
              that gives the same picture and hands back the disk?
Both are the same question in opposite directions, and both must be answerable
from ONE NUMBER attached to a file, because the number drives a human sort in the
Audit tab and then a grab / don't-grab decision. File size answers neither.

Bits per pixel is the natural normaliser:
    bpp = videoBitrate / (width * height * fps)
It removes resolution and frame rate, so a 720p file and a 1080p file are
comparable. But raw bpp is illegible — this entire library lives between 0.02 and
0.44, so every interesting difference is in the third decimal place. BPP+ is that
ratio made legible and made per-film.

1.2 LOGICAL CONSTRAINTS
These let a reader evaluate a new paper or metric in five minutes. Almost every
published video-quality result fails at least one.

  (a) *** NO REFERENCE. *** We never hold a pristine master; the file on disk IS
      the best copy of that film in the building. Every FULL-REFERENCE metric —
      VMAF, SSIM, PSNR — needs a master, so all of them are STRUCTURALLY
      UNAVAILABLE for the question we ask. This is why ITU-T P.1204.3
      (no-reference, bitstream-based) is closer to our problem than every Netflix
      ladder paper, and why "just use VMAF" keeps looking attractive and keeps
      being wrong: VMAF against our own copy measures distance from a copy we
      already suspect.

  (b) *** THE SOURCE IS ALREADY COMPRESSED *** — unknown provenance, unknown
      generation count, possibly a re-encode of a re-encode. Netflix's per-title
      work and Durbha/Bovik both ASSUME A PRISTINE INPUT, and that assumption is
      load-bearing in their maths. It is false for us, always.

  (c) *** THE MEASUREMENT'S INPUT IS OUR COPY, NOT A MASTER. *** State this
      precisely. The probe encodes short SAMPLES to a NULL OUTPUT: nothing is
      written, nothing on disk is modified, /data is mounted READ-ONLY in the
      container. WE NEVER RE-ENCODE MEDIA. The limitation is on the INPUT side —
      a sample of a starved file can only measure what is left IN that file, so
      the measurement inherits the copy's limits. That is the origin of the
      pinning problem (section 5), and it is a property of what we can OBSERVE,
      not of anything done to the media.

  (d) *** THE ANSWER MUST BE ONE SCALAR. *** Brennan: "I like BPP+ as a single #
      where I aim to upgrade stuff to around 100... If we can fold in multi
      dimension into our one number I'm game but if not then ehh." Everything bits
      CANNOT fix is a separate GATE beside the score (section 10), never a term
      inside it — folding corrupts a number that means one clean thing.

  (e) *** WHAT PRECISION IS FOR. *** NOT band membership. The colour bands are a
      reading aid for skimming a long list; a human reads 98 and 101 as the same,
      and a change that moves 23 films across a band edge is not thereby a
      problem. Precision must support (i) RANKING candidate releases against each
      other and against the copy already held, and (ii) a rough sense of DISTANCE
      FROM ~100, since Brennan upgrades toward 100 and downgrades toward 100.
      So the quantity that must be trustworthy is THE GAP BETWEEN TWO SCORES. An
      error moving both scores together is harmless; the same error on only one
      is not. This is the correct lens for every accuracy argument here — it is
      why source pinning matters so much (it hits the starved copy and not the
      good one, i.e. exactly one side of the comparison) and why the absolute
      value of HEADROOM_TARGET matters less than it looks.

1.3 PHYSICAL CONSTRAINTS
  - 4-core NUC that ALSO serves video. Encode experiments run strictly
    sequentially (trap 6).
  - Nightly thermal window 01:00-06:00, 240-min budget. An uncapped nightly task
    has already run the box hot all day once (the trickplay incident), which is
    why widening the unattended night budget is not done casually. A MANUAL
    SESSION is the sanctioned way to run it hard by day.
  - 8 TB drive: 5.81 TB used, 2.13 TB free (2026-08-18). Disk pressure is real
    but not acute — there is no hurry to reclaim.
  - 1080p CEILING. Nothing in the house plays above 1080p, so 2160p is never an
    upgrade (10.5).
  - THE PROJECTOR IS BEING REPLACED, native 720p -> 1080p. Every quality figure
    here was gathered while a 44% downscale hid compression and grain alike. That
    mask is coming off; see 2.3, section 9, 12.3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    BPP+ = round( 100 * sqrt( bpp / target ) )

    target = complexity * biasFactor(R) * HEADROOM_TARGET
    HEADROOM_TARGET = 1.0                    (the single global calibration knob)
    complexity      = the measured bpp of a CRF-20 encode of THIS FILM
                      (the nightly probe; section 4)
    biasFactor(R)   = the SOURCE-PINNING CORRECTION, >= 1.0 (section 5). Do not
                      omit it: it is applied at resolve time to every score, and
                      for starved files is worth up to x2.0 (hard ceiling) on the
                      denominator, i.e. about -29% on BPP+.

Equivalently, with R = srcBitrate / probeBitrate ("bitrate adequacy"):
    BPP+ ~= 100 * sqrt(R)
...but read trap 11 before plotting anything against R. That approximation is off
by a median factor of 0.792 and the discrepancy looks exactly like the model
failing to fit its own data.

100 IS NOT "AVERAGE". 100 means "this file spends exactly as many bits as a
CRF-20 encode of itself would spend". A per-film target, not a library constant.

2.1 WHY THE SQUARE ROOT                                          [DIRECTION]
Measured on this library's own content (raw/bitrate-plateau-2026-08-01.md),
visible error falls as roughly bitrate^-0.5 — quadrupling the bitrate halves the
distortion. The square root inverts that, so the index is proportional to 1/error
and its ratios mean something perceptual rather than something about disk:
    200 = about HALF the visible error of 100   (and 4x the bits)
     50 = about TWICE the visible error of 100  (and 1/4 the bits)
The exponent is fitted from our content and never independently checked. IT IS
THE WEAKEST LINK IN THE MODEL. It is also MONOTONIC, so it re-labels every file
without re-ranking any — by 1.2(e), the mildest possible kind of error.

2.2 BANDS
    >= 125   purple "wow"   ~20%+ less error than 100.
    100-124  green  "ok"    the target.
     75-99   orange "warn"  diminished. Might still be fine; human call.
      < 75   red    "bad"   compromised.
Cutoffs are the old linear 150/100/60 carried through the same square root
(sqrt(1.5)=1.22, sqrt(0.6)=0.77), rounded to 125/100/75. *** THEY ARE NOT
KNEE-DERIVED. *** Reading them as "where the curve bends" reads in something that
was never measured (2.5).

2.3 THE DISPLAY IS CHANGING (2026-08-18)
The bands were written against a native 720p projector, which downscales every
1080p file ~44% and so hides a lot. The 1080p replacement shows files close to
1:1. So the "wow" band stops being disk spent on the invisible — that was always
described as what pays off after a projector upgrade, and the upgrade is here —
and the same files may well look worse, since nothing about them changes except
that the mask comes off. The 1080p ceiling itself is unaffected (10.5).

2.4 WHAT THE NUMBER MEANS IN A UNIT A HUMAN ALREADY HAS INTUITION FOR
Since x265 bitrate runs roughly -15% per CRF point, BPP+ converts into a CRF
EQUIVALENT: "what CRF would an encode of this content at this bitrate be?"

    CRF_equivalent = 20 - ln(bpp/target) / ln(1/0.85)

    BPP+  30 -> CRF 34.8      BPP+ 125 -> CRF 17.3
    BPP+  50 -> CRF 28.5      BPP+ 150 -> CRF 15.0
    BPP+  75 -> CRF 23.5      BPP+ 200 -> CRF 11.5
     BPP+ 100 -> CRF 20.0
     Library today: p10 CRF 29.6 | p25 28.3 | MEDIAN 26.3 | p75 23.5 | p90 20.2

THIS REFRAMES THE HEADROOM QUESTION INTO AN ANSWERABLE ONE. "Should
HEADROOM_TARGET be 1.0 or 0.57?" is abstract. "Is a CRF-20 encode your definition
of correct, or is CRF 23.7?" is answerable from experience by anyone who has
encoded video — headroom 0.57 is exactly "100 means CRF 23.7". It does not
REPLACE a paired subjective test (CRF preference is itself content-dependent,
section 4) but it converts a calibration constant into a familiar judgement.
The -15%/point figure is now MEASURED: library median -13.6%/point, 2x spread
between films (section 6).

2.5 THE TARGET FORMULATION: MARGINAL QUALITY PER BIT
Brennan, 2026-08-18, from the Netflix per-title work: the transferable idea is not
the absolute bitrate but the SLOPE — how much quality another Mbps buys.
    3.0 -> 82,  3.5 -> 83,  4.0 -> 83.5,  5.0 -> 84    OVERSIZED (slope flat)
    2.0 -> 69,  2.5 -> 76,  3.0 -> 81,    3.5 -> 84    UNDERSIZED (still climbing)
Netflix spaces ladder rungs about one JND apart, which maps onto the bad -> OK ->
great -> oversized scale this project wants: band edges would become KNEE-DERIVED
rather than inherited.
WHAT WOULD BE NEEDED: a per-film rate-QUALITY curve, i.e. a quality axis (libvmaf,
16.A.6) on top of the rate curve the ladder gives. The pilot delivered the RATE
half and found NO KNEES in it (section 6). Netflix's scalar works because VMAF is
a perceptual axis; the lesson is not "one number is wrong" but "one number is only
meaningful if it rides a perceptual axis". That is the real open question about
BPP+: not whether a scalar is enough, but whether bpp/target is the right latent
axis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT COUNTS AS "bpp" — THE NUMERATOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three refinements, each of which fixed a real misscoring.

3.1 videoBitrate IS SANITY-CHECKED AGAINST THE FILE.
*arr's MediaInfo reports a nominal or peak rate rather than an average for some
HEVC files, and it is wildly wrong when it does. Challengers is 4.5 GB over
2:11:11 (4.6 Mb/s all-in) and Radarr claimed videoBitrate 30,317,898 — a 6.5x
overstatement that painted it purple and filed it as bloat. The video track
cannot out-rate the container, so anything above the size-derived total is
rejected (1.05 tolerance for container overhead).

3.2 AUDIO IS NOT CHARGED TO VIDEO.                                    [SOLID]
videoBitrate is 0 on ~18% of the library, so bpp falls back to the size-derived
total — and then every audio track inflates it. Measured on Lawrence of Arabia:
AC3 448k + DTS-HD MA 2.18 Mb/s = 2.63 Mb/s of a 16.61 Mb/s container, i.e. bpp
overstated ~19% and BPP+ ~9% (134 -> ~123).

The bias ran the WRONG WAY for our purposes: it flattered multi-dub and
lossless-audio releases most — exactly the class we do not want flattered.

*arr reports only ONE track's bitrate (the default), not the sum, so the audit
subtracts what is KNOWN and never guesses. bppSource() reports which happened:
    'video'                       exact, from MediaInfo
    'total-minus-audio-measured'  exact, our own ffprobe of every track
    'total-minus-audio'           exact enough (single audio track)
    'total-minus-audio-partial'   still a CEILING (more tracks than known rates)
    'total'                       no audio figure at all
Subtraction is capped at AUDIO_SHARE_CAP of the container so a corrupt reading
cannot zero out the video.

MEASURED: audio is a median 10.2% of the container, and up to 65.4% in the worst
case. Audio coverage is now 1031/1031 units, measured by our own nightly pass.
This is also the worked example behind the partial-data rule (section 16.B.4):
coverage grew from 8 films to 1031 and every film it reached was more accurate
for it.

3.3 HEVC GETS AN EFFICIENCY MULTIPLIER.
x265 at the same bpp looks better than x264, so raw bpp is scaled by
X265_EFFICIENCY before indexing. 52 files are HEVC; their median BPP+ is 83.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE DENOMINATOR — THE NIGHTLY CRF PROBE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is what makes BPP+ per-film rather than one-size-fits-all. Every night, in a
gated window, the controller takes 8 four-second samples spread across a title,
encodes them at CRF 20 TO A NULL OUTPUT, and records the bitrate that costs. That
is `complexity`: what THIS content costs to look right. Nothing is written;
/data is read-only in the container (1.2c).

WHY CRF: it is a PERCEPTUAL-QUALITY anchor, not a bitrate one. CRF-20 encodes of
Casablanca and Blade Runner 2049 are APPROXIMATELY the same visual quality; they
cost 3853 vs 1000 kb/s. That is exactly the per-film number the denominator needs.
"Approximately" is doing real work: fixed CRF is not exactly fixed quality across
content — published spread ~3-sigma 10 VMAF vs ~2.6 for quality-targeted encoding
(MainConcept, section 17). Netflix's per-title work rests on the same observation.
Whether that residual runs with grain HERE — which would make the denominator's
error systematic rather than random — is open question 4, never measured.

HEADROOM_TARGET = 1.0 means "100 = as many bits as a CRF-20 encode of this film".
Moving it does NOT require re-probing: x265 bitrate runs ~-13.6%/CRF point
(measured, section 6), so a preference for CRF 22 is a read-time constant. The
expensive measurement stays valid.

4.1 THE MEASUREMENT THAT KILLED THE FLAT CONSTANT                     [SOLID]
Before 2026-08-06 the denominator was one number, BPP_TARGET = 0.13, library-wide.
Measured complexity across 1031 units:
    min 0.0346 (The Father, clean digital) | p10 0.0705 | p25 0.0880 |
    median 0.1120 | p75 0.1497 | p90 0.2141 | max 0.9270 (12 Angry Men, B&W
    heavy grain) | spread 26.8x
The flat 0.13 was wrong by roughly 2x in BOTH directions and SYSTEMATICALLY: it
overrated grainy film-stock transfers (whose bits buy grain reproduction, not
detail) and underrated clean modern digital ones. The median 0.112 shows 0.13 was
a decent library-wide average and a poor per-film answer — the exact failure mode
you cannot see without measuring. It survives ONLY as the no-probe fallback.

4.2 PRECISION MACHINERY (what the nightly does beyond first measurement)
  PROBE_SAMPLES  8     the floor, not a luxury: scene complexity varies 8.2x
                       WITHIN a single film
  SAMPLES_MAX    16    used when a unit's spread or RSE says 8 was not enough
  POOL_MAX       64    samples accumulate across visits; SE falls as 1/sqrt(n)
  RSE_GOOD       0.12  +/-12% on the mean = precise enough to stop spending encodes
  RSE_WIDE       0.20
  SPREAD_WIDE    6     ~p75 of the library, measured 2026-08-17
  IMPRECISE_SHARE 3    one pick in 3 goes to refinement once fresh work runs out
  AUDIO_BATCH    8     audio files measured per tick
Pooling only happens when the bytes on disk are unchanged (`sameFile`), so a
replaced file starts clean rather than averaging two different encodes.
Nightly priority order: first-measurement -> audio backfill -> refinement.

4.3 THE POOLING BUG — REPEAT VISITS RE-MEASURED THE SAME CLIPS   [SOLID, FIXED]
     [found 2026-08-18 by Brennan, from banding in the lab's sample plot]
Sample offsets are deterministic (T = start + span*i/N), which is correct and
necessary — a ladder or re-probe must hit the same scenes. But x265 is ALSO
deterministic, so re-probing with the same N re-encoded IDENTICAL clips and
returned byte-identical bitrates. Those duplicates were pooled. Since SE =
sd/sqrt(n), the error bar SHRANK on evidence that did not exist — and it got worse
the more a film was "refined", the exact opposite of pooling's purpose.

    unit                     stored n   distinct   cxRSE reported   truth   under by
    Dunkirk (2017)              64         16          0.1986       0.3972    100%
    Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance  56         16          0.1790       0.3344     87%
    Casino Royale (1967)        16          8          0.1300       0.1903     46%
    Drive (2011)                24         16          0.1508       0.1844     22%
    Gladiator (2000)            24         16          0.1379       0.1648     19%

Every unit visited more than once was affected in proportion to how often; units
visited once (Frozen Planet II, Lessons in Chemistry) were correct.

RE-VERIFIED 2026-08-20 STRAIGHT FROM THE PRE-REPAIR BACKUP
(/config/probe-cache.json.bak-dedupe-20260819052712. — note the trailing dot in
the filename). Dunkirk then: 64 readings, 16 DISTINCT values, stored cxRSE
0.1986; recomputed on the distinct count it is 0.3972, so the reported error bar
was exactly half the truth. The 0.407 previously quoted here was 0.397.

*** AND A WARNING ABOUT CHECKING THIS CLAIM. *** A 2026-08-19 review reported
that this anecdote "does not reproduce" — because it recomputed from the CURRENT
cache, where the duplicates have already been re-counted and the number cannot
exist by construction. It reproduces to three decimals from the backup. A
before/after claim can only be checked against the "before" artifact; if that
artifact is a backup, the claim must name it, which is why the filename is now
written down. Dunkirk today: 48 distinct of 64, cxRSE 0.1667 — still the library
maximum, but the repair and the phase shift took it from 16 distinct clips to 48. WHY 24 YIELDS
16 DISTINCT: samplesFor() returns 8 or 16 and the 16-grid CONTAINS the 8-grid
(span*i/8 == span*2i/16), so a second visit at N=16 re-measured all 8 and added 8.
TWO FIXES, both needed:
  1. EFFECTIVE n. sampleStats counts DISTINCT clips — by position where known,
     falling back to distinct values for legacy entries (two scenes returning
     identical bitrates to 5 dp does not happen, so an exact repeat is a
     re-measured clip). Reported as `sampleNEff`; `sampleN` still counts readings.
  2. PHASE-SHIFTED GRIDS. probe-film.sh takes --phase (0 <= p < 1, in units of one
     sample gap); probe.js advances it per visit along a van der Corput sequence
     (0, 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/8...), so each visit lands in the largest remaining gap
     and pooling reduces the error bar for the right reason. `visits` is persisted.
CONSEQUENCE FOR EXISTING DATA: every pooled cxRSE written before this is too
small, and they are not re-derivable. The fix corrects future measurements and
re-derives effective n for existing ones, but the samples stay duplicated until
those units are visited again under a new phase.

4.4 UNCERTAINTY IS PUBLISHED, NOT HIDDEN
Until 2026-08-18 a number pinned to +/-3% and one pinned to +/-40% printed
identically, and the Audit tab would act on either.
THE SQUARE ROOT HALVES THE ERROR: a relative error e on the target becomes ~e/2 on
the index, so a complexity known only to +/-24% still yields a BPP+ good to about
+/-12%. bppRSE() returns rse/2, and BPP_RSE_LOOSE = 0.10 on the INDEX is a looser
gate than it first appears.
UI CONTRACT: past the threshold the score renders as `bpp+*`, real number and
error bar in the tooltip. A null RSE is treated as CONFIDENT, not doubtful — most
of the library has no per-sample data yet and marking 1000 films provisional would
make the mark meaningless. The mark is for films we KNOW are loosely measured.
CURRENT STATE (the weakest live area): 169 units carry a cxRSE (148 movies, 21
seasons), 116 pooled past 8 effective samples. Median cxRSE where it exists is
0.111 (p90 0.128), i.e. ~+/-5.5% on the index — the refine queue has been running
and the pool is growing. spreadRatio median 4.01, p75 6.24, p90 9.10.

4.5 TV SEASONS
A season is measured as a whole: episodes sampled, complexity episode-weighted,
all-or-nothing. `disagree` records how much sampled episodes disagreed. Median
disagree 0.241 across 146 seasons, 71 exceed 0.25 — season numbers are much softer
than movie numbers, and SEASON_DISAGREE_MAX = 0.25 gates them out of
disk-reclamation suggestions (that gate removed 92 GB of fiction in one pass).
A SERIES row is the episode-weighted mean of its own measured seasons, basis
`measured:series`, and requires EVERY season measured — a partial average would
silently describe a subset. Before it existed, series rows were scored against the
flat constant and rendered "never probed", i.e. "we know nothing" about shows that
are fully measured (all 146 season units across all 97 series are measured).

5. THE SOURCE-PINNING CORRECTION (biasFactor) — APPLIED TO EVERY SCORE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`complexity` is meant to be a property of the FILM. It is not, quite.

THE MECHANISM. The probe's input is our copy (1.2c), and CRF 20 cannot ask for
detail the source does not contain. A starved copy measures as an EASY film, its
target sinks toward that copy's own bitrate, and the deficient file scores close
to 100. THE WORSE THE COPY, THE MORE IT FLATTERS ITSELF — so BPP+ was
systematically OVERSTATED, most where accuracy matters. By 1.2(e) this is the
worst possible shape of error: it hits the starved side of an upgrade comparison
and not the good side.

FIRST PROOF, same film, same probe, only the source changed (Paris, Texas,
2026-08-12, a 2.22 GB copy replaced by a 12.73 GB Criterion transfer):
    srcBitrate x6.02  ->  complexity 0.05982 -> 0.13939   (x2.33)
And Easy Rider, measured BPP+ 78, was visibly full of grain-shimmer on flat walls;
Brennan put it at "a 60ish, or even lower like a 40" by eye. Corrected: ~65.

THE PREDICTOR IS R, no genre or year heuristics needed. R < 1 means the file sits
BELOW its own measured target — the probe ran out of source before it ran out of
appetite — so the number is a FLOOR. R > 1 means the probe had headroom.

5.1 THE STARVATION EXPERIMENT — THE DECISIVE RESULT            [SOLID, 2026-08-18]
Tooling: controller/scripts/probe-starve.sh + scripts/starve-experiment.js.
Data: data/starve-experiment.json.

DESIGN. Take 8 films the model ALREADY TRUSTS (R >= 1.2, no correction applied),
starve them to 1/2 and 1/4 bitrate, re-measure. The starving re-encodes ONLY THE
SAMPLED CLIPS — a full-film re-encode is ~7 h, and starving the exact clips the
ladder measures is equivalent and takes seconds. Content is byte-identical across
levels, so ANYTHING THAT MOVED WAS CAUSED BY STARVATION AND NOTHING ELSE. `cx20`
at each level IS the complexity the probe would report for that copy, so the
understatement is measured, not inferred. THE PREDICTION WAS RECORDED IN THE
SCRIPT BEFORE THE RUN, so it could not be fitted afterwards.

RESULT 1 — REAL, MONOTONIC, UNIVERSAL IN THE SAMPLE. 8/8 films flatten
monotonically as the copy is starved; mean flattening unstarved -> most-starved
4.12 slope points; 16 controlled points (8 films x 2 starved levels).

RESULT 2 — R PREDICTS THE UNDERSTATEMENT; LADDER SLOPE DOES NOT.
    Spearman(observed R,   pinning understatement)   -0.924
    Spearman(ladder slope, pinning understatement)   +0.335
*** THE LADDER-SLOPE HYPOTHESIS IS FALSIFIED *** (11.5). biasFactor's design,
indexing on R, is VINDICATED rather than merely necessary.

RESULT 3 — *** THE OLD R_TRUST = 1.2 CAP WAS WRONG. *** 6 of the 16 points sit
ABOVE R 1.2, where the live rule applied NOTHING; they average x1.270
understatement, and Requiem for a Dream at R 2.58 still shows x1.269.
WHY THE CAP EXISTED, AND WHY IT WAS REASONABLE: no banked upgrade pair sits above
R 1.2, because you only re-download films that were bad, so a factor up there
would have been invented rather than measured. Sound reasoning FOR OBSERVATIONAL
DATA. The starvation method simply is not limited that way, and where it looked,
it found pinning.

METHODOLOGICAL CAVEAT, FOUND AND CORRECTED — instructive, keep it. The experiment
used 3 samples per point where the probe uses 8-16, so its ABSOLUTE complexity did
not reproduce the live measurement on high-scene-variance titles (Blade Runner
2049 2.29x off, Requiem 1.87x, Fargo 1.46x; divergence tracks spreadRatio). WITHIN
a film the SAME clips are used at every level, so the understatement RATIO is
clean — scene-selection error largely cancels in a ratio — and only the x-axis was
noisy. FIX: anchor R to the LIVE probe, take only the RATIO from the experiment.
THE ANCHORING, RECOVERED AND WRITTEN DOWN 2026-08-20. It had never been recorded
beyond the phrase above, which is not enough to reproduce a fit:

    R_anchored = level * R_live * under          under = cx20(base) / cx20(level)

That is the OBSERVED R the library would compute for the starved copy: its bitrate
is level * src, and the complexity it would report is the base complexity divided
by `under`. The anchoring is using the LIVE probe's base complexity (8-16 samples)
in place of the experiment's own (3 samples), so the precise absolute scale comes
from the probe and only the RATIO comes from the experiment.

CONFIRMED BY REPRODUCTION: least squares of log(under/1.06) on log(R_anchored),
all 16 points, R_live taken PRE-audio-fix, returns A 1.337, B -0.349, rho -0.915 —
the shipped constants exactly. (The "n=15" in the older note is a slip: the fit
reproduces with all 16 points. An earlier version of this section guessed that
Blade Runner 2049 at level 0.5 had been excluded for setting the floor. The
reproduction shows it was not. Guess withdrawn.)

*** `under` IS ON BOTH SIDES OF THIS FIT, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. *** It is the
y-variable and it also sits inside R_anchored. That looks exactly like a mistake
and it is not: at runtime biasFactor only ever has the OBSERVED R of a starved
file, which is inflated by the very understatement being predicted. A calibration
curve must be fitted on the axis it will be evaluated on. The consequence is that
rho here CANNOT be read as evidence of a causal relationship — the self-reference
induces a POSITIVE bias, so a strong NEGATIVE rho survives it rather than being
produced by it. Use the unanchored own-R rho (-0.924) for the causal claim and this
one only for the calibration.

That gave 1.337 * R^-0.348 (rho -0.915, all 16 points) instead of the unanchored
1.414 * R^-0.298 — slightly weaker, the right direction to err.

*** THE x1.06 FLOOR WAS WRONG — MEASURED 2026-08-20, THE CURVE OVER-CORRECTS. ***
The starved copies were made with x265 and the probe also encodes x265, so some
measured understatement was CODEC MATCHING rather than starvation. A floor of
x1.06 was subtracted for it, estimated from ONE FILM (Blade Runner 2049 at level
0.5, barely starved, R > 5). The confound check finally ran: the same 8 films and
the same clips starved with libx264 instead, so the artefacts are h264-shaped and
the probe's x265 has nothing to match.

    codec-matching factor = under_x265 / under_x264
    16 matched points: MEDIAN 1.1295, mean 1.127, range 1.032-1.235
    bootstrap 95% CI on the median  1.099 .. 1.151     assumed: 1.06

THE ASSUMED VALUE SITS OUTSIDE THE INTERVAL — the effect is about TWICE what was
subtracted, and all 16 points exceed 1.0. Full report:
docs/REPORT-x264-confound-2026-08-20.md.

THE COMPARISON NEEDS NO R AXIS: `under` is a ratio of two measurements of the SAME
clips, so R, the codec normalisation and the anchoring all divide out. And the
controls agree bit-exactly (level 1.0 is copied, never re-encoded, and cx20 is
identical between arms on all 8 films), so the two arms measured the same material.

TWO RESULTS THAT MATTER MORE THAN THE NUMBER:
  * THE FLOOR IS A CONSTANT, so the model's SHAPE was right. Level 0.5 gives 1.1295
    and level 0.25 gives 1.1131 — agreeing to 0.017, and in the direction OPPOSITE
    to starvation depth, so the two effects are not tracking each other.
    Subtracting one constant was correct; the constant was wrong.
  * THE x264 ARM IS THE LIBRARY-REALISTIC CONDITION, not merely a control. 831 of
    898 movies here are h264: a real starved file IS an h264 encode probed with
    x265. The x265 arm was the artificial one. So the x264 understatements are not
    a comparison to be corrected — THEY ARE WHAT SHOULD HAVE BEEN FITTED, and they
    need no floor subtraction at all.

INTERIM ARITHMETIC (holding PIN_B): values too high by 1.1295/1.06 = x1.066, so
PIN_A ~1.255 rather than 1.337, and BPP+ on the corrected films is currently
~3.2% TOO LOW. The model is PESSIMISTIC — the safe direction, and ~1.5x the size
of the 5.2a audio fix in the opposite direction.

5.2a THE x-AXIS HAD AUDIO IN IT — FIXED 2026-08-20              [SOLID, MAJOR]
R must be video-only on both sides, for the same reason 3.2 took audio out of the
numerator. It was not. probe-film.sh takes srcBitrate from the video stream when
ffprobe reports one and falls back to the CONTAINER TOTAL when it does not, and on
the fallback path every audio track sat inside R. An inflated R makes biasFactor
too SMALL, so the correction was UNDER-applied — on multi-dub and lossless-audio
releases, i.e. exactly the class 3.2 exists to stop flattering. The same bias, one
layer deeper, on the correction's own input.

MEASURED (ffprobe ground truth, all 898 movies, 2026-08-20):
    container-path (polluted)  361      video-path (already clean)  537
    of the polluted rows, 271 changed biasFactor
    BPP+ was inflated by a median 2.17%, max 8.33% (The Witch, 39% audio)
    16 films sat in the wrong band

THE FIX IS SCOPED, NOT BLANKET, AND THAT DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE FINDING. The
obvious one-liner — subtract audioBps from every srcBitrate — would have deflated
the 537 already-clean rows by a median 2.1% (max 8.5%), a NEW error LARGER than the
one being repaired. probe-film.sh now records which numerator it used (`srcBasis`),
scripts/backfill-srcbasis.js filled it in for every pre-existing entry, and
videoR() in probe.js subtracts only on the container path. Applied: 251 movies fell,
all of them, median -2.35%; the controls that must not move did not.

THE FITTED CONSTANTS DID NOT NEED RE-DERIVING, and this is worth stating because it
looks like they should. All 8 calibration films are container-path files (audio
7.3-25.5%). But probe-starve.sh extracts its clips with -an -sn and starves them
with `-b:v level*SRCBPS`, so a starved clip genuinely CARRIES level*SRCBPS bits of
VIDEO, and the R attributed to it is that clip's true video-only R. The audio
inflated how much each level actually starved; the LABEL moved with it. Only levels
below 1.0 enter the fit, so the untouched control — the one copy that really does
carry audio it is not credited for — never lands on the x-axis. The experiment was
self-consistent; only the library-side application was wrong. DO NOT "FIX" THE
AUDIO IN probe-starve.sh: doing so would break that identity and mis-calibrate
PIN_A/PIN_B. There is a comment saying so at the point of temptation.

Fitted from our own banked pairs; every upgrade banks one (setEntry). Live fit
2026-08-20, 113 pairs (148 banked overall):
        R 0.00-0.30 x1.407 n=8  | R 0.30-0.40 x1.407 n=23
        R 0.40-0.50 x1.407 n=37 | R 0.50-0.70 x1.407 n=28
        R 0.70-1.00 x1.179 n=9  | R 1.00-1.20 x1.051 n=3 (interpolated to 1.0)
        R >= 1.20   x1.000  no correction at all

IT MOVED ON 2026-08-20 AND THAT IS THE POINT OF KEEPING IT. The four lowest
buckets read 1.562/1.562/1.458/1.429 the day before and now all read 1.407.
Nothing about the fitting changed — its x-axis did. Removing audio from R (5.2a)
lowered R on the container-path rows, they re-sorted into different buckets, the
medians moved, and the monotone guard then flattened the top four into one value.
A cross-check whose own input shares an axis with the thing being checked will
move whenever that axis is corrected; read a difference between this and the live
curve as a question, never as evidence on its own.
THREE GUARDS, because raw per-bucket medians are not safe as-is:
    BIAS_MIN_N = 8    a bucket with a handful of pairs is noise. The 1.00-1.20
                      bucket once had n=1 and gave x1.39 — HIGHER than the
                      better-sampled bucket below it (x1.24, n=4).
    MONOTONE          the correction must not grow as R grows.
    BIAS_R_TRUST=1.2  measured wrong (5.1 result 3); legacy path only.
Gaps are filled BY POSITION, never by carrying a neighbour — carrying was wrong in
both directions and both errors were live for one deploy. Non-monotonic pairs
(complexity FELL when the source improved — 18 of 96, noise or a changed cut) are
excluded entirely.

*** IT DOES NOT MEANINGFULLY SHARPEN ITSELF. *** A pair exists only when Brennan
replaces a film: ~78 a year against 147 banked, so doubling the evidence takes two
years, and the guards need 8 pairs PER BUCKET so sparse buckets stay sparse. "It
improves with no code change" is technically true and practically useless. THIS IS
THE ARGUMENT THAT MOTIVATED 5.1 — manufacture the pairs instead of waiting.

STILL A FLOOR, DELIBERATELY: 80 of the 129 corrected films have R < 1 even
AFTER their upgrade, so the better copy was itself pinned and one round of
correction under-corrects. It moves the number toward truth; it does not arrive.

WHY THE PAIRS SCATTER LOOKS RANDOM, and it nearly is: Spearman(old R, ratio) is
-0.333 (in-fit) / -0.410 (all pairs), within-bucket ranges like x1.00-x2.91. The
outcome is dominated by a variable the fit excludes — how big the upgrade was.
Spearman(bitrate growth, ratio) = +0.759, more than twice the predictor in use.
Indexing on R alone is right at SCORING time (a film on disk has an R and no
future copy) but wrong at FITTING time. Exactly why the controlled experiment
beats the pairs.

THE CONTINUOUS CROSS-CHECK: complexity_ratio ~ (srcNew/srcOld)^beta. beta is
reliably positive and around a third but NOT pinned — four defensible inclusion
rules on the same 51 pairs give 0.30, 0.32, 0.33, 0.41; bootstrap 95% CI
0.217-0.436 (n=35); leave-one-out tight (0.293-0.347), so no single pair drives
it; trimming outliers pulls it DOWN. Paris, Texas alone measured 0.47.
*** DO NOT QUOTE A TWO-DECIMAL beta. *** An earlier edition reported "beta =
0.351, R2 0.328" as settled; it is one draw from that interval. Extrapolating each
R to R_TRUST with beta = 0.351 predicts x1.876 / x1.542 / x1.411 / x1.276 /
x1.129 / x1.031 at R = 0.20 / 0.35 / 0.45 / 0.60 / 0.85 / 1.10 — agreeing with the
buckets within ~10% in the middle (R 0.4-1.1, where nearly all pairs live) and
diverging hard below R ~ 0.3, where BOTH are extrapolating (only 1 of 147 pairs
has an old R below 0.2).

A 2-VARIABLE MODEL DOES NOT RESCUE IT, worth knowing before anyone tries: adding
log(oldR) alongside log(growth) raises R2 by 0.033, oldR is not significant
(t=1.64, df=32), and its SIGN FLIPS between the 1- and 2-variable fits. Growth and
oldR correlate -0.657 — worse-pinned films got bigger upgrades, a SELECTION effect
from which films Brennan chose to replace, not physics.

SPLITTING THE FIT BY GRAININESS: ATTEMPTED, INCONCLUSIVE. Splitting on OLD
complexity gives grainy beta 0.378 (n=18) vs clean 0.282 (n=17) — the direction
the grain-absorption worry predicts — but bootstrap CIs overlap almost entirely
(0.21-0.51 vs 0.15-0.45), and splitting on NEW complexity gives 0.277 vs 0.009. A
gap that inverts on an arbitrary methodological choice is not a finding.

5.4 THE GRAIN CONTAMINATION WORRY (open question 3)                 [UNTESTED]
The OBSERVATIONAL fit cannot separate pinning from grain: when a starved copy of a
grainy film is replaced by a better transfer, complexity rises both because more
detail is present and because more grain is resolved. Some grain may therefore be
absorbed into a coefficient labelled "source pinning".
5.1 IS LARGELY IMMUNE TO THIS, a real advantage of the controlled method: the same
film at three starvation levels has the same grain by construction, so what it
measures is starvation. If the two methods agree where they overlap, the
contamination in the observational fit is bounded.
Closing it needs a grain covariate that is NOT raw complexity — raw complexity is
what the grain question is ABOUT and cannot also be its control. gridRatio (9.2)
is the candidate.

WORTH KNOWING: the pairs are not reproducible. The superseded copy is deleted at
swap time, so a prior only holds what PRIOR_FIELDS (probe.js) captured when it was
banked. Any new per-pair measurement must be added there BEFORE the next upgrades
land, or those pairs simply won't have it.

6. THE CRF LADDER PILOT                          [SOLID, 20 films, 2026-08-18]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four rungs (CRF 16/20/24/28), 4 samples each, identical scenes at every rung.
Tooling: controller/scripts/probe-ladder.sh + scripts/ladder-pilot.js; data in
data/ladder-pilot.json. COST, measured not guessed: ~10 min/film, ~3.5 h for the
pilot. Library-wide would be ~170 h, which is why it is a pilot.

FINDING 1 — THE RELATIONSHIP IS A CLEAN EXPONENTIAL. NO KNEES.
    R2 >= 0.977 on every film, median 0.998. Max |curvature| 0.008.
log(bitrate) against CRF is a straight line for all 20, across the full range of
content from Arrival (complexity 0.028) to 12 Angry Men (1.09). The
-15%-per-CRF-point form is the right SHAPE.

FINDING 2 — THE SLOPE VARIES ~2x BETWEEN FILMS, AND IT IS NOT NOISE.
    median -13.6%/point, range -9.9% to -19.4%, SD 2.98 points
Because each individual fit is precise, that spread is real content variation,
not measurement error. The library-wide constant should be -13.6%, not -15.0%.

FINDING 3 — SLOPE TRACKS SUPPLY ACROSS THE LIBRARY. *** BUT SEE 11.5. ***
    Spearman(slope, supply R)   -0.750
    Spearman(slope, biasFactor) +0.736
    Spearman(slope, src Mb/s)   -0.731
    Spearman(slope, complexity) +0.308   <- barely anything
    Spearman(slope, year)       +0.121   <- nothing

    shallowest: Blair Witch (14% supplied) -9.9%,  Tangerine (26%) -10.0%

WHAT SURVIVES: findings 1 and 2. The rate curve has no knees and the per-CRF

re-probing: x265 is ~-13.6%/CRF point, so a preference is a multiplier.

    is worth remembering before treating 572 red rows as 572 separate decisions.
Encoding that ignores content is exactly what per-title work exists to replace
(section 17, Netflix). We are on the consuming end of it.

8.7 DOES BPP+ OVER-REWARD GRAIN? NO — IT PENALISES IT.   [SOLID, 2026-08-19]

big denominator and a big file could look rewarded for reproducing grain nobody
asked for. The data says the opposite. By complexity quartile, 885 movies:

    Q1 (cleanest)  median cx 0.073   live BPP+ 71   (flat-denominator equiv 63)
    Q4 (grainiest) median cx 0.198   live BPP+ 55   (flat-denominator equiv 94)
    Q1: 61% "bad", 6% "wow"          Q4: 73% "bad", 1% "wow"

THE SIGN FLIP REMAINS, WEAKER AT RAW LEVEL (see 11.2 note on labels; the 2026-08-13
blind-test retraction removed the magnitudes, the direction survives on measured
complexity):
    Spearman(BPP+ under flat 0.13, complexity) = +0.442  <- rewards grain
    Spearman(BPP+ live,            complexity) = -0.328  <- penalises it

The specific failure mode — a grainy film ALSO generously supplied — has ZERO
instances. Of the 26 movies with complexity >= 0.30, the highest R is 1.09 and
none reaches "wow". R and complexity correlate -0.31 library-wide: grainy titles
here are systematically the most STARVED, not the most indulged.

So no formula change. If one were ever warranted it should be a DISPLAY
ANNOTATION, not a term (which would corrupt a number that means one clean thing)
and not a gate (ordinary grain is not a ceiling — 12 Angry Men CAN reach 100 with
a bigger file, unlike the Blair Witch class). Building it needs a grain metric
that survives the CGI and mean-luma degeneracy tests that killed gShare (11.3),
which does not exist.
THE TRIGGER TO REVISIT is cheap: watch the (complexity, R) plane. A title
migrating into "high grain cost AND R > 1.2" is the case this concern describes.
None exists today.

9.1 GRAIN BOILING IS REAL AND WAS MEASURED HERE.              [DIRECTION, n=2]
Brennan reported "random noise buzzing and flickering" in bright flat areas (Star
Wars dunes and sky, Blues Brothers, Jackie Brown, Jurassic Park, Taxi Driver,
Easy Rider skies). Two independent discriminators (scripts/probe-noise-origin.sh
+ analyze-noise-origin.js), bright pixels only, scene cuts excluded:

                                  Star Wars 9:19    No Other Choice (2025)
                                  (artifacts)       ("artifact and grain free")
    pbFloor  noise per frame          2.64              1.78
    iRatio   I-frame diff / P-B diff  1.01              0.44
    gridRatio across/inside 8x8 blk   1.165             0.995
    source bitrate                   10.0 Mb/s          2.0 Mb/s
    R                                 2.45              0.33

TEMPORALLY IT IS GRAIN — renewed every frame, no jump at keyframes. Compression
noise freezes inside a GOP and cannot do that. SPATIALLY IT CARRIES THE CODEC'S
8x8 FINGERPRINT — 1.165 against a 0.995 clean baseline. Grain knows nothing about
the DCT grid, so the encoder added that.

GRAIN BOILING: the film's own random pattern, quantised through a DCT at a
bitrate that cannot carry it, so each block approximates its grain slightly
differently. It explains every observation — bright flat areas are where grain
lives unmasked, and it "buzzes" because the pattern really is renewing.

WHY HE HAD NEVER SEEN IT: the 720p projector downscaled every 1080p file ~44%, an
effective grain filter. He saw it first on a phone at native resolution. The
1080p projector removes that mask (section 2.3).

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH. These are instrument readings, so they
survive the retraction in 11.1. What does NOT survive is the inference once built
on top: that grain contamination explained R's 0.146 against the blind-test
verdicts. Those verdicts were grain labels, and R scoring ~0 against a grain
label is R behaving correctly. GRAIN IS NOT ESTABLISHED AS BPP+'S CENTRAL
PROBLEM. It is a physical finding on two clips, not yet connected to any quality
judgement. The only defect demonstrated under control is source pinning.

n=2 IS A DIRECTION, NOT A COEFFICIENT. Easy Rider failed as a third control: 245
of 249 frames were dropped as camera motion. The measurement needs STATIC BRIGHT
scenes, which is the real obstacle to running it library-wide.

9.2 gridRatio IS THE BEST REMAINING ARTIFACT CANDIDATE.            [UNTESTED]
On theory it isolates the part more bits CAN fix, where blockMean measures the
part they cannot. Both are untested against a valid label, so this is a reason to
test gridRatio FIRST, not a result.

9.3 MORE BITS REDUCE BOILING BUT THE GRAIN STAYS. No upgrade makes Star Wars look

9.4 THE DEMONSTRATION OF HOW MUCH OF THESE FILES IS GRAIN. ~/grain-test/ holds

10. WHAT BPP+ IS DELIBERATELY BLIND TO — THE GATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BPP+ answers exactly one question: "does this file have enough bits for its own
content". Everything below is a CEILING on quality that bits cannot raise, so
each is a GATE beside the score rather than a term inside it (constraint 1.2d).

10.1 SOURCE TIER
    SRC_RANK: remux 5 | bluray/brrip/bdrip 4 | webdl 4 | webrip 3 | hdtv 2
              | dvd 1 | sdtv/cam/telesync 0
A perfectly-encoded HDTV capture can score BPP+ 120 and still be a worse master
than a starved Bluray. The Disk section refuses any candidate whose effective
rank is below what you already hold.

10.2 RE-ENCODES ARE A FULL TIER DOWNGRADE
*arr reports BRRip/BDRip as plain "Bluray", so the rank was blind to them.
    REENC_RE = /brrip|bdrip|web-?rip|hdrip/i ; REENC_TIER_COST = 1
    effSrcRank = srcRank - (isReenc ? 1 : 0)
A full tier is honest: BRRip is disc -> rip -> re-encode, the same generation
count as a WEBRip (stream -> re-encode), and WEBRip already ranks 3.
MEASURED: 120 of 885 probed movie files (13.6%) match on their ON-DISK path, 82
of those labelled "Bluray-*". Detecting on *arr's `originalFilePath` (the
pre-rename release name, which survives renaming) raises it to 187 of 886 (21%),
110 labelled "Bluray-1080p" — including Forrest Gump, Saving Private Ryan, Back
to the Future, Indiana Jones. The on-disk path undercounts; prefer the *arr field.
Consequences, all live:
  - Disk section refuses re-encodes outright (that section assumes "same master,
    fewer wasted bits"). Verified: 0 re-encode candidates across 29 rows / 263
    candidates.
  - Playback/source upgrades can now see through the label: replacing a BRRip
    with a genuine disc encode used to score 4-against-4 and register as no
    upgrade at all.
  - Upgrade section admits a re-encode regardless of its BPP+. Only 6 genuinely
    new rows, since 181 of the 187 were already under the BPP+ 100 gate. The 18
    now flagged include Donnie Darko at BPP+ 101, which the bitrate gate would
    have hidden forever.
Both sides of every comparison use the same effective rank, so holding a BRRip
does not refuse you another one as a "downgrade from itself".

10.3 AUDIO LAYOUT. A 1.5 GB encode that dropped 5.1 Atmos for stereo AAC is a
downgrade no bitrate ratio can see. AUDIO_RANK is a separate comparison in
candidate ranking. (Note the playback ground truth: everything ends as stereo in
this room, so lossless/multichannel is not treated as quality — audioCls()
deliberately returns '' for everything.)

10.4 EDITION / IMAX. Curated lists, not measurements. IMAX_BEST is keyed
"title year" (e.g. 'dune 2021') across ~15 titles where sequences were genuinely
shot large-format. WHY A LIST AND NOT ASPECT RATIO: open matte and pan-and-scan
BOTH move a 2.39 film toward 1.78, but the first ADDS picture and the second
CROPS the sides off; without knowing intended framing, a 1.78 copy of a scope
film is as likely to be vandalism as a gift. IMAX_AR_MAX = 2.0 suppresses false
positives on films already framed at 1.78. "IMAX Enhanced" is matched SEPARATELY
and NOT trusted as framing — it is a DTS/HDR licensing badge. The list is keyed
with YEAR because normEdTitle() strips "(year)", so a bare 'dune' key matched
both Villeneuve's Dune (2021) and Lynch's Dune (1984). IMAX PREFERENCE NEVER
HARD-BLOCKS; it only reorders. Maintain alongside controller/oscar-winners.json
and film-awards.json.

10.5 4K IS NEVER AN UPGRADE. Nothing in the house plays above 1080p. 2160p
candidates are excluded regardless of score.

10.5a BANDING — NOW MEASURABLE, AND IT IS A REAL SECOND AXIS      [SOLID, 2026-08-20]
Banding is the one artifact BPP+ is STRUCTURALLY blind to: flat gradients
quantised into visible steps, which a file can suffer while being perfectly
adequate on average. It was unmeasurable here until 2026-08-20.

WHY THE "NO MASTER" CONSTRAINT DOES NOT BIND. CAMBI is genuinely NO-REFERENCE —
it keys on flat regions within a single frame. Only libvmaf's plumbing wants two
inputs, so both are pointed at the same file. Banding is therefore measurable in
PRODUCTION, forever, with no master. VMAF proper is NOT usable that way (a
same-file comparison scores ~100 by construction) and stays calibration-only.

*** THE TOOL IS A SECOND ffmpeg AND MUST STAY SEPARATE. *** Nothing here has
libvmaf (controller 5.1, jellyfin 7.1.4 has vmafmotion only, host 4.2), so
scripts/setup-vmaf-tool.sh fetches a PINNED static build into tools/. NEVER use it
for complexity and never "fix" the controller by upgrading its ffmpeg: that x265
made all 1044 complexity measurements and is the only ruler for them.

FIRST NEW SIGNAL TO SURVIVE ITS OWN DEGENERACY TESTS. 40 films, stratified by
complexity AND R (all 26 grain-heavy films are also starved, so complexity-only
sampling would make grain and supply inseparable). Thresholds fixed before the
numbers were read; |r| > 0.6 would have failed it:
    vs complexity (grain)     -0.245 pearson / -0.392 spearman   passes
    vs mean luma (darkness)   -0.254 / -0.295                    passes
    vs BPP+ (redundancy)      -0.313 / -0.322                    passes
For scale, gShare died at -0.885 against luma (11.3).

AND THE MECHANISM IS THERE, not just independence. Among the SMOOTH half, starved
copies band 2.33x more than supplied ones (median cambi 2.682 vs 1.151) — banding
tracks starvation on exactly the content where banding is physically possible.

IT DISAGREES WITH BPP+ IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, which is the point:
    The Graduate      BPP+ 75 warn, cambi 4.13 — adequate on bits, bands anyway
    Army of Shadows   BPP+ 81 warn, cambi 3.36 — same
    Bridge on the River Kwai  BPP+ 67 bad, cambi 0.033 — starved, but bits are the
                      ONLY problem, so a replacement would actually fix it
Grain dithers gradients away, which is why the grainiest tercile bands LEAST
(median 0.936 vs 2.087 mid). The relationship is NON-MONOTONIC and the smooth
tercile banding less than the middle (1.467 vs 2.087) is UNEXPLAINED.

PREVALENCE, from a second TARGETED run of 45 more films restricted to BPP+ >= 75
(a random sample mostly draws films the score already condemns, and those get
replaced regardless of banding):
    OF 58 FILMS BPP+ CALLS ACCEPTABLE, 8 BAND VISIBLY = 14% (95% CI ~5-23%)
    warn 75-99  6/34   ok 100-124  2/17   wow >=125  0/7
Rate falls with the score, which is the sanity check it needed. Two of the eight
are in the GREEN band with supply ABOVE transparency — Fargo (BPP+ 109, R 1.22)
and Obsession (BPP+ 103, R 1.12). No bitrate reasoning reaches those: they have
the bits and they still band.

THE BLIND SPOT IS SMOOTH CONTENT, as the mechanism predicts: banding films have
median complexity 0.058 against 0.102 for clean ones, 6 of 8 below 0.10. Grain
dithers gradients away; smooth modern digital has nothing to hide the steps.

AND THE DEGENERACY TESTS REPLICATE on those 45 films, which were never used to set
any threshold: vs complexity -0.444, vs luma -0.022, vs BPP+ -0.273. LUMA COMES
BACK AT -0.02 — the darkness degeneracy is dead outright, not merely under the
line. Complexity rises to -0.444: partial dependence, consistent with grain
suppressing banding, still well inside 0.6.

Full report: docs/REPORT-cambi-2026-08-20.md (85 films total).

WHERE IT GOES: a GATE beside the score, never a term (no valid label to weight
it). If it ever enters the score it multiplies the DENOMINATOR, capped ~1.6 —
never additively, because the sqrt then halves a misread to <= ~2 points.

*** SHIPPED AS A NIGHTLY JOB, 2026-08-20 (controller/lib/banding.js). *** It is no
longer a research script: "Audit · banding probe" in the Jobs tab, backfilling the
library at 4x2s clips per unit, manually triggerable for daytime catch-up. The 85
films measured during the research round were imported rather than re-encoded.

IT SHARES THE PROBE'S GATES AND ITS ONE NIGHT BUDGET. Not its own — that is the
safety property: the box never runs more than PROBE_NIGHT_BUDGET_MS of encoding a
night no matter which job spends it. A second budget would silently double the
nightly heat, and 12.4 is why that ceiling exists. Banding takes the window while
the probe has only REFINEMENT left; genuinely fresh complexity work still wins,
because a missing complexity number degrades every score for that title while a
missing banding number degrades nothing.

`cambi` IS null WHEN UNMEASURED, WHICH IS NOT "CLEAN". Most of the library has no
reading yet. Every consumer must distinguish the two or it will assert the whole
unmeasured library is banding-free. In bpp-lab it is a COLOUR and an X-AXIS (not a
filter — filtering answers nothing interesting); unmeasured rows are drawn grey and
are DROPPED from the banding axis rather than coerced to zero, which would pile
~90% of the library on the left edge as "least banding of all".

10.5b CAN BANDING BE FOLDED INTO BPP+? NOT YET, AND THE REASON IS SPECIFIC.
Brennan proposed the obviously right shape — predict EXPECTED banding from the
film's characteristics, and read measured/expected as "needs more bitrate", the
same move BPP+ already makes for bits. Tested 2026-08-21 on 85 films
(scripts/banding-expected.js, docs/REPORT-banding-excess-2026-08-21.md):

  IT WORKS DIRECTIONALLY. Excess tracks supply better than raw banding does
  (R -0.412 -> -0.542, BPP+ -0.416 -> -0.528), and the residual is clean of the
  content it was regressed out of (cxEff -0.004, luma -0.055, year +0.003).

  *** BUT RAW bpp PREDICTS BANDING BETTER (-0.684) THAN BPP+ DOES (-0.416), AND
  THAT IS THE MECHANISM. *** Banding is quantisation coarseness, set by ABSOLUTE
  bits per pixel, not by bits relative to content difficulty. A grainy film needs
  many bits (high complexity, so BPP+ normalises them away) but its grain also
  DITHERS BANDING AWAY. So the per-film normalisation that makes BPP+ right for
  adequacy works AGAINST it for banding. The cxEff coefficient flips sign between
  the two models and says the same thing: -0.50 content-only (complex films band
  less), +0.27 holding bpp fixed (at equal bits, harder content bands more). Both
  true, different questions.
  CONSEQUENCE: banding cannot be folded in by adjusting BPP+'s existing
  denominator, because the denominator is the part that is wrong for banding.

  AND A RATIO OF SMALL NUMBERS IS NOT A SIGNAL. At high bpp expected banding goes
  to ~0, so any reading gives a huge ratio. The illustrative adjustment gave 2001:
  A Space Odyssey (cambi 0.32, BELOW the library median 1.08) the MAXIMUM penalty,
  141 -> 111. That is the model failing, not the film.

  THE OBVIOUS REPAIR ADDS NOTHING: requiring high absolute banding AND positive
  excess flags 16 films; the absolute threshold alone flags the same 16. The guard
  removes 0 of 16.

WHAT IS CONFIRMED IS THE GAP. Among films at similar BPP+, banding still spreads
25-101x — so BPP+ genuinely does not capture it, which is exactly why it is
reported beside the score rather than inside it.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER, concretely: predict expected banding from the thing
that physically causes banding-proneness — THE FRACTION OF FRAME AREA THAT IS FLAT
OR NEAR-FLAT GRADIENT — instead of proxies like year and luma. CAMBI's internals
already segment flat regions and the same static build ships `siti`. A film that is
40% smooth sky has a different banding expectation from one that is all texture,
and neither year nor luma captures that. If content R^2 rose from 0.15 to ~0.5 the
ratio would stabilise and this section should be revisited. Two cheaper
contributors are already in motion: the banding job is backfilling 960 more units
(a 12x larger sample), and a forced-choice label would let the coefficient be
CALIBRATED rather than invented.

10.5c THE ARTIFACT-RESIDUAL FRAMEWORK — Brennan's idea, and the most promising
      direction open to this model.                          [DIRECTION, 2026-08-21]
Full design: docs/DESIGN-ARTIFACT-RESIDUAL.md.

"At its core BPP+ is saying: is your file big enough to look good. And what does
looking good mean if not that it's free of bad artifacts?" So: predict how much of
an artifact a film OUGHT to show from its content AND its bitrate, measure how much
it does, and let the disagreement CORRECT THE SCORE — up when a copy is cleaner
than one that size should be, down when it is worse. BIDIRECTIONAL: on 85 films,
36 up, 46 down, 3 unchanged. Not a penalty term, not a re-anchoring.

IT IS NOT THE SAME AS 10.5b, which asked whether banding reveals a deficiency to
add to the DENOMINATOR and was rejected. This does not touch the denominator; it
treats the artifact as an INDEPENDENT ESTIMATOR of what BPP+ estimates. That
inverts 10.5b's methodology rule: there the expected-model had to be blind to
bitrate, here seeing bitrate IS the mechanism.

THE MAGNITUDE IS MEASURABLE, WHICH IS THE BEST PART. BPP+ is already denominated in
bits, so if a film bands like a copy carrying bpp' instead of its actual bpp, its
honest score is the score of bpp'. Given S = d log(artifact)/d log(bpp), the
conversion is arithmetic. Two measured guards on top:
  RELIABILITY SHRINKAGE. The residual is signal plus noise; Kelley's correction
  shrinks it by its split-half reliability. Measured 0.579 at 4 clips per film.
  This is what makes the framework safe to EXTEND — a junk detector contributes
  ~nothing instead of contributing garbage, with no subjective label needed.
  ACTED ON: BANDING_SAMPLES 4 -> 8 (reliability 0.73; 12 clips would give 0.80).

*** THE PER-FILM LADDER CHANGED THE ARITHMETIC (5 films, 5 starvation levels). ***
    Skyfall -1.534 R2 0.962 | Confessions -0.910 R2 0.987 | Goldfinger -0.618 R2
    0.996 | E.T. -0.554 R2 0.990 | El Mariachi -0.435 R2 0.691
  Per-film curves are REAL and SMOOTH. Two findings:
  1. EVERY per-film slope is FLATTER than the cross-film -1.78 (mean ~-0.81). The
     cross-film fit overstates sensitivity ~2.2x — the SAME between-vs-within
     confound the pinning work hit when observational pairs disagreed with the
     controlled experiment (5.1). Only a ladder holds content constant.
  2. THE NAIVE 1/|S| SCALING IS BACKWARDS. It gives a FLAT responder a BIGGER
     shift, but if banding barely moves with bitrate then banding is UNINFORMATIVE
     about bitrate. Inverse-variance weighting gives shift ∝ |S|*r, NOT r/|S|.
     That is why E.T., the flattest responder, took the maximum penalty in the
     naive version.

WHERE IT STANDS: the SHAPE is fully measured (direction, per-film |S| scaling,
reliability shrinkage). What remains is ONE overall scale, needing the ratio of
BPP+'s own error variance to the artifact's — which needs one subjective anchor
(question 0). From "an invented coefficient" to "one free parameter for the whole
model" is the real progress here.

NOT DEPLOYED. Under the naive form it moved far too much (median 10.9 points, 29
band flips of 85) and penalised demonstrably clean films — 2001: A Space Odyssey
at cambi 0.32, BELOW the library median, took the maximum penalty. That is the
expected-model failing (R^2 0.488) and the residual absorbing the failure.

IT GENERALISES, and that is the point. Any artifact needs two things in order: a
SPLIT-HALF RELIABILITY check (no label required — this is the unlock) and a MEASURED
slope against bitrate. blockMean/blurMean have been marked UNTESTED for months on
the assumption that judging a detector needs a label; it does not, it needs repeated
readings, which probe-film.sh computed all along and then averaged away. FIXED
2026-08-21: sampleBlock/sampleBlur are now stored and exposed, so both can be graded
by exactly the test that graded CAMBI, at zero extra cost.
And GRAIN fits in the direction people forget: LESS grain than a 1974 film should
have is a denoised or generation-lossed source — a quality LOSS that currently
RAISES the score, because a denoised copy measures as easy content.

THE CAUTION: several corrections must not double-count. Reliability screens each
detector alone; INDEPENDENCE needs checking pairwise. Blocking, blur and banding all
rise together under starvation, so they are unlikely to be independent — the right
treatment is probably one combined residual, not three additive shifts.

10.6 GRAINY AND UNDER-PROVISIONED — DEFINED, MEASURED, NOT YET RENDERED
        grainStarved = complexity >= 0.30 && R <= 1.0
Both terms already exist per row; nothing new is measured. Full analysis in
docs/REPORT-grain-gate-2026-08-20.md.

MEASURED 2026-08-20, after the refit: ALL 26 movies at complexity >= 0.30 qualify.
The maximum R in the entire group is 1.066 (Citizen Kane, the only one above 1.0).
Bands: 18 bad, 8 warn, ZERO ok, ZERO wow — median BPP+ 67.5. STILL not one

WHY IT IS A GATE AND NOT A SCORE CHANGE. The score is right — grain IS screen

11.1 THE UNPAIRED BLIND TEST — RETRACTED (run 2026-08-13, invalidated 08-18)
Eleven films Brennan had ACTUALLY WATCHED, judged on a phone at native
resolution, scored 1 = artifacts / 0.5 = maybe / 0 = clean. Spearman vs the eye:
    src Mb/s 0.905 | complexity 0.824 | R 0.146 | blurMean -0.266 | blockMean -0.090
WHAT WENT WRONG: what Brennan was scoring as "artifacts" was GRAIN. Nobody can
separate the two by eye from a single UNPAIRED clip — which is why subjective
standards use paired or reference-anchored presentation, not "rate this one".
THE TABLE INVERTS ON THAT READING: src Mb/s 0.905 is near-tautological against a
grain label (high-bitrate files reproduce grain faithfully, and grainy catalogue
films get generous disc encodes — both push the same way); complexity 0.824 the
same, since complexity is very largely grain cost (8.3); and R 0.146 is the ONLY
one of the three that is NOT a grain proxy, because R divides grain out of the
numerator AND the denominator. So R scoring ~0 here is R behaving as it should.
An earlier edition called 0.146 "the model's central problem" — withdrawn.
WHAT IS LEFT STANDING: nothing. The test neither validates nor falsifies BPP+.
TWO CONSEQUENCES THAT MUST NOT BE LOST:
  - THERE IS STILL NO VALID SUBJECTIVE LABEL ANYWHERE IN THIS LIBRARY. Every
    fitted weight, band edge and calibration constant is therefore unvalidated.
  - THERE IS NO CONFIRMED CASE of a film judged genuinely compression-damaged.
    Every artifact report on record resolved to grain. BPP+ has never been shown
    to misrank anything: THE HONEST STATE OF THE MODEL IS UNTESTED, NOT BROKEN.
The durable lesson is procedural and is now trap 8.

11.2 SSIM AND VMAF DO NOT SOLVE GRAIN — AND SSIM IS DISQUALIFIED OUTRIGHT
Both over-reward grain reproduction. Grain is stochastic, so its pixels land
differently in source and encode, and pixelwise full-reference metrics penalise
that. On the grainy catalogue titles that matter, an SSIM ladder KEEPS CLIMBING
AND NEVER SHOWS A KNEE — already on record ("your SSIM-keeps-improving-at-247-
BPP+ result", RESEARCH-quality-metrics-2026-08-01.md). Netflix's own VMAF blog
lists FILM GRAIN FIRST among unaddressed areas, and Netflix/vmaf issue #1192 is
an open bug about exactly this. A ladder measured with either never bends on a
grainy title. Do not substitute SSIM for VMAF "for now"; that rebuilds a known
dead end. (Both are also full-reference, i.e. excluded by constraint 1.2a for the
upgrade question — they can only ever measure distance from our own copy.)


UPDATE 2026-08-20, AND KEEP THE DISTINCTION SHARP: this entry is about VMAF and
SSIM, and it still stands for both. It does NOT apply to CAMBI, which ships inside
libvmaf but is a different thing — no-reference, banding-specific, and now measured
here as an independent axis (10.5a). VMAF needs a master and is calibration-only;
CAMBI needs none and works in production. Do not cite this entry to argue against
the banding axis.
11.3 gShare — FALSIFIED (docs/REPORT-grain-2026-08-13.md)
A grain metric built by encoding with x265 `-tune grain` and differencing
bitrates. It did not measure grain: Up (2009), rendered CGI with no photochemical
grain at all, scored 0.407 against Casablanca 0.422 and Alien 0.421, and the
metric was largely a restatement of DARKNESS (r = -0.885 against mean luma). DO
NOT REVIVE IT. Any replacement must pass the same two degeneracy tests — does it
separate CGI from film stock, and is it independent of mean luma.

11.4 blockMean / blurMean — UNTESTED, NOT FALSIFIED. KEEP THIS DISTINCTION SHARP.
Their correlations (-0.090 and -0.266) came from the retracted labels and were
withdrawn WITH them, along with the "do not revive blockMean" verdict and the
false-positive case list behind it. blockMean's -0.090 says blockiness and
grain-visibility are unrelated, which tells us nothing about whether it detects
blocking. THAT IS NOT AN ARGUMENT TO REVIVE THEM: the evidence either way does
not exist. They remain stored and displayed, and inputs to no score.

11.5 THE LADDER-SLOPE INSTRUMENT — FALSIFIED (2026-08-18)
THE HYPOTHESIS: a starved source has thrown detail away, so lowering CRF buys
nothing back and the bitrate stops responding — a SHALLOW slope; a well-supplied
source keeps costing bits per CRF point — a STEEP slope. Therefore ladder slope
measures source exhaustion DIRECTLY, on any film, in ~10 min, and crucially
WITHOUT CIRCULARITY: R = srcBitrate/probeBitrate has the possibly-pinned
complexity in its denominator, whereas slope comes from the ladder's SHAPE.
WHY IT LOOKED STRONG: rho -0.750 against supply across 20 films (section 6, finding 3),
against rho -0.333 for the observational pair fit.
HOW IT DIED: the starvation experiment holds CONTENT CONSTANT and varies supply.
Under that control, slope predicts the understatement at +0.335 while observed R
predicts it at -0.924. The library-wide correlation was cross-film confounding —
grainy old films are both starved and shallow-sloped for separate reasons.
DO NOT REVIVE IT. And note the general lesson: a correlation measured ACROSS the
library is not evidence about a mechanism WITHIN a film. Only a controlled
experiment separates them, and one costs ~10 minutes per film.

12. HOW THE SCORE IS ACTED ON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Audit tab is where BPP+ becomes decisions. Precision is the stated goal:
neither false positives (flagging good copies) nor false negatives (missing bad
ones).

12.1 OVER-SUPPLY (the Disk section)
    OVERSUPPLY_R = 2            flag only past 2x transparent
    OVERSUPPLY_MIN_GB = 2       below this it is not worth a human's attention
    overBytes = bytes * (1 - OVERSUPPLY_R / R)
    SEASON_DISAGREE_MAX = 0.25  seasons whose episodes disagree are ineligible
Rows are admitted on band OR over-supply and sorted by overBytes.

*** A CORRECTION WORTH KEEPING: over-supply is measured above the 2x FLOOR, not
above transparency. An early report described a Westworld swap as "cutting into
the picture" citing 0.24 GB of surplus; the real surplus above transparency was
8.0 GB (44%). BPP+ already IS a surplus measure — a candidate at BPP+ 100 lands
exactly on the transparency anchor, not below it. The genuine objection to that
swap was generation loss, not bit starvation. ***

12.2 UPGRADE ELIGIBILITY
    upgradeEligible = short || unprobed || reenc || bppPlus < UPGRADE_BPP_PLUS_MAX
Serve-time filtering in rankCands() applies the gates, which avoids bumping
VERDICT_VERSION and re-verifying the whole library for a filter change.

12.2a THE DENOMINATOR CANCELS WITHIN A FILM — A FEATURE, STATE IT      [SOLID]
Every candidate for a row is scored with THAT ROW'S key, so it gets the row's
target: rescoreCand() in audit.js sets bppPlus = bppIndex(c.bpp, row.key), and the
upgrade gain compares bppIndex(candBpp, row.key) against bppIndex(row.bpp,
row.key). Same key, same target, so the target DIVIDES OUT:

    cndPlus / curPlus  ==  sqrt(candBpp / rowBpp)

WITHIN ONE FILM, THE RANKING IS PURE bpp (x codec x resolution). Complexity,
biasFactor, HEADROOM_TARGET, cxEff — the entire per-film apparatus — cannot move
which release wins. It moves the LABEL and the band, not the choice.

THIS IS GOOD, AND IT IS THE STRONGEST PROPERTY OF THE DESIGN: a wrong denominator
CANNOT corrupt a same-film decision. A film measured badly gets a wrong-looking
number beside a still-correct pick. It also means the precision work in 4.4 buys
label quality, not decision quality, within a film.

WHERE THE DENOMINATOR DOES DECIDE: ACROSS films — "is my copy of this adequate for
its own content", which is what the band, the audit's admission rule and the
upgrade queue's ordering all rest on. Both statements are true and the document
used to assert only the second, which oversold per-film precision as
decision-critical everywhere. Found by adversarial review, 2026-08-19.

14. TRAPS FOR ANY AGENT WORKING ON THIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HOST ffmpeg IS 4.2 AND HAS NO blockdetect/blurdetect — it fails SILENTLY and
   yields empty columns. Always run detector work via `docker exec controller`
   (ffmpeg 5.1).
2. `-vf` IS AN OUTPUT OPTION and must precede the output URL or ffmpeg emits
   nothing.
3. I-FRAMES ARE INSERTED AT SCENE CUTS, so a naive iRatio reports "compression"
   on any clip containing one. Without the cut guard, Spirited Away (hand-drawn,
   no grain, judged clean) scored 2.23 "COMPRESSION".
4. The frame-type ffprobe interval must cover EVERY decoded frame, or later
   frames read as `?` and a keyframe inside the window is silently averaged into
   the P/B floor.
5. killProbe() SENDS SIGKILL, which cannot be trapped — a script-side cleanup
   handler cannot fix the zombie leak. Reaping at PID 1 (`init: true`) is the
   only place it can be solved.
6. THIS BOX HAS 4 CORES. Run encode experiments strictly sequentially.
7. A SINGLE top/docker stats/iostat SAMPLE LIES. Sample properly before claiming
   a process is eating the box.
8. SUBJECTIVE TESTING HAS TWO TRAPS, AND THE SECOND ONE ALREADY COST US THE ONLY
   DATASET (11.1).
   (a) JUDGE ONLY FILMS BRENNAN HAS WATCHED. A first-time viewer cannot separate
       "this encode looks wrong" from "this film always looked like that". The
       first blind list was mostly unseen films and had to be rebuilt from
       Jellyfin's played list.
   (b) NEVER ASK FOR A VERDICT ON A SINGLE UNPAIRED CLIP. Grain and compression
       noise are not separable by eye that way — the 2026-08-13 test asked
       "artifacts: yes/no" and got back a grain rating, and the whole experiment
       had to be retracted. Show TWO versions of the SAME SCENE and ask which is
       worse. Grain is then identical across the pair by construction, so only
       compression can move the answer. Forced-choice also kills the tie-heavy
       Spearman that made n=11 look more informative than it was.
9. nextUnit() ONLY PICKS FRESH OR STALE UNITS. A film already measured from the
   file still on disk is NEVER selected again. This is the structural hole that
   stranded audio measurement for four silent days while the library sat at
   99.9% "measured". runAudioBackfill() and nextImprecise() exist to reach
   already-measured units; do not fold them back into nextUnit(), which would
   make it never return null and starve everything behind it.
10. The unit key ('mv:<radarrId>' / 'tv:<sonarrId>:<season>') is built
    independently by audit.js and probe.js from the same *arr ids. That
    agreement is load-bearing and must not drift.
11. `R` IS NOT THE RATIO BPP+ DIVIDES BY, and treating it as one produces a
    convincing false alarm. R divides by the UNCORRECTED probe rate; BPP+ divides
    by the CORRECTED target (cxEff * headroom), so R overstates it by roughly
    biasFactor. MEASURED across 898 movies (2026-08-20):
        bppPlus / (100*sqrt(R))          median 0.847
        bppPlus / (100*sqrt(bpp/target)) median 1.000 — an identity, as it must be
    Plotting the library against R therefore puts EVERY film below the
    100*sqrt(R) curve and looks exactly like the model failing to fit its own
    data. bpp-lab's Quality curve view did this until 2026-08-18. Use
    `bpp/target` from /api/probe/dataset.

    AND R ITSELF IS NOT src/probe. Two corrections sit between them, and skipping
    either has burned an analysis already:
      (a) CODEC NORMALISATION — the probe always encodes x265, so an h264 source
          is divided by 1.6. R = srcBitrate / (probeBitrate * eff). Omitting it
          inflates every h264 row by 1.6x and makes starved copies look supplied.
      (b) AUDIO — `srcBitrate` is the container total on 470 of 1044 units
          (`srcBasis: 'container'`); R has that audio subtracted and `rawR` does
          not (5.2a). Median container audio share is 9.8%, max ~47%.
    The dataset carries `R` (corrected, what scoring uses), `rawR` and `srcBasis`
    so an offline analysis can reproduce any of the three deliberately.
12. AN EXPERIMENT THAT USES FEWER SAMPLES THAN THE NIGHTLY PROBE CANNOT BE
    COMPARED TO IT ABSOLUTELY (5.1). Take RATIOS within a film, where scene
    selection cancels, and anchor any absolute axis to the live probe.

   resolution, and record "visible on the projector?" as a SEPARATE binary — the
   real decision is whether an upgrade changes what Brennan sees in the room.
   This changes meaning when the 1080p projector arrives.

5. CAPTURE A GRAIN FIGURE — additive, time-sensitive.
       grainShare = 1 - complexity_denoised / complexity_raw
   One extra encode per unit, splitting the denominator into content cost and
   grain cost. Add to the probe entry AND to PRIOR_FIELDS.
   *** READ 11.3 FIRST: A METRIC OF THIS NAME HAS ALREADY BEEN FALSIFIED. *** What
   differs is only the method — an explicit denoise pass (hqdn3d / nlmeans)
   re-encoded and differenced, rather than a `-tune grain` bitrate delta. Real
   methodological difference, NOT a reason to assume it works: run the same
   degeneracy tests (CGI vs film stock, independence from mean luma) first.
   WHY TIME-SENSITIVE: superseded copies are deleted at swap time, so a pair only
   holds what PRIOR_FIELDS captured when banked. 113 banked today, and every
   upgrade before instrumentation banks a pair without it.
   INSURANCE, NOT A FIX: justified by being cheap, purely additive (changes no
   score) and hard to recover later — not by "grain is the problem", which is not
   established (section 9). PRIOR ART: AV1 film-grain synthesis denoises, encodes
   and re-synthesizes grain from parameters for exactly this reason (~50% savings
   on heavy-grain sequences).

6. THE QUALITY AXIS — BLOCKED ON libvmaf. DO NOT SUBSTITUTE SSIM (11.2).
   The ladder is the RATE half only; a real knee needs a perceptual y-axis. The
   controller's Debian ffmpeg 5.1 has psnr, ssim and vmafmotion but NOT libvmaf,
   and jellyfin-ffmpeg 7.1.4 does not either. THE PATH: a SECOND, static ffmpeg
   built with libvmaf for the ladder ONLY — it must NOT replace the Debian ffmpeg
   the nightly probe uses, because a different x265 build makes all 1031 existing
   complexity measurements incomparable.
   THE PRESCRIBED FIX FOR GRAIN, which is Netflix's own AV1 pipeline design:
   DENOISE FIRST, then encode, then measure on the denoised signal (encoding
   artifacts are masked by grain amplitude anyway). So this is denoise -> ladder ->
   VMAF, sharing its denoise pass with item 5 — not "install libvmaf and plot it".
   WORTH HAVING IT FOR: CAMBI, a banding detector that is NOT pixelwise and so
   does not share the grain failure (the survey's verdict was "USE THIS"); the
   DOWNGRADE curve on clean content, exactly what disk reclamation needs ("shrink
   this to 2 GB and quality goes to X"); and one cheap discriminating test — does
   a VMAF-vs-our-copy curve bend on a CLEAN film and refuse to bend on a grainy
   one?
   NOT WORTH DOING: using VMAF to test whether fixed CRF is fixed quality (open
   question 4). Grainy films score lower VMAF at the same CRF and the fixed-CRF
   residual is ITSELF believed to track grain, so the test would measure VMAF's
   grain bias and report it as an encoder property.

7. THEN CONSIDER SCORE CHANGES. All want item 4 in hand first: moving
   HEADROOM_TARGET off 1.0; a gridRatio artifact term; a grainShare term, or
   grainShare as a GATE rather than a term (the Blair Witch class is "bits cannot
   help", a gate-shaped fact); re-examining the sqrt exponent.
   THE SHAPE OF THE ANSWER IS ONE NUMBER (1.2d): a single sortable score plus a
   short explanatory line, e.g.
       87 — GREAT   compression: strong · grain: normal · artifacts: low ·
                    upgrade potential: low
   those annotations being the gates made legible — compression ~ R, grain ~
   grainShare, artifacts ~ gridRatio, upgrade potential ~ source ceiling and
   biasFactor. Three of the four do not exist and none can be weighted without
   item 4. NOT a multidimensional dashboard.

SET ASIDE, so they are not re-proposed cold:
  X. LIBRARY-WIDE MULTI-CRF LADDERS — the headline recommendation of an external
     literature review, answered by the pilot: no knees, slope varies 2x but is
     not an input to the score, and the slope hypothesis is dead (11.5). ~170 h of
     encoding for a constant. Do not build the cron.
  Y. PROBING AT 720p to match the projector — right for the display we had,
     obsolete before it was tried. PROBE_REFERENCE_WIDTH stays 1920, and the
     survey's "measure at 720p, effectively a free grain filter" dies with it.

16.B HOW TO ADOPT A NEW MEASUREMENT INTO BPP+ — THE ORDER, AND WHY
Any pending measurement (grainShare, gridRatio, CAMBI, VMAF) comes through this
gate. The steps are ordered by what makes the next one safe.

1. MEASURE IT ON A PILOT, NOT THE LIBRARY. A stratified subset answers "does this
   vary with content" for a few hours of encoding. The library-wide version costs
   ~170 h and is justified only once the answer is yes.
2. DECIDE WHETHER IT IS EVEN A PER-FILM QUANTITY. If the pilot shows it is
   uniform, it is a CONSTANT, not a measurement: fix the constant, delete the
   experiment, do NOT build a cron. A nightly job re-measuring a constant is pure
   cost. This is the most likely outcome and the easiest to miss, because building
   the cron feels like progress. (Ladder slope is the worked example: it varies,
   and it still does not earn a cron.)
3. VALIDATE BEFORE WEIGHTING. *** NOT OPTIONAL, AND THE ONE THAT WAS SKIPPED. ***
   blockMean was measured, stored, correlated, given a plausible mechanism and
   nearly shipped inside wBPP+ — and the experiment that appeared to settle it was
   retracted wholesale (11.1). A term needs a WEIGHT, a weight needs a fit, a fit
   needs a valid label. There is none in this library today. Until there is, a new
   measurement may be STORED and DISPLAYED but must not enter the score.
4. USE PARTIAL DATA WHERE YOU HAVE IT. Brennan, 2026-08-18: "I prefer accuracy. If
   only part of the library has data for something, we use it when possible. Just
   like audio." Measured audio was applied per-film as coverage grew from 8 films
   to 1031; waiting for full coverage would only have kept everyone wrong longer.
   THE DISTINCTION THAT MATTERS is whether the MISSING-DATA DEFAULT IS BIASED, not
   merely less precise:
     - Default unbiased (just noisier): partial application strictly reduces total
       error. Apply it. This is the audio case.
     - Default biased: partial application still reduces error for the films it
       reaches but OPENS A SYSTEMATIC GAP between the two groups. Apply it anyway,
       SAY SO ON THE ROW, and do not read a ranking across the two groups as one
       scale.
   (An earlier edition argued the opposite — that partial coverage makes the index
   "measure its own coverage" — and was wrong; the lesson from the incident behind
   that rule is to MARK the difference, not withhold the fix.)
   THIS REQUIRES the basis to be visible: `cxBasis` and the bpp+* mark already do
   it, and any new input needs the same.
5. ONLY THEN: CRON, UI, PLAYGROUND, in that order. And PROBE_VERSION must NOT be
   bumped for a scoring change — it invalidates the cache and throws away weeks of
   nights. It is for changes to how a measurement is TAKEN.
6. VERSION THE SCORE ITSELF. Changing the formula makes every historical number
   incomparable: cached audit verdicts, banked pairs, the calibration anchor, every
   figure in this document. Decide at adoption time whether old values are
   migrated, recomputed or discarded — and record which, because "the numbers
   changed and nobody wrote down why" is unrecoverable a month later.
7. UPDATE THIS DOCUMENT IN THE SAME CHANGE (section 0).

ON RUNNING THE CRONS HARD: the mechanism exists and it is the MANUAL SESSION
(/api/probe/session/start), which waives the 01:00-06:00 schedule while keeping
every safety gate — Movie Mode, someone watching, the tick lock. Brennan drives it
through the daytime at his discretion; that is the intended use. What should NOT
be done casually is widening the unattended NIGHT budget, the thermal envelope of
a 4-core NUC that also serves video, inside which an uncapped nightly task has
already run away once (the trickplay incident). A human choosing to run it now is
different from the box deciding to. And generating data faster only helps once
step 3 is passed: unweighted data accumulates at any speed and improves nothing.

EXTERNAL, READ IN THIS ORDER. Priority revised 2026-08-18: P.1204.3 and the
Google/YouTube paper sit AHEAD of the Durbha and Netflix ladder papers, because
they attack the no-reference / already-compressed problem we actually have.
Items 1 and 2 were MISSING from the earlier literature review; item 7 was
OVER-WEIGHTED in it.

  1. ITU-T P.1204.3 — bitstream-based NO-REFERENCE video quality model.
     https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9123110/
     https://github.com/Telecommunication-Telemedia-Assessment/bitstream_based_models
     VERDICT: THE CLOSEST EXISTING THING TO WHAT BPP+ IS TRYING TO BE. Predicts
     subjective quality from the bitstream alone (H.264 / HEVC / VP9), no
     reference, trained and validated against ~5,000 lab-tested sequences, with
     model variants for TV / PC / tablet / phone. Open-source parser available.
     CAVEAT: trained on streaming-ladder encodes of CLEAN sources; its corpus has
     essentially no film grain, so pointed at a grainy 1976 Bluray rip the likely
     failure is that it reads grain as degradation — the exact error we are trying
     to escape. Read it for METHOD and per-device framing; do not integrate before
     the label instrument exists (16.A.4).
  2. Google / YouTube — Video transcoding optimization based on input perceptual
     quality (2020).
     https://research.google/pubs/video-transcoding-optimization-based-on-input-perceptual-quality/
     VERDICT: THE ONLY PAPER HERE WHOSE PROBLEM IS OURS. It assumes the input is
     ALREADY IMPERFECT and asks how much further degradation it can tolerate,
     rather than assuming a pristine master. Directly analogous to the
     source-pinning correction (section 5).
  3. Norkin & Birkbeck — Film Grain Synthesis for AV1 (DCC 2018).
     https://norkin.org/pdf/DCC_2018_AV1_film_grain.pdf
     VERDICT: USEFUL, AND THE REASON 16.A.5 IS SHAPED AS IT IS. AV1 denoises,
     encodes and re-synthesizes grain from parameters — normatively — because
     grain cost and content cost are separable and grain is enormously expensive
     to reproduce literally. Savings up to ~50% on heavy-grain sequences. Also the
     standard warning that aggressive denoising takes detail with the grain, which
     is the Heat problem (9.4).
  4. Netflix — Per-Title Encode Optimization (2015).
     https://netflixtechblog.com/per-title-encode-optimization-7e99442b62a2
     VERDICT: FOUNDATIONAL FOR THE PER-FILM DENOMINATOR, and the direct
     justification for killing flat BPP_TARGET = 0.13 (4.1). They measure MULTIPLE
     quality points per title and take a convex hull; they do not treat one QP as
     transparency. Different ladders per device class, the same reasoning as our
     1080p ceiling. LIMIT: THEY ENCODE A PRISTINE MASTER (constraint 1.2b). Do not
     import the methodology wholesale.
  5. MainConcept — Constant Target Quality encoding driven by perceptual fidelity.
     https://blog.mainconcept.com/constant-target-quality-encoding-driven-by-perceptual-fidelity
     VERDICT: ONE USEFUL NUMBER. Fixed CRF is not fixed quality — measured spread
     ~3-sigma 10 VMAF against ~2.6 for quality-targeted encoding. The evidence
     behind the caveat in section 4 and open question 4.
  6. Netflix — Optimized Shot-Based Encodes (2018), and VMAF itself.
     https://medium.com/netflix-techblog/optimized-shot-based-encodes-now-streaming-4b9464204830
     https://github.com/Netflix/vmaf
     VERDICT: PARTLY USEFUL. Good for what a real rate-quality trajectory looks
     like, and for CAMBI as an example of a distortion-SPECIFIC detector kept
     separate from the headline score — the same architecture as our gates.
     VMAF itself is FULL-REFERENCE and cannot replace R (1.2a), and its grain
     failure is documented in 11.2. Shot-level optimization is not worth it for a
     ~1,000-title home library.
  7. Durbha & Bovik — per-shot bitrate ladders from VIF features (2023 / 2025).
     https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07780      https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01932
     VERDICT: OVER-WEIGHTED IN THE EARLIER REVIEW; further from our problem than
     1 or 2. Predicting rate-quality structure from cheap source features instead
     of exhaustive encoding is the same instinct as our sampled probe, but it
     EXPLICITLY ASSUMES AN UNCOMPRESSED SOURCE (1.2b).
  8. FFmpeg blockdetect / blurdetect.
     https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html
     VERDICT: THE TOOLING BEHIND blockMean/blurMean, which are untested, not
     falsified (11.4). Note trap 1: the HOST ffmpeg is 4.2 and does not have them.

STILL MISSING FROM THIS LIST, and worth someone's search time: no-reference
quality work on ALREADY-COMPRESSED consumer video (the piracy-scene / archival
case rather than the streaming case); grain-aware no-reference metrics; and any
published treatment of "measure a re-encode's headroom without a master", which
is precisely the source-pinning problem and which we have so far solved only by
experiment (5.1).
