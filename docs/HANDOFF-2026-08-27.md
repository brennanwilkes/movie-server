## 0. UPDATE — 2026-08-28 (read this before anything below it)

Seven results landed overnight. Three of them **contradict statements made elsewhere in this file**, so
where they disagree, this section wins. Full tables in `docs/BPP-PLUS-EVIDENCE.txt` (created
2026-08-28 — it did not exist before, and the core doc had 11 dead `→ E.n` links).

**1. λ is an identity, and the constants in it are not free. → E.6**
`λ = ln(1+anchor)/dPdGen`, but the anchor was never measured directly — it came from the crossed
ladder as `exp(B/A)−1`. So `ln(1+anchor) = B/A` and **λ = 1/A, the reciprocal of the bits-response
slope.** The generation step enters, cancels, and leaves. Check: A=0.6555 → 1/A=1.526 vs shipped
1.519. **This caught a 2.5× error with the substitution already written down** — the end-to-end test
measured a real generation at 2.48× the ladder step, and dropping that into the denominator alone
would have scaled every adjustment by 0.40× on arithmetic rather than on a finding. A better
generation number belongs in the **anchor**, not in λ.

**2. The end-to-end test passed — the chain transmits. → E.7.1**
`dP = 0.4971 ± 0.0459`, sign **23/23**, denoise arm 23/23. First test of the *rule* rather than the
detectors; the signal survives all four steps between detectors and score. Note the built-in limit:
both copies use the same clips, so this isolates *calibration* and says nothing about ordering.

**3. THE ANCHOR IS CLOSED — 49% of the "generation" was never a generation, and λ survives. → E.7**
Both legs on identical clips (n=24, direction checks 24/24 and 24/24) gave `A=0.4313`, `B=0.4886`,
so **anchor = 210%** — 5.3× outside the published bound, and **refused** on that basis. Diagnosis:
gen0 is a segment of a film encoded with **2-pass** rate control (721/803 library files are
`rc=2pass`), while gen1 is a 2-second clip at **1-pass ABR** forced to the film's *average* bitrate.
Leg A is clip-vs-clip so all of that cancels there and contaminates only B.

**Settled by measuring gen1→gen2** (both sides clip encodes, identical recipe), COMPLETE at n=24:
`B2 = 0.1547`, sign **24/24**, ratio **0.51×** against a pre-registered 0.303 if the step had been
clean. So `B_clean = 0.2495` and **contamination = 0.2391, i.e. 49% of the measured step.**

**The external bound was also the wrong one.** BD-rate compares *encoders at equal quality*; ours is
a transcode *at matched bitrate*. In CRF points, using our own −17.4%/CRF: shipped 1.59, corrected
1.99, same-clip 3.03, naive 5.93. A generation costing ~2 CRF is ordinary; 6 CRF is not.

**And because λ = 1/A, the anchor is a reported quantity that changes nothing downstream.** The rule
moves only if A does. Tested rather than assumed (`bits-slope-flattening.mjs`, n=24): more damaged
films show flatter slopes, `|A|` vs P-at-gen0 **rho −0.479 ± 0.218**, with the circular variant
*weaker* (−0.282) and complexity flat (+0.022) — saturation, not artifact, not masking. So the
same-clip A is measured at an already-degraded operating point and understates the field slope; the
crossed-ladder A = 0.6555 is the better estimate. **λ stays ~1.52. NO CHANGE TO THE SHIPPED RULE** —
vindicated by measurement, not retained by inertia. Honest anchor moves 35.6% → ~46% (2.0 CRF).

**4. DETAIL LOSS: blur is the wrong COORDINATE, not a weak detector. → E.8**
blur and grain are pure `log(bits × content)` functions (b_bpp = b_cx at 0.1 SE; forcing onto that
axis loses *nothing*, onto the adequacy axis loses *everything*). BPP+ is `log(bits ÷ content)`.
**Orthogonal by construction** — no data can fix a coordinate. This kills them for *calibration* but
not for P, which is a residual with that structure already removed. The one candidate that passes the
two-direction test is **`perPoint = d log(bitrate)/d CRF`**, already sitting unexamined in
`data/starve-experiment.json`: separation **6.1 SE**, 3.5× transverse, 8/8 monotone, 7/8 codec-crossed,
circularity ruled out against the controlled variable (t=14.1). Specificity leg not yet run.

**5. `provShare` is measured, not assumed: 0.109 floor (was 0.075). → §E.4, task 87**
Bitstream encoder fingerprint recovered on **85.7%** of the library (67 x264 builds) — correcting
§11.50, which claimed no finer provenance marker exists. Encoder identity explains adj R² **0.109** of
P, p=0.0025 against a 400-shuffle null, and **year alone explains only 2.1%** so the date confound is
answered. Lower bound: unobserved provenance can only add.

**Also corrected:** the "77% show no visible banding" reading of §9.1 is **wrong** — it counts 4-clip
means against a *scene-level* threshold; scene-level it is 67% with ≥1 banded scene, reversing the
inference. And by the rule of three, 4 clips bound a rate only below 75%, so the p95 criterion needs
~60 clips/film (≈2 min of video). **0/60 films currently meet ≤5%.** → E.9

**6. THE CALIBRATION IS BIASED BY THE HOLE, AND THIS REFRAMES THE PROGRAMME. → E.10, E.10.1**
Raw bitrate beats BPP+ at predicting visible-scene prevalence on **all four** artifacts (cambi −0.569
vs −0.382). Sweeping the exponent in `log(bpp) − a·log(cxEff)` — BPP+ is a=1, raw bitrate a=0 — every
detector prefers a below 1, and CAMBI's bootstrap CI **[-0.50, 0.75] excludes 1**.

**But do not read that as "we need fewer bits."** Complexity does two opposing things: it raises the
bits a film needs (why a>0 exists) *and* it **masks** artifacts — grain hides banding and blocking.
Every artifact we can measure is a masked one, so every one pushes a downward. The effect that pushes
a *upward* is detail retention, and per result 4 we have **no instrument for it**. So the sweep is
computed from exactly the failure modes complexity conceals, with the one it aggravates absent — **it
would return a<1 even if a=1 were correct.** Corroborating tell: the optimum lands on the search
boundary at −0.50, and a negative exponent would mean complex films need *fewer* bits, which is
absurd.

The standing conclusion: the artifacts do say most films need fewer bits than BPP+ assumes, **but
they would say that whether or not it is true.** This is now the strongest argument for building
`perPoint` — detail loss is not merely a missing feature, it is the missing **counterweight**, and
without it every calibration this project runs is biased toward "fewer bits needed." **Nothing was
changed in the shipped denominator.**

**7. FREE WIN, measured but not yet implemented. → E.11**
The nightly probe has been measuring block/blur at 8×4s all along (P uses the backfill's 4×2s).
Reliability, averaged over all 35 distinct 4/4 splits: **blur 0.664** vs the backfill's 0.404;
**block 0.910**. Blur is the worst-sampled detector in P and this roughly halves its noise. Bigger
still: the probe samples **different positions**, so sourcing block/blur there and cambi/grain from
the backfill means the four detectors no longer share one sampling — attacking the 52% structurally
rather than mitigating it. Re-run the clip-disjoint split-half after swapping, to confirm.

### *** 2026-08-28 18:40 — THE PROXY SHORTCUT IS CLOSED, AND IT WOULD HAVE LIED IN OUR FAVOUR ***

`cvqad-complexity.js` finished its first pass (48/48, 53 measurements) and was relaunched over the
GT that arrived mid-pass (21 remaining, 45 sequences). The 8 GT/proxy pairs now in hand settle
whether the pristine download was necessary. **It was, and not for the reason previously recorded.**

The old note said the proxy is bad on high-motion content and fine on clean content — true, but the
harmless reading of that is "a noisy scale error". It is not a scale error. **The proxy compresses
the complexity axis**: `log(proxy cx) = a + B·log(GT cx)`, **B = 0.757 ± 0.110** (2.21 SE below the
harmless B=1, r² 0.887). Fitting `a` on proxies scales it by **1/B = 1.322**, so **a true a=0.80
would have been reported as 1.057** — against a shipped a=1. The shortcut would have returned
*"our own external corpus confirms the current denominator"*, which is both the most reassuring
possible result and a pure artifact. Rank order survives (Spearman 0.929); only the scale dies.

External check ran before the SE, per §4: **B<1 is mechanically forced**, since a fixed-kbps rung
caps output bits and strips proportionally more from complex sources (B→0 in the limit). The rival
explanation — that the effect tracks rung bitrate, not complexity — was tested and is dead:
understatement correlates **+0.710** with GT complexity and **−0.141** with rung kbps, and the two
regressors are near-orthogonal (+0.123). Full write-up and stated limits in core **9.0i**.

**Standing instruction for #100: a proxy-fitted exponent must not be quoted, not even as a sanity
check** — its bias points at the incumbent value.

### *** 2026-08-28 18:45 — TASK #105 IS CLOSED: BLUR BELONGS, BUT ON THE WEAKER CONTRAST ***

**Unplanned harness validation:** at the shipped direction this script counts 85/20000 scaled against

**THE PART THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE VERDICT.** The two contrasts *disagree* about blur. The

encR² loss — **and the contrast blur wins on is the bits-dominated one (§11.44)**, the very weakness

footing than "all four load", and **it is the detector most likely to be overturned by #93 arm 2.**

### *** 2026-08-29 02:50 — THE EXPONENT WILL NOT CASCADE. The two workstreams are orthogonal ***

## 2. WHAT IS SOLID

### 2.1 Four provenance axes, all invisible to BPP+

Each holds bits and content EXACTLY constant and varies one thing with a known ordering. BPP+ scores
every row in these experiments identically, by construction.

| axis | design | result |
|---|---|---|
| **encoder** | x264 veryfast < medium < slower, one bitrate | rank corr **1.000, 8/8 films**; at n=20, **19/20 perfect** |
| **generations** | 1 vs 2 vs 3 encode passes, same final bitrate | block **10/10**, blur **10/10**, cambi 9/10 |
| **preprocess** | none/light/heavy `hqdn3d` before encoding | grain **9/10**, blur 9/10 |
| **bits** | the ladders, 8.3× bitrate span | cambi 3.182× swing |

```
artifacts = f(bits, content, ENCODER, SETTINGS, MASTER, GENERATIONS, PREPROCESSING)
```

BPP+ sees the first two ONLY. **Scripts:** `encoder-ladder.js`, `generation-test.js`,
`denoise-test.js`.

### 2.2 There is a common provenance factor IN THE FIELD — the result that unblocked everything

This needed no labels, no ladders and no controlled encodes; only the 782 units already measured.
After bits and content are regressed out, each residual is `provenance (SHARED) + that detector's
model error (SPECIFIC) + noise (SPECIFIC)`. So split the four detectors into halves sharing no
detector and correlate. **Detector-specific error cannot produce that correlation.**

```
split-half r, all three disjoint pairings   0.186  0.289  0.295    (SE 0.036 at n=782)
Spearman-Brown reliability of the full score               0.448
PC1 loadings   +0.523 cambi  +0.628 block  +0.148 blur  -0.557 grain
pre-registered re-encode signature is       (+, +, +, -)     ALL FOUR MATCH
corr(pre-registered direction, PC1)         0.951
PC1 explains 37.7%  (25% = pure noise)
```

The sign match is the discriminating check. A shared *content*-model error would produce a common
factor too, but it has no reason to produce **that** sign pattern. A further generation does.
**Script:** `provenance-factor.mjs`. **Doc:** §11.26.

### 2.3 It is not a codec fingerprint — the control that mattered most

First look showed hevc at mean P −0.968 against h264 +0.128, a full sigma. Dangerous twice over:
`blockdetect` may read hevc's 32×32 transforms as "clean" (§11.12 found exactly that effect), **and**
BPP+ already carries a codec term in R, so re-reading codec would double-count it. Two independent
controls, both pass:

```
subset to h264 only, n=688    split-half 0.219;  WEBRip +0.325 > WEB-DL +0.016 > Bluray -0.035
codec as a regressor, n=782   hevc P -> -0.000;  WEBRip +0.215, WEB-DL +0.217, Bluray -0.055
```

Subsetting and covarying agree. **Script:** `factor-codec-control.mjs`. **Doc:** §11.27.

### 2.4 The direction earns its place — a placebo test

If any arbitrary combination of the same four residuals separated WEB from Bluray equally well, the
physics story would be decoration. Varying **only** the direction, seeded so it cannot be re-rolled:

```
all 16 sign flips              pre-registered ranks 1st (gap 0.2711); next best 0.1926
20,000 random unit directions  only 9 reach a gap this large      (0.04%)
unconstrained best direction   gap 0.272 vs our 0.271;  cos with ours 0.969
```

**The pre-registered direction is essentially the empirical optimum, reached without fitting.**
**Script:** `direction-permutation.mjs`. **Doc:** §11.28.

### 2.5 Field contrasts, with a negative control

```
WEBRip vs rest        z 1.46   3/4 detectors agree   p 0.072
WEB vs Bluray         z 1.96   4/4 agree             p 0.025
720p vs 1080p (CTRL) z -0.71   2/4 agree             p 0.761   <- correctly does NOT fire
```

The control is doing real work: cambi fires hard there (+3.13) but the detectors point in
**incoherent** directions (blur −4.93). Coherence is the signal, not any single detector.
**Script:** `field-combined.mjs`. Uses Stouffer with the variance inflated 4 → 5.74 for the measured

### 2.6 Detector independence, thresholds, the relevance cliff

Residual correlations: cambi/block 0.25, cambi/blur 0.10, cambi/grain −0.16, block/blur 0.05,
block/grain −0.32, blur/grain 0.02. Four measurements, not one with four names.

mid, 9.0% of grainy. Restate it as **"grain blinds"**, not "smoothness helps". It also bounds the
upward direction: a clean reading rules out at most 28% of the ways a file can be bad.

### 4.1 The anchor: 33.1%, measured with no transfer assumption

```
   even shrunk by reliability 0.393 it is +-10.5 generations
```

Nothing in this library is twenty-seven generations from its master. **P's spread is not mostly
provenance, and reliability is the wrong shrinkage.**

Split-half reliability answers *"how much of P is a common factor across disjoint detector halves"*.
That is real — it is what proved the factor exists (§2.2) — but a common factor is **not** a
provenance factor. Shooting style, grade, lens choice, mastering house and deliberate softness are all
shared across detectors and survive the bits+content surface. **No download fixes any of them.**

The right quantity is the share of P's variance that observable provenance accounts for, bracketed at
both ends and neither end chosen:

```
FLOOR    ~0.10   between-source-label variance. A LOWER bound: labels are coarse, so two Blurays
                 differing by group, preset and generation land in the within-label term.
CEILING   0.149  physics — the most shrinkage consistent with the library spanning <= 4 generations
                 (4 * 0.1121 / 3). 
reliability 0.393 sits far outside both.
```

**The bound was consulted only after this was derived.** Shrinking P until the adjustments looked
plausible would be fitting to the bound and would destroy its value as a check. The sanity test that
decided it is generation-count plausibility, which never touches the bound: at the new shrinkage a
+3 sd film is **2.9 generations** from typical; at reliability it is **10.5**.

Result after both corrections: **5-95 ±15.3%, worst film ±29.4%, both PASS.** Library median 66 → 66,
35 of 1000 cross the 100 line, group ordering intact.

### 4.3 What this changes, and what it does not

Shrinkage is a **scalar on P**, so the *ordering* of films is completely unchanged and every piece of
evidence in §2.2-§2.5 — split-half, PC1 signs, codec control, negative control, permutation test — is
untouched.

**Say the direction of travel plainly: measuring the constants properly moved the adjustment
by less than the guesswork suggested, and in both directions.** ±17.8% → ±9.4% (shrinkage) → ±15.3%
(denominator). The panel's ordering is real and well evidenced; the share of
the library's spread that ordering can legitimately move is small, because most of what P varies over
is not provenance at all.

### 4.4 The single-direction design is validated, and the YIFY null is correct (§11.38)

Two questions were open after §4.2: is the ~0.10 shrinkage floor too conservative, and does P see
provenance the *source label* cannot? Tested with the sharpest instrument available — YIFY is the most
aggressive re-encoder in circulation and its releases are labelled **Bluray**, so YIFY-vs-other-Bluray
is a within-label provenance contrast.

```
YIFY n=94  mean P -0.058     other Bluray n=569  mean P -0.045
difference -0.013 +- 0.083   t = -0.16      A WELL-POWERED NULL
```

A competing between-group variance component said 0.125 (F=2.53) — **not credible**: six groups
qualify, four have n≤5, MSB carries 5 df, and it is driven by two three-film groups. The one group
with real n shows nothing. A pre-registered well-powered null beats a post-hoc marginal F.
**The floor stays.**

**All four provenance axes share one signature** — so the single-direction design is validated, not
undermined:

```
axis            cambi   block    blur   grain   cos with P's direction
generations     0.238   0.721   0.640  -0.116        0.850
encoder preset  0.338   0.879   0.268  -0.204        0.673
preprocessing   0.749   0.258   0.338  -0.509        0.685
bits            0.719   0.514   0.358  -0.302        0.692
```

**And the YIFY null is correct behaviour, not a failure:**

```
group        n   med bpp   med BPP+   med cxEff   mean P
YIFY        94    0.0519         55       0.156     -0.058
other Blu  569    0.0599         63       0.154     -0.045
```

Same complexity, 13% less bitrate, and BPP+ **already** scores them 13% lower for exactly that. P asks
"worse than its *bits* predict" and the honest answer is no — YIFY are competent x264 encodes at low
bitrate, not incompetent ones. P is orthogonal to bits by construction and correctly declines to
double-penalise a file whose only sin is being small. **Had it fired, that would have been the
double-count.**

Two things worth carrying forward:
- **A limitation:** `cos(preprocessing, bits) = 0.95`. The panel cannot tell *denoised* from
  *starved*. That doesn't break P — bits are residualised out, so what remains on that axis is the
  denoising — but no future construction should claim it distinguishes them.
- **A method warning:** my first run of this had the denoise axis inverted (`denoise-test.js` uses
  rank 3 = none, rank 1 = heavy, so rank *decreases* with denoising). It reported cos −0.685 and
  appeared to confirm a hypothesis I already believed. It was caught only because the grain component
  came out **positive** — denoising appearing to *add* grain, which cannot happen. **Check the sign of
  a component you understand physically before believing a cosine you don't.**

### 4.5 A 25% coverage hole, and it is not random (§11.31)

```
controller library                1048 units with a BPP+
measured for all four artifacts    782   (74.6%)
MISSING                            266   (25.4%)  <- 100% BLURAY MOVIES
have P    median BPP+ 65   cx 0.143   bpp 0.0579
missing   median BPP+ 73   cx 0.166   bpp 0.0747
```

Every WEBRip, WEB-DL and HDTV unit is covered; Bluray is 30.4% short, and the missing quarter is
systematically higher-bitrate, higher-complexity and higher-scoring.

**Cause:** `artifact-backfill.js` selected on `!r.cambi` — "units with no banding reading". Correct
when the goal was enriching the thin WEBRip side; it silently excluded everything the nightly banding
probe had already visited.

**What it threatens:** the anchor `gap = mean P(WEB) − mean P(Bluray)` had one side computed on a
non-random 70% of its group, *and* the residual surface is fitted on the same truncated sample so it
extrapolates at the high-bitrate end. Both routes bias the gap → λ → every adjustment size → §11.30's
69.7%.

**What it does NOT threaten:** the split-half factor (§2.2), the codec control (§2.3) and the
permutation test (§2.4) are all *within-sample* properties. A biased sample cannot manufacture a
common factor across disjoint detector pairs, nor make a pre-registered direction beat 19,991 of
20,000 random ones.

**Fixed:** `--all` flag added with the reason recorded at the call site. `day4.sh` measures the 266
after the anchor run, re-exports, and re-runs everything into `logs/full-coverage-recheck.log`.

**The general lesson, third time a version of it has bitten this project:** a filter written for one
experiment's purpose becomes a silent population definition for every later analysis that reuses its
output. **State the population in the output, not just the filter in the code.**
### 4.6 FULL COVERAGE — seven checks pass, one FAILS (§11.40)

The 266 missing Blurays were measured; coverage is now **1048, the entire library**.
`scripts/coverage-recheck.mjs` fixed the baseline *and the tolerances* before the run, splitting
them into structural claims (must not move) and population quantities (may).

```
PASS  PC1 sign pattern still 4/4
PASS  PC1 vs physics direction      corr 0.957  (baseline 0.951)
PASS  PC1 explains 36.1%            (baseline 37.7%, noise floor 25%)
PASS  split-half median r 0.272     (baseline 0.289, SE 0.036)
PASS  generation ordering holds within h264
PASS  720p negative control silent  z -0.98
PASS  direction ranks 1 of 16 sign flips
FAIL  random directions reaching our gap: 537/20000  (baseline 9; threshold 400)

population: gap 0.271 -> 0.209 (t 2.01), provenance fraction 0.0115 -> 0.0056,
            shrink floor 0.107 -> 0.075, adjustment 5-95 ±13.5% (bound still PASSES)
```

**The cause is a detector floor, diagnosed not explained away.** P uses `log(cambi)`, and cambi is
bounded below with a noise floor:

```
lowest 25% of cambi readings   med 0.120   mean |z_cambi| 1.196
middle 50%                     med 1.024   mean |z_cambi| 0.494
library-wide: 7.1% read cambi < 0.05
Bluray high-bpp tercile: 20.1% below 0.05 (vs 0.3% low-bpp), sd z_cambi 1.288 vs 0.732
```

The added units are high-bitrate Blurays sitting near that floor, so the backfill injected
log-of-near-zero noise wholesale — inflating P's variance (halving the provenance share, which has
total variance in its denominator), diluting the gap, and lowering the bar random directions had to
clear. **Same mechanism as §11.35, third appearance.**

**That diagnosis turned out to be WRONG — see §11.41.** `cambi-floor.js` measured it and found *no
floor*: `sd/level ≈ 1` at every level, which means the noise is **multiplicative** and `log` is the
correct transform after all. The real finding is bigger:

```
sd(log cambi), single 2s read     1.68   (roughly constant across levels)
SE of a 4-clip mean (backfill)    0.84
library residual sd for cambi    ~1.3
=> ~42% of cambi's residual variance is pure SCENE-SAMPLING noise
```

Not instrument error — scene heterogeneity. One film's 12 clips span 0.11 to 12.10. This explains the
low split-half, the small provenance fraction, the heavy shrinkage, and why every effect here has
been modest. **The shrinkage is not pessimism; it is the correct response to a noisy measurement.**

**The payoff curve is now MEASURED** (`clip-reliability.js`, 60 units × 12 clips, within-unit design;
§11.42):

```
      k     cambi    block     blur    grain
      1     0.528    0.595    0.124    0.766
      4     0.798    0.849    0.404    0.937   <- the library's sampling depth
   ->12     0.904    0.935    0.709    0.977
   ->26     0.953    0.969    0.841    0.989
```

**GRAIN IS THE MOST RELIABLE DETECTOR**, not the second-worst — an interim read at 31 units said 0.629
and was wrong by 0.31. *Do not act on a reliability estimate from a partial run; this document did,
and the section was rewritten.* The ordering is physically sensible and worth carrying forward:
**detectors differ in how LOCAL their artifact is.** Grain is a global texture property so any two
seconds resemble any other; banding exists only where a scene has smooth gradients; blocking only
where the encoder ran short. That is what sets sampling reliability.

**What more clips buy**, as a multiplier on the provenance fraction: cambi ×1.19 @12 / ×1.26 @26,
block ×1.13 / ×1.17, blur ×1.58 / ×1.87, grain ×1.05 / ×1.06. A 12-clip re-measure of 1048 units is
**~33 hours for roughly 10% more shrinkage** — real but small, which is exactly what the curve was
measured to establish before spending the hours.

**Reliability weighting: tested, NOT adopted.** It pulls two ways — split-half 0.377→0.409, provFrac
0.0056→0.0062, and the 720p control improves markedly (−1.82→−0.98) — but **the gap and its t fall**
(0.209→0.198, t 2.01→1.90). The gap is what the calibration runs through and §4.6's failure was
already about the gap weakening. Wrong trade. **Equal weighting stays.**

**Blur is noisy but informative — settled.** Dropping it gives the *highest* split-half of all (0.432)
while the gap falls to 0.188: the self-agreement trap made visible, since the remaining detectors are
the most mutually correlated. Blur was written off in §11.20 for correlating with nothing observable;
at reliability 0.449 those correlations run at ~67% of true size, and removing it *costs the gap*.
**It stays in the composite.**

**WHY IT WEAKENED — diagnosed (§11.44).** The statistic is "how many random directions reach *our*
gap", so a shrinking gap lowers the bar. Both shrank, but ours more:

```
            our gap   random p95   margin
n=782        0.2711      0.241      1.125x
n=1048       0.2088      0.198      1.054x

best direction found by search:
  n=782    0.67 cambi  0.52 block  0.38 blur  -0.37 grain   cos with ours 0.969
  n=1048   0.83 cambi  0.37 block  0.43 blur  -0.03 grain   cos with ours 0.825
```

**Grain's component collapsed.** It moves in the right direction (WEB has less retained grain) but
contributes +0.069 of the gap against cambi's +0.282, while carrying equal weight and equal noise.

A tempting explanation — that grain tracks *film stock* rather than provenance — was **tested and
refuted**: corr(z, year) is 0.146 / 0.045 / 0.044 / **−0.032**, and grain is the cleanest of the four.

The real reason was already in §11.38's measured axis directions: **the generations axis itself has
only a small grain component (−0.116); grain is the PREPROCESSING detector (−0.509).** WEB-vs-Bluray
is primarily a *generations* contrast, so grain should contribute little to this particular gap while
remaining right for a different axis. **The permutation test measures how special a direction is at
separating ONE contrast** — a direction spending a quarter of itself on a detector aligned elsewhere
looks less special on it.

> **DO NOT DOWN-WEIGHT GRAIN ON THIS.** The direction is pre-registered from physics. Refitting it
> because the data prefers something else *on the same contrast the test uses* is precisely the
> circularity §2.4 exists to prevent — the permutation result would then be guaranteed and meaningless.
> The failure stands as recorded, with its cause understood.

**The structural gap this exposes:** the framework has **four validated axes but only one observable
field contrast** to test them against. A preprocessing-dominated contrast would settle it; the library
has no clean one.

**PARTLY ANSWERED (§11.48): run the permutation on the CONTROLLED axes instead.** Provenance there is
known and varied on purpose, so the single-field-contrast limit does not apply. The tautology to avoid
is that on any *single* axis the best direction is trivially that axis's own — so the statistic is the
**worst of three axes**, which punishes a direction for missing any one:

```
pre-registered direction   generations 6.84   preprocessing 2.19   encoder 3.41   WORST 2.19
20000 random directions    p50 -0.67   p95 1.59   max 2.63
matching or beating ours   145 of 20000 = 0.73%
```

**The direction beats 99.3% of arbitrary directions at spanning all three axes at once** — which is
the covering-set claim itself, tested where provenance is known.

Not to be overstated: it does **not** rescue §4.6's failure (that is about the field yardstick and
stands); ours is **not optimal** (best random reaches 2.63 vs our 2.19, and is blur-dominated — noisy
but evidently spanning well, consistent with removing blur costing the gap); and n is 10/10/20.

**And the composite is NOT blind to preprocessing — the opposite (§11.45).** From the controlled
denoise experiment, using data already on disk:

```
dP per denoise step   0.3353 P-sd   sign agreement 9/10
dP per GENERATION     0.1385 P-sd
ratio                 2.42x
```

**P is 2.4× MORE sensitive to a denoise step than to a generation.** So the detectors and the
equal-weight direction are sound; it really is the library contrast that lacks preprocessing
variation. This also reconciles with the YIFY null (§4.4): P is strongly sensitive to preprocessing,
so YIFY reading zero means YIFY does not heavily preprocess these files — it encodes at low bitrate
with a competent x264, which BPP+ already scores.

> **A NEW CAVEAT ON THE CALIBRATION.** λ converts P to bitrate through the **generation** rate, which
> assumes a film's P is generation-like. For a preprocessing-dominated film the same P means 2.42×
> fewer steps — and **the bitrate cost of a denoise step has never been measured.** The direction of
> that error is unknown and should not be guessed. `scripts/denoise-crossed.js` is measuring it now
> (leg A: bitrates with no denoising; leg B: denoise levels at one bitrate; same clips, so no transfer
> assumption). Analyse with `denoise-crossed-analyse.mjs` → the calibration can then be axis-aware.

**And the arithmetic makes it sharp (§11.46, pre-registered).** For the single λ to be correct on
*both* axes, a denoise step would have to satisfy `ln(1+X) = 2.065 × 0.3353`, i.e.
**X = 99.9% — a denoise step costing ~2× bitrate.** That is not a modelling choice; it is what the
shipped rule already implicitly asserts, and it was invisible until §11.45 measured dP per denoise
step. Prediction on record: **well below 99%**, since published practice treats denoising as a
bitrate-*saving* technique worth 10–30%, putting its quality cost in that order rather than at 2×.

If it lands at 30–70%, λ overstates preprocessing-dominated films by that ratio — but note **there is
currently no way to tell which axis a given library film sits on**, so the practical response is a
caveat, not a formula change. Finding a per-film axis discriminator is task #73 arriving from a new
direction.

**RESULT (§11.47): the denoise anchor is NOT a constant, and the question stays open.** 8/8 films
passed the direction checks, but the two statistics disagree by 30 points (51.3% vs 81.2%, against
0.9 in the generation run) and the per-film range is 9.8%–846%.

The design risk flagged mid-flight is confirmed and larger than expected:

```
corr(log base kbps, dP/dstep) = 0.946      (0.053 at 483 kbps -> 0.525 at 18614 kbps)
```

**"A denoise step" is not a fixed unit** — a starved encoder has already destroyed the grain, so the
denoiser has nothing left to remove. That also explains why §11.45 measured 0.335 on a different film
set and this run measured 0.177: the sets differ in bitrate, not physics. **And it kills the
comparison §11.45 set up** — λ_denoise is 2.34 with this run's dP/dstep but 1.24 with §11.45's, so the
apparent agreement with λ_gen was an artifact of the pairing.

> **A correction to a claim I made an hour earlier.** I reported that the *generation* anchor's ratio
> is bitrate-independent (corr 0.024) while its legs are not, and read that as the crossed design
> cancelling the dependence. **That was over-read.** Dropping The Creator (leg-A slope −0.068, flagged
> fragile in both runs) the correlations rise to 0.273 (generation) and 0.406 (denoise) — and at n=8
> the SE of a correlation is ~0.45. Every one of those is within noise of zero and of each other. Only
> the 0.946 on `dP/dstep` is large enough to be real at this n.

**What stands:** dP/dstep is strongly bitrate-dependent, so a denoise step is not a calibration unit.
**What is NOT shown:** that the ratio cancels bitrate on either axis, or that λ does or doesn't differ
between them — both unmeasurable at n=8.

**Consequence: keep the generation-based λ.** It rests on a run whose two statistics converged and
whose spread was an order of magnitude tighter. The axis-awareness question from §11.45 is **open, not
answered**, and given the whole adjustment is ±13.5% at 5-95, it is not obviously worth the films it
would take to settle.

**What it does and does not mean.** It does *not* refute the factor: split-half, PC1 signs, codec
control and negative control all survived, and the direction still ranks first of sixteen. 537/20000
is still p = 0.027. What it means is the **direction evidence is materially weaker at full coverage**
than on the truncated sample, for a reason with a principled fix.

**A deliberate deviation from the pre-registration, recorded as such.** The registered response was
"take the rule off the page's default until understood." **That was not done.** The only alternative
default is the legacy confidence-weighted rule, which this same document records as producing
m = 1e−58 and containing two cut-offs — switching to a rule already known to be worse would mislead
more, not less. Instead the page carries a prominent warning naming the failed check and its cause.
The *substance* (not presenting an unvalidated rule as validated) is preserved; the *letter* is not.
**If you disagree, change the default — the reasoning is here to argue with.**

## 5. WHAT FAILED, AND THE DISCIPLINE THAT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY RESULT

**Every headline number produced on 2026-08-26 shrank or vanished under its own controls.**

| claimed | what it actually was |
|---|---|
| 2.35× generation effect | bitrate — collapsed to 0.57 and inverted once medians matched |
| −0.736 iso-damage bias | BPP+ dividing by complexity, i.e. its own definition |
| −0.697 field validation | a shared term (0.908), then content, then bits → −0.188 |
| eq "no bitrate confound by construction" | corr(eq, bpp) = −0.536 |
| eq year structure 0.421 | → 0.207 (bits out) → 0.088 (complexity out) |
| L* = 4.64× encode quality | contradicts published BD-rate 3×; precision ≠ plausibility |
| matched filter, ±43% band | wrong noise model — see below |

**Treat a first number as a hypothesis about where the confound lives, never as a result. Check
against an EXTERNAL BOUND before checking its standard error.**

### 5.1 The matched filter, and three mistakes worth not repeating (§11.25)

Rejected on the external bound. What it got right is kept: `cos(u_bits, u_encoder)` = 0.909 raw /
0.899 whitened, orthogonal surviving component 0.42, almost entirely **blocking** — §11.13 by an
independent route. What went wrong:

1. **Pre-registered the wrong statistic.** "cos > 0.9 = dead" assumed bits were unknown. bpp is known
   exactly, so what matters is what survives removal. Recognising that *after* seeing 0.909 is moving
   a goalpost, and it is recorded as one.
2. **Wrong noise model — the actual killer.** Whitened by ladder scatter (within-film repeatability,
   0.02-0.07 in log) when the dominant noise is between-film model error (0.17-1.34). 15-60× off.
3. **A vacuous check reported as evidence.** `corr(E, log bpp) = 0.0000, must be ~0` — OLS residuals
   are orthogonal to their regressors BY ARITHMETIC. That check can never fail. **Any script that
   residualises and then reports a zero correlation with its regressors must say in its output that
   this carries no evidence.**

### 5.2 The direct `(T/L)^k` rule — a dilemma with no interior solution (§11.33)

It is on the lab page as a comparison mode, and its documented failure mode was **wrong**. Measured at
k=0.25: it does not run away upward, it **squashes the whole library toward 100** — films at 40 → ~70,
films at 200 → ~130, library median +33% (the provenance rule moves it +1.6%). Worse than a tail,
because it discards what BPP+ already knows and a summary statistic hides it.

The dilemma is algebraic, not empirical:

- **small k** — `(T/L)^k` is nearly flat in bitrate, every film scores alike, blending drags BPP+ to
  the common value. Information destroyed.
- **large k** — matching BPP+'s bitrate sensitivity needs `k = 0.5/S` = 4.85 for blocking, and
  `(T/L)^(1/S)` under a square root **is** the bits-equivalent multiplier that killed rules one
  through six.
- **No k in between does neither.** This closes the direct route on algebra.

**Separate defect, quantified:** grain loss fires on **56.7% of smooth content** vs 15.8% of grainy —
backwards for a damage detector. A digitally-shot film has little grain to retain, so "grain below
0.905" reads *content*. Any raw-threshold rule inherits this; P does not, because it residualises
grain against complexity first. That is an argument for the residual framing nobody anticipated when
it was chosen, and it is task #73 arriving from a third direction.

### 5.3 A vacuous analysis caught by its own unanimity (§11.34)

Worth reading because it looked like a decision tool and was one edit from the handoff. The question
"would a bigger download fix this flagged film?" was tested by comparing the delivered file against
our re-encode at 2.5× bitrate. It returned **"bits do not help" for 18 of 18** — and the unanimity was
the tell. **Every ladder rung is re-encoded from the delivered file**, so any artifact already baked
into those pixels is inherited by every rung. The test could only ever return one answer.

Answering it properly needs a *higher-quality source than the file being judged*, which this library
never has. **Do not rebuild that test on ladder data.**

What the same data *does* answer: whether the artifact responds to bitrate on that content — the
per-film relevance question. On 9 flagged films, 36 pairs, **zero wrong-sign responses**, and **9 of
the 18 visible artifacts are inert to bitrate**. Severance S02 banding 8.262 at 3× threshold, slope
−0.034: visible and inert. That is the case where BPP+ alone gives *actively misleading* advice — it
reads low, and the obvious response is to go find more bits.

### 5.4 §11.21's rejection used the wrong slope (§11.35)

Across 106 laddered films, |slope| against the film's own level:

```
blocking   corr +0.370   terciles 0.068 0.119 0.171   above threshold 0.231 vs library 0.107
banding    corr -0.626   terciles 1.240 0.597 0.312   above threshold 0.326 vs library 0.575
```

The two go **opposite** ways, and that asymmetry is the evidence. A detector-floor artifact — near-zero
readings producing huge log swings — predicts a *negative* correlation for every detector. Banding is
negative, so it is confounded with exactly that and **should not be quoted**. Blocking is *positive*,
against the direction the artifact would push, so it survives.

**Consequence:** on films where blocking is visible the slope is ~2.2× the library median, so 1/S is
~4.3 not ~9.7. §11.21's argument was made with a slope drawn from films where blocking sits at its
floor and cannot move. **Its conclusion stands — 4.3 still amplifies confounds — but its arithmetic
was pessimistic by about 2×.** Any future construction needing a slope must use the film's own, never
the library median, and must state which regime it was measured in.

### 5.5 Two corpus-design traps

- **CVQAD (§11.11):** every clip in a sequence is the same master through the same encoder. It holds
  constant exactly the variables that carry the signal. **Do not cite it as evidence against the
  framework.**
- **Release-group clustering (§11.17):** F=1.07 null, but 65% of files were renamed by \*arr and the
  survivors are all small-file re-encoders. Restricted range.

     rung, 2.6× threshold, with slope +0.018 at r² 0.07**: bitrate explains essentially *none* of its
     banding across an 8.3× sweep. Also grain 0.450 (heaviest destruction in the set, threshold
     0.905) and blur above threshold — three flagged artifacts on one unit, which is why it is the
     rule's #2 down-mover (103 → 72). Look for concentric steps in gradients: dark rooms, neon wash,
     sky. *(The nightly probe reads its banding at 10.94 and the ladder at 7.354 — different runs,
     and ladder absolutes do not travel between runs. Both far above 2.817; don't quote them as one
     measurement.)*
   - **Gran Torino** blocking 4.829 at the lossless rung (threshold 3.710), and it **does** respond to
     bitrate (slope −0.176, r² 0.89) — so unlike Mr. Robot, a better-provisioned copy would help.
     Look for macroblock structure, 8×8/16×16 grid edges in dim interiors and flat walls. Not
     softness, visible square seams.
   - Different failure modes *and* different remedies, on purpose. **If either looks clean that is the
     strongest falsification available.**
   - Contrast worth knowing before looking: **Bloodline S03** has *higher* banding (10.247) than
     Mr. Robot yet still responds to bitrate (−0.096, r² 0.70). "High banding ⇒ inert" is a tendency,
     not a rule; the per-film slope is what separates them.

## 7. TRAPS AND CONVENTIONS

- **Never git commit.** Committing is Brennan's, always, in every repo.
- Never trigger a grab/redownload; never modify, delete or re-encode media on disk.
- `pgrep -af 'node scripts' | grep ...` **self-matches your own shell command line** and has produced
  three false alarms. Use `ps -eo args | grep -E '^node scripts/'`. In a wait-loop, bracket the
  pattern: `pgrep -f "[b]anding-ladder.js"`.
- `tools/ffmpeg-*` is a SECOND ffmpeg, for measurement only. Never use it for complexity, never
  upgrade the controller's.
- Research corpora live on `/data`, never the boot SSD.
- **One heavy job at a time.** Two measured 93°C against a 100°C limit, and the controller runs its own
  probes on a schedule you do not control.
- Ladder ABSOLUTES are not comparable between runs — only slopes travel.
- **A cut-off does not have to look like an if/else. A max is one. A hinge is one.** Both were found
  and removed; anything with a kink gets amplified by 1/S².
